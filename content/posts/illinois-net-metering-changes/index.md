**Since January 1, 2025, new home solar systems on ComEd, Ameren Illinois and MidAmerican get "supply-only" net metering: each kWh you send to the grid offsets the supply part of your bill, worth about 10.1 cents at ComEd today, but not delivery charges.** ComEd and Ameren systems connected before 2025 (and MidAmerican systems whose net metering application was submitted before 2025) keep full retail net metering, unless the owner took the generation rebate or makes a major change. The rest of this guide shows what that means in dollars, who keeps the old deal and what the rebates are worth.

The prices used here are the default supply prices that took effect on October 1, 2026, covered in our [ComEd and Ameren Illinois rate update](/posts/illinois-electricity-rate-changes-2026/). For how other states handle exports, see our [net metering state guide](/posts/net-metering-state-guide/).

## What Changed for Illinois Net Metering on January 1, 2025?

The Illinois Shines program, run for the Illinois Power Agency, summed it up in a July 2024 consumer FAQ: "Full retail rate net metering will no longer be available to residential and small commercial customers net metering customers interconnecting new renewable energy systems after December 31, 2024." Customers who enroll on or after January 1, 2025 "will only receive supply credits."

State law spells out what "supply" means. Under 220 ILCS 5/16-107.5(n), excess kWh are credited against "kilowatt-hour supply charges," defined as the per-kWh values "for energy, capacity, transmission, and the purchased energy adjustment." And the same subsection says: "The customer remains responsible for the gross amount of delivery services charges."

In plain terms: your exports cancel out the electricity part of the bill, but you pay the delivery (wires) charge on every kWh you pull from the grid, even if you exported just as many.

### Before and after: which charges your exports offset

| Bill component | Pre-2025 systems (full retail net metering) | Systems connected Jan. 1, 2025 or later (supply-only) |
|---|---|---|
| Supply (energy and capacity) | Offset by exports | Offset by exports |
| Transmission | Offset by exports | Offset by exports |
| Purchased energy adjustment | Offset by exports | Offset by exports |
| Per-kWh delivery (distribution) charges | Offset by exports (billed on net use) | **Not offset:** billed on all kWh drawn from the grid |
| Fixed monthly customer and meter charges | Paid regardless | Paid regardless |
| Leftover credits | Expire at the end of the annualized period (state law) | Carry forward; Ameren and CUB say they do not expire |

Sources: Illinois Shines FAQ (July 26, 2024); 220 ILCS 5/16-107.5; Ameren Illinois and Citizens Utility Board net metering pages.

### Two ways to take the credit

When you apply, you choose between a 1:1 kWh credit and a monetary credit, and the law says that choice is made "at the time of application." Ameren calls it "a one-time decision and cannot be changed later." With kWh netting, Ameren says delivery charges are still billed on your total kWh drawn from the grid. With monetary credits, Ameren says the credit is "based on either Ameren Illinois' current month's supply pricing" or your alternative retail supplier's price, and it is applied to the Supply section of the bill.

## Am I Grandfathered Under the Old Rules?

You keep full retail net metering if all of these are true:

1. **Your system was interconnected before January 1, 2025.** ComEd required that a system "have received permission to operate from ComEd prior to January 1, 2025." Illinois Shines lists the same cut-off for Ameren. For MidAmerican, the FAQ says a completed net metering application had to be submitted before January 1, 2025.
2. **You did not take the distributed generation rebate for your solar.** Illinois Shines says only customers who "did not request a Distributed Generation Rebate from the utility will continue to have full retail net metering."
3. **You have not made a qualifying modification** (see below).

Those systems "will continue to have full retail net metering for the life of the system unless material modifications are made," according to Illinois Shines. ComEd says eligibility lasts "for the lifetime of the system." If you leased your system or bought power under a PPA and later buy the system, Illinois Shines says it keeps full retail net metering "even if ownership of the project changes." The FAQ does not directly address selling the home, so get written confirmation from your utility if you are buying or selling a house with solar.

### What resets your eligibility

Illinois Shines lists the triggers by utility:

- **ComEd:** changes "that require a new interconnection application."
- **Ameren Illinois:** "an increase to the system's nameplate capacity of more than 100%."

If you plan to add panels to a pre-2025 system, ask your utility in writing whether the change needs a new interconnection application before you sign a contract. Adding a battery is treated differently for the rebate (see below), but confirm the interconnection question with your utility too.

## How Much Is an Exported kWh Worth Now?

For a household on the new rules buying default supply from the utility, the credit is roughly the utility's "price to compare," which bundles supply, transmission and adjustments. From October 1, 2026 through May 31, 2027, per the Illinois Commerce Commission's Plug In Illinois site:

| Utility | Price to compare (Oct. 1, 2026 to May 31, 2027) | Components |
|---|---|---|
| ComEd | 10.103¢ per kWh | Supply 8.413¢ + transmission 1.69¢; purchased electricity adjustment "expected to average near zero" |
| Ameren Illinois | 10.441¢ per kWh for the first 800 kWh; 8.262¢ above 800 kWh | Supply 7.31¢ (5.131¢ above 800 kWh) + supply cost adjustment 0.313¢ + transmission 2.765¢ |

Ameren's listed parts add up to about 0.05 cents less than its published price to compare; Plug In Illinois does not explain the gap. If you buy supply from an alternative retail supplier or a municipal aggregation program, your credit follows that supplier's price instead.

## Worked Example: 300 kWh Exported in a Month

Assumptions: a single-family home without electric heat, on default utility supply, that sends 300 kWh to the grid in a winter month (October to May) and stays within Ameren's first 800 kWh block. Delivery rates are the January 1, 2026 figures published by the Citizens Utility Board (CUB); they can change. Taxes and smaller per-kWh riders are left out.

| | ComEd | Ameren Illinois |
|---|---|---|
| Supply-side credit rate | 10.103¢ | 10.441¢ |
| **Value of 300 kWh under the new rule** | **$30.31** | **$31.32** |
| Per-kWh delivery charges the old rule also offset | 6.354¢ (distribution facilities 6.228¢ + Illinois electricity distribution 0.126¢) | 4.572¢ (non-summer distribution, first 800 kWh) |
| Extra value under the old rule | $19.06 | $13.72 |
| Value of 300 kWh under the old rule | $49.37 | $45.04 |

So under supply-only net metering, an exported kWh at ComEd is worth about 61% of what it was worth under full retail crediting in this example ($30.31 ÷ $49.37). At Ameren it is about 70% ($31.32 ÷ $45.04). In summer, Ameren's distribution charge is higher (7.811¢ per kWh, per CUB), so the delivery value the old rule offset is larger; summer supply prices also differ.

**What it means for a solar decision:** every kWh your panels produce while the house is using power still saves the full retail rate, delivery included. Only the surplus you push to the grid earns the lower supply-side value. That makes sizing to your own usage, shifting loads such as EV charging or laundry to sunny hours, and batteries worth a closer look for new Illinois systems. Our [solar export credit rates by utility](/posts/solar-export-credit-rates-by-utility-2026/) piece compares this with other utilities' export rates.

## What Are the Illinois Solar and Battery Rebates?

ComEd and Ameren Illinois both pay a one-time distributed generation (smart inverter) rebate:

- **Solar:** $300 per kW. Ameren lists "$300/KW-DC" for generator inverters. CUB says the rebate "requires the use of a smart inverter" and that, by law, it "will be at least $300 per kilowatt of generating capacity."
- **Battery storage:** $300 per kWh of storage capacity, per Ameren ("$300/KW-hour") and CUB.

**The trade-off for pre-2025 systems:** CUB's October 2025 rebate sheet says an owner with full retail net metering "will lose that status IF you elect to take the DG Rebate for generation," but "will NOT lose that status if you elect to take this DG Rebate for storage." Ameren says that after a generation rebate, excess generation credits "will no longer be applied to their delivery service charges," while supply and transmission crediting "will continue." There are strings attached to the storage rebate. CUB says ComEd requires recipients to take its hourly pricing rate (Rate BESH), and Ameren requires residential recipients to enroll in Peak Time Rewards, Real Time Pricing or Power Smart Pricing, a choice CUB says binds future residents at the address. Rebate terms can change, so talk to your utility's rate specialist before applying.

For new systems already on supply-only crediting, the generation rebate should not change your net metering, since delivery charges are not credited anyway; confirm with your utility.

## Does This Apply to Co-ops, Municipal Utilities and MidAmerican?

- **MidAmerican** is covered. Illinois Shines lists it with ComEd and Ameren among the utilities whose net metering changed. CUB lists MidAmerican's private generation contact as PrivateGeneration@midamerican.com or 1-877-815-0010.
- **Electric cooperatives and municipal utilities** are not covered by this rule. Illinois Shines notes that they "may limit access to net metering," and CUB says Illinois law "requires all municipal utilities and co-ops to publish their net metering policies." If you are served by one, ask it for its current policy.

## When This Advice Does Not Apply

- **Your system was interconnected before 2025** and you have not taken the generation rebate: you are on full retail net metering, and your leftover credits follow the annual rules described in our [net metering true-up guide](/posts/net-metering-true-up/).
- **You are on hourly supply pricing** (including ComEd storage-rebate customers on Rate BESH): your credits are calculated differently, and this example does not model them.
- **You buy supply from a third party:** your credit follows your supplier's contract price, not the utility's price to compare.
- **Commercial or large systems:** different rate classes and rules apply.
- **You are on ComEd's Delivery Time-of-Day rate** (update, November 19, 2026): delivery is billed at different prices by hour, so the flat delivery rate in this example does not apply. The Citizens Utility Board does not recommend combining legacy net metering, Delivery Time-of-Day and a fixed supply rate. See our [ComEd delivery time-of-day guide](/posts/comed-time-of-day-rates/).
