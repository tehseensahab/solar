Vermont's Public Utility Commission has done two things in 2026 that matter to anyone with, or considering, rooftop solar in the state. On May 29, 2026 it raised the per-kWh net-metering credit for new systems, effective August 1, 2026. On September 9, 2026 it opened a rulemaking, Case 26-1786-RULE, that the Commission says is meant to rethink how net-metering credits are calculated. This article separates what has already taken effect from what is only being studied.

**Scope:** this covers Vermont's net-metering program as set by the Vermont Public Utility Commission. It does not apply to other states. For other states, see our [net metering state guide](/posts/net-metering-state-guide/).

## At a glance

| Item | What the Commission's orders say | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Statewide blended residential rate | $0.2071 per kWh, up $0.0231 from $0.18398 | Ordered May 29, 2026; effective August 1, 2026 |
| Compensation for new residential-scale systems (Category I) | $0.1571 per kWh, up from $0.14398 | Applies to complete applications filed on or after August 1, 2026 |
| Siting adjustor, new Category I and II | Negative $0.05 per kWh, down from negative $0.04 | Same date |
| Existing systems | Most see about +$0.0231 per kWh, from the higher blended rate | Same date; siting adjustors for existing systems unchanged |
| Net-metering rule rewrite (Case 26-1786-RULE) | Opened; first workshop scheduled | Order of September 9, 2026; no proposed rule published |

## How Vermont calculates a net-metering credit

The Commission says compensation is built from three numbers.

1. **Blended residential rate.** The base value of the bill credit. For most customers it is the utility's standard residential rate or an average of all Vermont utilities' residential rates. Excess generation at the end of each month is multiplied by this rate and appears as a credit on the bill.
2. **Renewable energy credit (REC) adjustor.** A REC is a tradable certificate representing the environmental attributes of one megawatt-hour of renewable power. If the customer transfers RECs to the utility, the adjustor is $0.00 per kWh. If the customer keeps them, it is negative $0.04 per kWh.
3. **Siting adjustor.** A per-kWh charge meant to reflect cost differences by system size and to steer projects toward roofs and previously disturbed land.

The Commission notes the two adjustors apply to all kWh the system produces, including electricity used on site, and appear as a charge on the bill. The blended rate applies to the excess exported. So the headline "per kWh" figures below are the Commission's summary for a typical customer, not a prediction of your bill.

## What changed on August 1, 2026

The May 29, 2026 order (Case 26-0291-INV, the biennial update, which the rules require every two years) did three things:

- **Raised the blended residential rate** from $0.18398 to $0.2071 per kWh. The Commission adopted the Vermont Department of Public Service's calculation, which no commenter objected to, and applied it to existing and new systems.
- **Kept the REC adjustors unchanged** ($0.00 if transferred, negative $0.04 if kept).
- **Cut the siting adjustor by $0.01** for new Category I and II systems, to negative $0.05 per kWh. Category III and IV adjustors were not changed.

The Commission declined the Department's recommendation to skip this year's update and freeze the rate. It wrote that its rule created an expectation that the rate would be adjusted every two years.

### Compensation for new systems, by category

The table reproduces the Commission's Table 4. Figures are per kWh, for a customer whose utility uses the statewide blended residential rate and who transfers RECs to the utility. If a customer's utility has a lower residential rate, compensation is based on that lower rate.

| Category | Size and siting | Before August 1, 2026 | August 1, 2026 to July 31, 2028 |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Up to 15 kW | $0.14398 | $0.1571 |
| II | Over 15 kW to 150 kW, preferred site | $0.14398 | $0.1571 |
| III | Over 150 kW to 500 kW, preferred site | $0.11398 | $0.1371 |
| IV | Over 15 kW to 150 kW, not a preferred site | $0.10398 | $0.1271 |

Our arithmetic checks against the order: $0.2071 minus $0.05 is $0.1571, and $0.1571 minus $0.14398 is $0.01312, which the order rounds to $0.0131. A new Category I system's compensation rose about 9% ($0.01312 divided by $0.14398).

One inconsistency to flag. The introduction of the May 29 order says the three components sum to about $0.153 per kWh, while its Table 4 and the September 9 order both give $0.1571. The September order cites a June 5, 2026 technical-correction memo, which we did not read. We use $0.1571.

### Who this applies to

- **New systems:** the order applies to systems whose complete Certificate of Public Good (CPG) application is filed on or after August 1, 2026. A CPG is the state permit a net-metering system needs. The last day to file under the old rates was July 31, 2026.
- **Existing systems:** the higher blended rate applies to them too, but their siting adjustors do not change. The Commission says most existing systems will see about $0.0231 per kWh more.
- **Large and ground-mounted systems:** the Commission says it expects no applications for Category IV systems because of Act 179 of 2024, which requires new net-metering systems to serve customers on the same or an adjacent parcel. It also notes it had received no Category III applications so far in 2026.

We did not review individual utilities' compliance tariffs. The order directed utilities to file them by June 15, 2026 to take effect August 1, 2026. Check your own utility's tariff for what your bill shows.

## The rule rewrite: what is and is not decided

On September 9, 2026 the Commission opened Case 26-1786-RULE to update Commission Rule 5.100, the net-metering rule. Its reasoning, in its own order:

- Vermont's net-metering program has produced more than 390 MW of capacity, most of the more than 600 MW of distributed solar in the state. Vermont's peak demand is about 1,000 MW.
- Vermont has more distributed solar relative to peak load than other New England states, at 71.7% against 58.3% for Maine and 35% for Massachusetts, citing Department of Public Service comments.
- Solar has moved Vermont's peak demand to after sundown, which the Commission says reduces the value of more behind-the-meter solar unless it is paired with a battery.
- A new residential-scale system's compensation of about $0.1571 per kWh compares with roughly $0.105 per kWh for a utility-scale distributed solar facility, a gap of about 5 cents. The Commission says the investigation will examine whether that differential is justified.

**What is scheduled:** a virtual workshop on October 2, 2026 at 9:30 a.m., where the Commission asked Vermont Electric Power Company (VELCO), the state's transmission company, to present on distributed generation and peak load. The Commission expects more workshops.

**What is not decided:** the order publishes no proposed rule, comment deadline or timeline for a final rule. The Commission says the initial focus is the compensation framework and that other parts of the rule will wait. It also says any new framework should be easy to understand and should provide a transition for existing systems that reflects owners' "reasonable investment-backed expectations" based on the compensation in place when they filed a complete application. It did not say what that transition would be. We did not find a record of what was presented at the October 2 workshop.

The Commission has also said that customers have "always been and will continue to be subject to future changes in underlying utility rates and rate structures that could affect the value of a net-metering credit." If you are weighing a system, that sentence matters more than any single rate in the table.

## What this means for a buyer or owner

- **Considering a system:** payback depends on your usage, system cost and how credits are calculated, and the last of those is under review. Our [payback guide](/posts/solar-payback-period-without-federal-tax-credit/) shows how to calculate it, and our [export credit comparison](/posts/solar-export-credit-rates-by-utility-2026/) shows how other utilities compare. The federal 30% credit for homeowner-owned systems is no longer available for expenditures after December 31, 2025, per the IRS.
- **Existing owner:** the August 1 change raised your base credit. Whether future rule changes affect you depends on a transition the Commission has not defined.
- **Retail rate context:** the blended rate follows Vermont retail rates. Green Mountain Power's most recent rate change is in our [Vermont rate guide](/posts/vermont-electricity-rate-changes-2026/).

## Limitations

This article relies on the Commission's two orders and its web notices. We did not read the June 5 technical-correction memo, the Department's comments, or utilities' tariffs. The $32,500 cost of a 10 kW system in the May order is a figure from a commenter that the Commission cited, not a measured average, and we do not rely on it. The orders describe a typical customer; actual credits depend on your utility, system category, REC election and application date. This is not legal or financial advice.
