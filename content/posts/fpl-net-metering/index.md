**FPL net metering trades kWh for kWh: power your panels send to the grid offsets power you take from FPL later, for up to 12 months, at the full per-kWh price on your bill.** Credits still unused at the end of the calendar year become a bill credit at a rate based on FPL's avoided energy cost, not the retail price. You also pay FPL's $30 minimum base bill every month, however much your panels produce.

That combination rewards a system sized to cover most of your own yearly use, not more. This guide walks through the rules in FPL's tariff and Florida's net metering rule, with the numbers. For the rate itself, line by line, see our guide to [FPL rates per kWh and time-of-use hours](/posts/fpl-rates-2026/); for other Florida utilities, see the [Florida electricity rates and solar page](/rates/florida/).

## How Does FPL Net Metering Work?

FPL's net metering rules are in its tariff (Sheet No. 10.010, effective Jan. 1, 2026) and in Florida Public Service Commission Rule 25-6.065, which applies to the state's investor-owned utilities. In plain terms:

1. **A two-way meter.** FPL installs a bi-directional meter that measures power flowing into your home and power flowing out. Florida's rule says the utility installs net metering equipment "at no additional cost to the customer."
2. **Monthly offset.** FPL says: "If you produce more energy than you receive, the excess amounts are applied to your next month's electric bill." The tariff says the excess "will be credited to the customer's energy consumption for the next month's billing cycle."
3. **Twelve-month carry-forward.** Credits "will be accumulated and be used to offset the customer's energy usage in subsequent months," for "not more than twelve months," per the tariff.
4. **Year-end payout.** In the last billing cycle month of each calendar year, unused credits are converted to a bill credit "using the average annual rate based on the Company's COG-1, As-Available Energy Tariff."
5. **No cash during the year.** FPL's FAQ says: "You don't receive any monetary credit for the energy your system produces." Credits are kWh, not dollars, until year-end.

## What Is a Net Metering Credit Worth on an FPL Bill?

Because credits are kWh, each one is worth whatever a kWh costs you that month. On FPL's standard residential rate (RS-1), the per-kWh charges on the rate sheet effective October 2026 add up to:

| Monthly usage band | Price per kWh (RS-1, Oct. 2026) | Value of 1 offset kWh |
|---|---|---|
| First 1,000 kWh | 12.258¢ | 12.258¢ |
| Each kWh above 1,000 | 14.258¢ | 14.258¢ |
| Base charge | $10.52 per month | Not offset by solar |

These totals add the energy, fuel and clause charges on FPL's rate sheet; our [FPL rates guide](/posts/fpl-rates-2026/) lists each line. Taxes and fees are extra.

**Worked example (our math, before taxes and fees).** A home uses 1,400 kWh in a month on RS-1.

- Without solar: $10.52 + (1,000 × 12.258¢) + (400 × 14.258¢) = **$190.13**.
- With solar that cuts net use to 600 kWh: $10.52 + (600 × 12.258¢) = **$84.07**.
- Savings: **$106.06** for 800 kWh offset, about 13.3 cents per kWh, because the first 400 kWh of solar knocked out the higher-priced kWh above 1,000.

Within a month, the same 800 kWh earn the same savings whether your panels covered your use as it happened or sent power out at noon that you drew back at night. Credits carried into a later month are worth that month's price band: 12.258 cents if that month stays under 1,000 kWh. That is the main advantage of FPL's kWh-for-kWh system over states that pay a lower rate for exports.

## What Happens to Unused FPL Solar Credits at the End of the Year?

Credits that build up in sunny months are used up in months when you take more power than you send. Whatever is left after the last billing cycle of the calendar year is turned into a dollar credit on your bill.

The rate is "an average annual rate based on the Company's COG-1, As-Available Energy Tariff," per FPL's tariff and Florida's rule. FPL's COG-1 sheet says as-available energy is priced "based on the Company's actual hourly avoided energy costs." In other words, it reflects what FPL would have spent to produce the energy, not the retail price you pay. The tariff does not list it as a single cents-per-kWh figure, and we did not find FPL's current annual rate published, so ask FPL for the most recent figure.

FPL's pages differ slightly on timing: the FAQ says the credit goes on your December bill and the net metering page says a cash credit for December excess is applied to your January bill. Either way, the reset happens once a year, at calendar year-end, not on the anniversary of your installation.

**What it means for sizing.** Credits still unused at year-end, including surplus your panels make late in the year, are credited at that avoided-cost rate, not at 12 to 14 cents. In our reading, the best fit is a system sized close to your annual use, with summer surplus carried into lower-production months. For a broader look at how true-ups work in other states, see [net metering true-up](/posts/net-metering-true-up/).

The Florida PSC's 2025 net metering report lists $1,712,195.48 in total payments by FPL to its net metering customers, across 124,467 interconnections. That is about $14 per interconnection on average (our math).

If you close your FPL account, unused credits are paid at the same COG-1-based rate, per the tariff.

## Does the $30 Minimum Bill Apply to Solar Customers?

Yes. FPL's FAQ says: "Our $30 minimum base bill ensures all customers contribute to fixed system costs, which do not vary with electricity usage." The tariff's net metering sheet says customers pay "the greater of" the minimum charge in their rate schedule or the base charge plus any demand charge, "regardless of whether excess energy is delivered" to the grid.

FPL's rate sheet says residential customers whose monthly base electric service costs fall below $30 pay a minimum $30 base bill. If the costs that count toward the $30 are the base charge plus the non-fuel energy charge, a month with less than about 248 kWh of net use lands on the minimum. That is our reading, not FPL's.

What that means:

- A month of net-zero use still costs about **$30 before taxes and fees**, not $0.
- Sizing a system to drive several months to near zero buys less than it seems, because the last kWh in those months save little or nothing below the minimum.

## What Size Solar System Qualifies for FPL Net Metering?

Systems up to 2 MW qualify, in three tiers based on AC gross power rating:

| Tier | AC size | Panel (DC) size, about | Extra fees | Liability insurance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Up to 10 kW | Up to 11.76 kW | None allowed beyond normal customer charges | Not required |
| Tier 2 | Over 10 kW to 100 kW | Over 11.76 to 117.6 kW | Application fee | $1 million |
| Tier 3 | Over 100 kW to 2 MW | Over 117.6 kW to 2.35 MW | Application fee | $2 million |

FPL says the AC gross power rating "equals the DC rating of your solar array times 0.85." The DC column is our math from that rule. Florida's rule bars extra fees, including application fees, for Tier 1 and caps the insurance a utility can require at $1 million for Tier 2 and $2 million for Tier 3. FPL's FAQ requires coverage of "not less than" those amounts, so in practice FPL asks for exactly the cap. We did not find FPL's Tier 2 and 3 application fee amounts in the sources we reviewed.

Two more limits from the rule:

- A system cannot exceed 90% of your utility distribution service rating.
- The utility must sign the interconnection agreement within 30 calendar days of a completed application, or 90 days for Tier 3 systems that need a study.

In the PSC's 2025 report, FPL had 124,449 solar net metering interconnections with 1,220,598 kW (about 1.2 GW) of AC capacity, an average of about 9.8 kW AC each (our math), right around the Tier 1 limit.

## How Do You Apply for FPL Net Metering?

FPL's net metering page lists four steps: start an application, submit it, get approved, and receive permission to operate. In practice your installer usually files it. Before you sign a contract, ask:

1. **What is my yearly kWh use, and how much will the system produce?** Use 12 months of FPL bills. Our [solar system size calculator](/solar-system-size-calculator/) helps you estimate it.
2. **Will the system produce more than I use in a year?** If so, credits left at year-end get only the COG-1-based rate.
3. **What will my bill be in my lowest-use months?** Expect at least the $30 minimum base bill plus taxes and fees.
4. **Which tier is the system, in AC?** Above 10 kW AC means an application fee and insurance.
5. **Who files the application, and when will FPL install the two-way meter?** Do not turn the system on before permission to operate.

## What FPL Net Metering Does Not Cover

- **Time-of-use.** If you join FPL's optional time-of-use rider (RTR-1), the value of each offset kWh depends on when you use power; our [FPL rates guide](/posts/fpl-rates-2026/) covers its hours. See [how time-of-use rates change the value of solar](/posts/time-of-use-rates-and-solar/).
- **Northwest Florida.** FPL publishes separate residential rate sheets for Northwest Florida, so check the sheet for your area before using the per-kWh figures above.
- **Changes ahead.** Florida's rule and FPL's tariff can be changed by the PSC. We read the rule as last amended in 2008 (Cornell LII copy) and FPL's tariff effective Jan. 1, 2026; check for changes before you sign.
