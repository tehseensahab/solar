**Selling a house with a solar lease usually comes down to one step: transferring the lease or power purchase agreement (PPA) to your buyer.** Tesla says its agreements transfer to the new owner about 98% of the time. When a buyer won't take the contract, you can usually prepay or buy out the rest of it, or in some cases move the panels. The key is starting early, as soon as you have an offer.

This guide covers leases and PPAs, where a solar company owns the panels on your roof. If you own your system outright or with a loan, the questions are different; see [whether solar panels increase home value](/posts/do-solar-panels-increase-home-value/) for how owned systems are appraised. For how leases compare with buying in the first place, see [solar lease vs. buying](/posts/solar-lease-vs-buy/).

## Your Four Options at a Glance

| Option | What happens | Who pays | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transfer to the buyer | Buyer takes over the remaining contract | Buyer pays the monthly bill from closing | Buyer may need to pass a credit check |
| Prepay the remaining term | You pay off the rest of the contract; the buyer gets the power with no bill | You, usually built into the sale price | Prepaying may not give you ownership |
| Buy the system out | You buy the panels and they convey with the house as owned equipment | You | Not every contract allows it, or only after a set year |
| Move the panels | Company removes and reinstalls them at your new home | Usually you | New roof must qualify; costs extra |

The options come from the providers' and the FTC's guidance cited below. Your contract decides which you actually have, so read it first.

## Option 1: Transfer the Lease or PPA

Transfer is the default, and the big providers have dedicated teams for it. Here is how two of them describe the process:

**Tesla.** The company says the transfer "should be initiated as soon as you are in contract with a buyer," and only the system owner can start it. You request it in your Tesla account and enter the buyer's and escrow details. Tesla then contacts the buyer to review the agreement, sends a transfer agreement to sign electronically, releases any title filing once it receives the signed agreement and any fee, and the seller confirms the closing with Tesla through escrow. Any balance or prepayment due "is due before or at the time of closing."

**Sunrun.** Sunrun asks sellers to contact its service transfer team "as soon as you receive an offer on your home." The buyer completes an online application with a soft credit check, which Sunrun says "will not impact their credit or affect their home loan." Both parties sign a transfer form, and after closing you send Sunrun the settlement statement, closing disclosure or recorded deed. Sunrun says customers on prepaid yearly leases skip the credit step.

What the FTC says to check in your contract: whether you are allowed to transfer it, whether you must give written notice first, and whether the buyer will have to "meet credit requirements or pay any fees before taking over the contract."

### Making the transfer easy for a buyer

Solar United Neighbors, a nonprofit that helps homeowners go solar, links most buyer refusals to poor disclosure and missing savings information. Give your agent, and the buyer, these numbers up front:

1. The monthly payment, and the yearly escalator if there is one.
2. The years left on the contract.
3. Your past 12 months of electric bills, so the buyer can see what the system saves.
4. Who maintains the system. Under a lease or PPA, the company that owns it handles operations and maintenance, which can be a selling point.

Solar United Neighbors also suggests making the transfer a contingency in the sale contract, so a problem shows up before closing rather than at it.

## Option 2: Prepay or Buy Out the Contract

If the buyer won't take over the contract, or doesn't qualify, you can usually pay it off.

- **Prepay the rest.** Sunrun says you "can prepay for the remainder of your service and bundle it into the price of your home." The buyer then gets the power with no monthly bill. Sunrun says it can remove the system at no extra cost when the agreement ends.
- **Buy out the system.** Some contracts let you buy the panels, after which they convey with the house like any other owned equipment. Solar United Neighbors notes that buying the system may not be possible until typically five years into the contract.

**What a payoff can cost: an illustration.** Suppose your lease costs $120 a month, rises 2.9% a year, and has eight years left. The remaining payments add up to about $12,760 ($120 × 12 in the first year, growing 2.9% a year for eight years). Without the escalator, it would be $11,520 ($120 × 96). These are hypothetical figures, not any company's terms. Your company may offer a discount for paying early, so ask for the actual payoff figure in writing.

That number is the reason to start early: a buyer who takes over the contract saves you from paying it.

## Option 3: Move the Panels

Some providers let you take the system with you. The FTC tells consumers to check whether the contract "let[s] you move the system to your new home" and "what will that cost." Solar United Neighbors puts removal and reinstallation at $500 to $2,500, and notes that the new roof must suit the system and that you need new utility approval to connect it. Your provider decides whether your new roof qualifies.

## The Paperwork: UCC-1 Filings and Notices

Many solar companies record a **UCC-1 fixture filing** or a **notice of solar contract** in the county property records. It tells the world that the company, not you, owns the equipment. It is not a mortgage on your house, but a buyer's lender or title company will see it and want it dealt with.

Typically the company releases the filing for the closing and re-files it under the new owner after a transfer. Tesla's terms show the time and cost involved:

- **Fee:** a $150 document processing fee to temporarily release or subordinate a UCC-1 or similar recorded solar contract notice.
- **Time:** "approximately 15 to 20 business days" if you ask Tesla to record the release.
- **If the sale falls through:** Tesla begins re-filing 60 days after sending a release, so tell the company if closing is delayed or canceled.

Sunrun says it will temporarily remove its lien-related filing during the transfer process "at no cost to you."

## How Leased Panels Affect Appraisal and Price

Fannie Mae's appraisal guidance says the appraiser "may not include the value of the solar panels in the appraised value of the property" when the panels are leased or covered by a PPA. In practice, price the home as if the panels were not there, and use the savings, not the equipment, as the selling point.

Research backs that up. As we covered in our [solar and home value guide](/posts/do-solar-panels-increase-home-value/), a Berkeley Lab study of California sales found no evidence that buyers paid more for homes with third-party-owned solar.

## A Timeline That Works

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| Before listing | Find your contract. Note the transfer terms, payoff or buyout terms, payment and term left. Gather 12 months of bills. |
| In the listing | Disclose the lease or PPA and its terms. |
| At the offer | Contact the solar company and start the transfer. |
| Under contract | Buyer completes the company's application and signs the transfer. Ask about the UCC-1 or notice release. |
| At closing | Pay any balance due. Confirm closing with the solar company. |
| After closing | Send proof of closing if asked. Expect a final bill; Tesla says within two billing cycles. |

## If Your Solar Company Has Gone Out of Business

If the company that sold you the lease has failed, your contract may now be serviced by a different company. The transfer steps are similar, but finding the right contact can take longer, so start earlier still. See our guide to [what happens when your solar installer goes out of business](/posts/solar-installer-out-of-business/).

## Limitations

Contract terms vary by company, state and contract date; this guide describes the published processes of Tesla and Sunrun and general guidance from the FTC and Solar United Neighbors. We did not review individual contracts. The payoff illustration is hypothetical. Mortgage lenders apply their own rules to how a buyer's lease payment counts against their loan; the buyer should ask their lender. This is not legal advice.
