**Regulators approved Public Service Company of Oklahoma's (PSO) rate case on November 5, 2026, and PSO says a typical home using 1,100 kWh will pay about $5.80 a month less than it does under the interim rates in bills since July 1, starting with the December billing cycle.** Oklahoma Gas & Electric (OG&E) filed its own request on October 1, and nothing is approved for OG&E.

This article covers PSO and OG&E. It does not cover cooperatives or municipal utilities.

## Quick Summary

| Item | Utility | Date | Status | Residential impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Original request | PSO | Filed Jan. 2, 2026 | Not adopted | About 15%, more than $25 a month |
| Interim rates | PSO | July 1, 2026 | Replaced by new rates in December billing | About $11 a month at 1,100 kWh |
| Attorney General settlement | PSO | June 2026 | Not adopted | About 1%, $2.45 a month |
| Commission decision | PSO | Voted Nov. 5, 2026 (2-1) | Approved; new rates from the December billing cycle | About $5.80 a month less than interim, at 1,100 kWh (PSO estimate) |
| Rate review | OG&E | Filed Oct. 1, 2026 | Not approved | $23.88 a month (request) |

## PSO Rate Case Decision: What Changes in December

PSO filed on January 2, 2026. Because the Oklahoma Corporation Commission did not vote within 180 days, PSO started interim rates on July 1, about $11 a month for a typical customer using 1,100 kWh. The commission voted 2-1 on November 5, 2026, with Commissioners Todd Hiett and Brian Bingman in favor and Chair Kim David against, according to KTUL and GCN; GCN said a commission spokesperson confirmed the vote. KTUL describes the decision as Order No. 761871 in Cause PUD2025-000075, formally titled an "Interim Order."

| Question | Answer | Who says so |
|---|---|---|
| How big is the increase? | 3.55% is the figure reported. The commission said it is "significantly lower than PSO's original request of $25 a month increase." A spokesperson later said 3.55% "represents what PSO has indicated" to its staff, which still had to review it. | Oklahoma Corporation Commission (via GCN and KTUL) |
| What happens to a typical bill? | A home using 1,100 kWh is expected to see its total bill fall about $5.80 a month compared with interim rates. PSO says the base-rate portion rises about $32 a month within that lower total. | PSO statement, Nov. 19, 2026 (via KTUL) |
| When? | The December billing cycle, once revised tariffs are approved. | PSO; the order as described by KTUL |
| Refunds of interim rates? | PSO says industrial customers get refunds and residential customers do not. The order, as KTUL describes it, requires any excess interim collections to be returned with interest within 90 days after revised tariffs take effect. The commission said it could not yet confirm who gets refunds. | PSO; the order as described by KTUL; commission spokesperson |
| Settlement? | The commission adopted neither PSO's request nor the Attorney General settlement, which would have held the residential increase to about 1%. | GCN |
| Data centers | All three commissioners approved a large-load tariff for customers adding more than 75 megawatts, with details still under study. | GCN |

**Our math:** before July, a typical 1,100 kWh bill had no interim increase. Interim rates added about $11.00; the new rates take about $5.80 back off. $11.00 − $5.80 = **about $5.20 a month more than before July 1**, if nothing else on the bill had changed. AARP Oklahoma, a party to the case, said it helped cut the residential increase to $5.35 a month, an overall 3.7% for residential customers; those are AARP's figures, not PSO's or the commission's.

Because PSO says residential customers will get no refund, the earlier calculation on this page, which assumed a refund of the gap between the $11 interim increase and the settlement, no longer applies. KTUL reported that the order does not let PSO track part of its transmission costs, which PSO says "could lead to another base rate case." For how interim rates and refunds work in general, see [how electric rate cases work](/posts/how-electric-rate-cases-work/).

## OG&E: New Filing

OG&E filed its first rate review since 2023 on October 1, 2026, asking for $395 million. It estimates $23.88 a month for an average residential customer, with new rates in about six months. See our [OG&E article](/posts/oge-files-oklahoma-rate-case-2026/) for details.

## Oklahoma Net Metering and Solar Export Credits

Oklahoma's net metering rule gives retail value up to what you use, and avoided cost beyond that.

- **Size limit:** 300 kW or less.
- **Within the billing period:** the Oklahoma Corporation Commission says customers receive "compensation at the full retail energy rates, but only up to their consumption level" at that location.
- **Surplus:** utilities buy excess production at their avoided energy cost, with payment or credit in the next billing cycle.
- **Sizing rule:** a 125% limit keeps system output close to expected use.

**What it means:** production up to your monthly use is worth the retail energy rate. Anything above it earns the avoided cost. PSO's December change to base rates and other charges may change that retail value, so recheck the per-kWh energy charges on your bill once the new tariffs are in effect.

## Limitations

PSO figures come from PSO and commission statements reported by KTUL and GCN, earlier reporting by KGOU, KRMG and the Tulsa Flyer, and AARP Oklahoma. We did not open the Corporation Commission's order itself, and the commission said its staff was still reviewing PSO's figures in November, so the exact December bill change can differ from PSO's $5.80 estimate. KTUL also printed an unattributed $5.32 decrease; we use PSO's own $5.80. The $5.20 comparison with pre-July bills is our subtraction and ignores fuel and other changes since July. Net metering terms on this page were last reviewed on October 5, 2026.

## Oklahoma Statewide Average

The U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) puts Oklahoma's average residential price at 14.82¢/kWh in August 2026, up 2.8% from 14.42¢ in August 2025. The U.S. average was 18.16¢. At 1,000 kWh that works out to $148.20 (1,000 × 14.82¢). This is EIA's statewide figure: all residential revenue divided by all residential kWh, across every utility, fixed charges included. It is preliminary, and it is not any one utility's rate.

**Changes since first publication:** Nov. 28, 2026: PSO rate case decided Nov. 5 (2-1 vote); typical 1,100 kWh bill about $5.80 a month below interim rates from December billing (PSO); PSO says no residential refund of interim collections (commission has not confirmed); settlement and refund estimate removed.
