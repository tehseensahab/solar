Southern California Edison (SCE) raised its residential rates slightly on October 1, 2026. SCE's rate advisory lists the average residential rate moving from **34.4 to 34.5 cents per kilowatt-hour (kWh)**. For a customer on its TOU-D-4-9pm time-of-use plan using 500 kWh a month, SCE estimates the bill rises **$2.79**, from $185.85 to $188.64, or 1.5%.

This corrects and updates our [California rate guide](/posts/california-electricity-rate-changes-2026/), published September 30, which said that no October 1 change had been posted as of September 29. The advisory page we read now lists one. The page does not show when SCE posted it, so we cannot say whether it appeared after our check.

This article covers SCE's bundled residential customers, meaning those who buy both electricity supply and delivery from SCE. Customers who buy supply from a Community Choice Aggregator (a city or regional program that buys power on residents' behalf) get a different generation rate from that provider. It does not cover PG&E or SDG&E customers.

## The Change at a Glance

| Item | Before | After | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Average residential rate | 34.4¢/kWh | 34.5¢/kWh | +0.1¢ (about +0.3%) |
| Average residential rate, with California Climate Credit | 33.2¢/kWh | 33.3¢/kWh | +0.1¢ |
| Typical bill, TOU-D-4-9pm at 500 kWh | $185.85 | $188.64 | +$2.79 (+1.5%) |
| Same, with Climate Credit | $179.85 | $182.64 | +$2.79 (+1.6%) |
| CARE customer, 500 kWh | $111.06 | $111.25 | +$0.19 (+0.2%) |
| CARE customer, with Climate Credit | $105.06 | $105.25 | +$0.19 (+0.2%) |

Effective date: October 1, 2026. Source: SCE rate advisory. "TOU-D-4-9pm" is a time-of-use residential rate in which the highest prices apply from 4 p.m. to 9 p.m. CARE is California's discount program for income-qualified households. These are SCE's estimates for the stated customer type and usage, not your bill.

### The math

- **Dollar change:** $188.64 − $185.85 = **$2.79**.
- **Percent change:** $2.79 ÷ $185.85 = **1.50%**, and $2.79 ÷ $179.85 = 1.55%, which SCE rounds to 1.6%.
- **Average rate:** 34.5 − 34.4 = 0.1¢, or about 0.3% (0.1 ÷ 34.4). The 0.3% is our calculation from rounded figures, so the true change could be a little smaller or larger.
- **Effective price per kWh at 500 kWh:** $188.64 ÷ 500 = 37.7¢, up from 37.2¢. This is the whole bill divided by kWh, so it includes fixed charges and is higher than the average rate.

### Why the typical bill moves more than the average rate

The 0.3% and the 1.5% are different measures. The 34.4 to 34.5 cent figure is an average across residential customers. The $2.79 is SCE's estimate for one plan at one usage level. SCE's advisory says that implementing the 2025 General Rate Case Phase 2 decision "redistributes costs across rate groups," lowering the total collected but giving residential customers a modestly larger share. We read that as the likely reason, but SCE does not link the two numbers explicitly.

Do not compare these dollar figures with the typical bills in our September 30 article ($187.56 and $187.40). The current advisory names the plan (TOU-D-4-9pm); we did not confirm that the earlier figures used the same plan, so a direct comparison could mislead.

## What Drove It

SCE's advisory lists these items, each approved or implemented under decisions of the California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC):

| Driver | Amount | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Implementation of the 2025 General Rate Case Phase 2 decision (Decision 26-04-033) | Not stated | Redistributes costs across rate groups; the total collected falls, residential share rises modestly |
| Woolsey Fire restoration costs | $49.674 million | Increase, all customers |
| Woolsey Fire wildfire claims recovery bonds | $140.110 million | Increase |
| Recovery of memorandum accounts, 2022 Energy Resource Recovery Account review (Decision 26-01-003) | $59.682 million | Increase |
| Return of an overcollection in the Asset Transfer Franchise Memorandum Account (Decision 26-04-006; Advice 5807-E) | $0.246 million | Decrease |
| Removal of fully recovered costs | $628.062 million | Decrease, all customers |

The advisory does not give a net total, and one item (the General Rate Case Phase 2 redistribution) has no dollar amount, so we do not add these up. The largest single item is a decrease, which helps explain why the average rate barely moved.

## What It Means for Solar Owners

SCE's help pages refer to solar customers on the Solar Billing Plan and on the older Net Energy Metering (NEM) 1.0 and 2.0 tariffs. The October 1 advisory is about what customers pay, and it does not describe any change to those crediting rules. Here is what it does and does not tell you:

1. **The value of power you avoid buying is essentially flat.** Average rates moved 0.1¢ per kWh. What your solar avoids depends on your plan and the hour: the plan's name indicates a 4 to 9 p.m. peak window, which you should confirm in the tariff. Check your plan's actual per-kWh prices in SCE's tariff; the advisory gives averages only. See [time-of-use rates and solar](/posts/time-of-use-rates-and-solar/).
2. **The Base Services Charge is fixed.** SCE's help pages list $24.15 a month for customers not enrolled in assistance programs, $12.08 for FERA-enrolled customers and customers in qualifying deed-restricted affordable housing, and $6.00 for CARE. SCE says solar customers pay it every month regardless of energy usage, that generation cannot offset it, and that remaining Net Surplus Compensation or Energy Export Bonus credits (Solar Billing Plan) pay it. At $24.15, that is $289.80 a year (24.15 × 12). SCE also says it lowered per-kWh prices by about 10% when the charge began, in November 2025. The October 1 advisory does not mention a change to the charge. For why this matters, see [fixed charges and solar](/posts/fixed-charges-and-solar-savings/).
3. **Export credits are a separate question.** This advisory is about what you pay, not what SCE pays for exports. See our look at [export credit rates by utility](/posts/solar-export-credit-rates-by-utility-2026/).
4. **The Climate Credit.** SCE lists a $36 California Climate Credit applied twice a year (April and October), according to its January 1, 2026 advisory. The "with Climate Credit" bills in the advisory differ from the plain bills by $6.00 a month, which is consistent with $36 spread over six months. That is our inference, not SCE's wording.

## What to Do

- **Look at your bill, not the average.** Find your rate plan name and your kWh. If you are not on TOU-D-4-9pm at about 500 kWh, your change will differ. See [how to read your electric bill](/posts/how-to-read-your-electric-bill/).
- **If you are quoting solar,** ask installers which rate plan they modeled and whether the Base Services Charge is in the savings estimate. A quote built on an older rate may be off by more than $2.79.
- **Watch for the next filing.** Our California guide covers what PG&E's pending General Rate Case and SDG&E's changes mean for those customers.

## Limitations

All figures come from SCE's own advisory and help pages. They are the utility's estimates for a specific plan and usage level, not your bill. The advisory page shows no posting date. We did not read the CPUC decisions or SCE's tariff sheets, and we did not find the October 1 effect of the change on individual solar tariffs. This is general information, not financial advice.
