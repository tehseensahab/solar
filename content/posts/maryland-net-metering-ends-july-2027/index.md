Maryland homeowners who sign for rooftop solar now face a hard stop on today's net metering terms. Under the Utility RELIEF Act, utilities must offer the current net metering contract only until **the earlier of** statewide customer-generator capacity reaching 3,000 megawatts (MW) or **July 1, 2027**. After that, a successor program set by the Maryland Public Service Commission (PSC) takes over. The Act says that program should pay out less. What it will pay is still undecided.

Net metering, as Maryland's law defines it, is the measurement of the difference between the electricity supplied by the utility and the electricity a customer's own system generates and feeds back to the grid over the billing period. How exports are credited drives solar payback, so the rules matter.

## What the law says, and what it leaves open

| Question | What is settled | What is not |
|---|---|---|
| Law | House Bill 1532, the Utility RELIEF Act of 2026, approved by the Governor on May 12, 2026 as Chapter 353 | |
| Current net metering offer | Utilities must offer the standard contract, first come, first served, until the earlier of 3,000 MW statewide or July 1, 2027 | Which step counts toward the cutoff (application, approval or in-service date) is not defined in the passages we reviewed |
| Replacement program | The PSC must approve a framework by February 1, 2027 for a program beginning July 1, 2027 | The credit for exported power |
| Direction on pay | The General Assembly's stated intent is incentives "less than" current net metering | By how much |
| Existing customers | Those under a net metering contract on July 1, 2027 stay eligible for a period set by PSC regulations | How long |
| Successor program size | Available until combined capacity of both programs reaches 6,000 MW | |

Two capacity numbers appear in the Act and are easy to confuse. **3,000 MW** is the limit on the current program. **6,000 MW** is the combined limit across the current and successor programs.

## The timeline

## What the successor program must do

The Act directs the PSC to design a program that:

- provides incentives for distributed generation (small-scale generation at or near where power is used), though smaller than today's;
- minimizes ratepayer costs in both the short and long term; and
- balances fair compensation for exports, and the benefit of reduced load on the grid, against grid needs, ratepayer costs and effects on customers who do not have solar, including low- and moderate-income customers.

The PSC is also directed to prioritize applications from customers who were in the utility's net metering interconnection queue when the successor program started. Because the statute leaves the export credit to the PSC, any figure you see quoted today for "net metering 2.0" in Maryland is a guess, not a rule.

## Who this affects

- **A homeowner who signs before July 1, 2027** is exposed to the cutoff question. The Act says the contract is offered "until" the earlier of the two events, but the passages we reviewed do not say whether application, approval or in-service date decides who gets in. The PSC's regulations and utility tariffs will matter. Ask your installer how they define it and what happens if the project slips.
- **A homeowner who already has a net metered system** is not cut off on July 1, 2027. They remain eligible for a period the PSC will set.
- **A homeowner who waits** will get the successor program's terms, which are not yet known and which the Act says should be less generous.

This is not a recommendation to rush. A hurried contract with a vague in-service date can leave a buyer with neither net metering nor the project they wanted. ## Questions to ask an installer in Maryland

1. Which event does the contract use to decide whether I get today's net metering: application, utility approval, or permission to operate?
2. If the utility's approval slips past July 1, 2027, who bears the cost of the change?
3. Does your savings estimate assume full retail credit for exports? If yes, what does it show if the credit is lower?
4. Is the system a purchase, lease or power purchase agreement, and which program would it interconnect under?

## Permitting rules in the same Act

The Act also changes county and municipal permitting for residential rooftop solar. For systems installed on a residential rooftop with a capacity under 20 kilowatts (measured as the inverter's alternating-current rating), and permitted through solar permitting software, a county or municipality may not set a permit fee above $500 beginning August 1, 2027. A third-party payment processing charge may be added on top. Counties and municipalities must also complete required inspections for software-permitted projects within an average of 5 business days of a complete request.

## Limitations

- We reviewed the enrolled text of HB 1532 and the PSC's May 14, 2026 notice. We did not review the PC 78 docket filings, utility tariffs or any implementing regulations, and we found no PSC order setting the successor credit as of October 9, 2026.
- We did not find a published figure for Maryland's current customer-generator capacity relative to the 3,000 MW limit.
- The Act's definition of "eligible customer-generator" and how it applies to leased systems were not reviewed in full.
