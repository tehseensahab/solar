Kansas did not have a new base rate change on October 1, 2026 that we could find. Evergy's most recent increase started one year earlier, with the October 2025 billing cycle.

This article covers Evergy's Kansas Central territory. It does not cover Evergy Kansas Metro, Liberty, cooperatives or municipal utilities.

## Quick Summary

| Item | Detail |

| Decision date | September 25, 2025 |
| Approved | $128 million a year |

## What the Commission Approved

The Kansas Corporation Commission (KCC) approved a unanimous settlement in docket 25-EKCE-294-RTS:

- **Revenue:** $128 million, about 65% of the request.
- **Bills:** about $8.47 a month for a residential customer using 900 kWh.
- **Return on equity:** 9.7% for transmission delivery charges. One commissioner, Dwight Keen, dissented in part on that point, citing affordability.

**Our math:** $128 million ÷ $196.4 million = 65%.

## Why Your October 2026 Bill Can Still Differ From Last Year

- **Fuel and purchased power.** Recovered through a separate adjustment.
- **Transmission delivery charge.** Updated on its own schedule.
- **Weather.** October usage varies a lot from year to year.

## What This Means for Homeowners With Solar

Kansas requires Evergy and the state's other investor-owned utility to offer net metering, though the credit for surplus is slightly below retail.

- **Monthly surplus:** EnergySage reports that leftover energy at the end of a billing cycle is converted to a dollar credit at the utility's monthly system average cost per kWh, which is a little under the retail rate.
- **Expiry:** credits carry forward month to month and expire each year on March 31.
- **Smaller utilities:** may offer "parallel generation" instead, paying 1.5 times the avoided cost for exported power.
- **Property tax:** systems installed after 2016 get a 10-year exemption on the added property value.

**What it means:** because credits expire every March 31, a system that overproduces in summer and fall can lose value if you do not use those credits over the winter. Size to your annual use, not above it.

Rules checked October 5, 2026. Solar tariffs change, so confirm the current terms with your utility before you sign a contract. Our [solar payback guide](/posts/solar-payback-period-without-federal-tax-credit/) shows how the retail rate and the export credit change the math.

## Compare With Nearby States

**Statewide average for context:** the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) puts Kansas's average residential price at 15.27¢/kWh in July 2026, up 5.6% from 14.46¢ in July 2025. The U.S. average was 18.31¢. At 1,000 kWh that works out to $152.70 (1,000 × 15.27¢). This is total residential revenue divided by kWh sold across every utility in the state, fixed charges included, and it is a preliminary estimate. It is not any one utility's rate.
