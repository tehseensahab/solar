Kansas did not have a new base rate change on October 1, 2026 that we could find. Evergy's most recent increase started one year earlier, with the October 2025 billing cycle.

This article covers Evergy's Kansas Central territory. It does not cover Evergy Kansas Metro, Liberty, cooperatives or municipal utilities.

## Quick Summary

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Decision date | September 25, 2025 |
| Approved | $128 million a year |
| Requested | $196.4 million a year |
| Residential impact | About $8.47 a month at 900 kWh |
| Effective | October 2025 billing cycle |
| October 2026 | No new base rate change found |

## What the Commission Approved

The Kansas Corporation Commission (KCC) approved a unanimous settlement in docket 25-EKCE-294-RTS:

- **Revenue:** $128 million, about 65% of the request.
- **Bills:** about $8.47 a month for a residential customer using 900 kWh.
- **Return on equity:** 9.7% for transmission delivery charges. One commissioner, Dwight Keen, dissented in part on that point, citing affordability.

**Our math:** $128 million ÷ $196.4 million = 65%.

## Why Your October 2026 Bill Can Still Differ From Last Year

- **Fuel and purchased power.** Recovered through a separate adjustment.
- **Transmission delivery charge.** Updated on its own schedule.
- **Weather.** October usage varies a lot from year to year.

## What This Means for Homeowners With Solar

We did not verify Kansas's current net metering or export credit rules for this article, so we give no export rates here. Ask your utility for its current solar tariff, and check how exported energy is credited before sizing a system. Fixed monthly charges are not reduced by solar.
