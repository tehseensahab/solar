Arizona did not have an approved rate change at its largest regulated utility on October 1, 2026. Arizona Public Service (APS) and Tucson Electric Power (TEP) both have increases pending before the Arizona Corporation Commission (ACC).

This article covers APS, which serves about 1.5 million customers, and TEP, which serves about 455,000. Salt River Project (SRP) sets rates through its own board and is not covered.

## Quick Summary

| Item | Utility | Status | Residential impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Last base rate increase | APS | Effective March 1, 2024 | About 8.3%, $10 to $12 a month |
| Pending rate case | APS | Vote due by Dec. 31, 2026 | About 16%, roughly $20 a month (request) |
| Pending rate case | TEP | Not decided; TEP expects approval in late 2026 | About 12%, $12.92 a month at 638 kWh (request) |
| October 1, 2026 | APS | n/a | No approved change found |

## APS: What Is Proposed

- **Revenue:** $579.2 million.
- **Bills:** an average 16% for residential customers, about $20 a month, according to KJZZ. Small, medium and large businesses would see 6% to 8%. Data centers would see about 45%.
- **Return on equity:** APS asked for 10.7%. The state's residential consumer office recommended about 9%.
- **Formula rates:** APS wants annual formula-based adjustments for five years in place of traditional rate cases. Consumer advocates say that would mean yearly increases with less scrutiny.

**Timeline, per the Arizona Corporation Commission:** the hearing began May 18, 2026 and concluded July 7 after 31 days. The administrative law judge's Recommended Opinion and Order is expected by late November 2026. The deadline for the commission to vote on it is December 31, 2026. New rates are expected in early 2027.

## TEP: Still Pending

TEP asked for new rates from September 2026, but the case has not been decided. TEP's rate page, checked on October 4, 2026, says the company "anticipates that the review will conclude with approval of new rates in late 2026." The docket is E-01933A-25-0103.

**What TEP says its request would do:**

| Plan | Monthly increase |
|---|---|
| Basic plan, median use of 638 kWh | $12.92 |
| Basic plan, summer median of 823 kWh | $14.14 |
| Time-of-use plan | $13.32 |
| Demand plan | $15.59 |

TEP describes this as 11.6%, or about 12%. News reports earlier in the year described the request as 13% to 14% and about $172 million, with a 10.5% return on equity against 9.55% today. The Attorney General's office argued TEP could maintain reliability with 4%.

The hearing ran from April 22 to May 12, 2026. The commission still has to receive a recommended order and vote at an open meeting.

**If you are a TEP customer:** your rates did not change on September 1. Until the commission votes, the current rates apply.

## What This Means for Homeowners With Solar

Arizona ended net metering for new customers under a 2016 Arizona Corporation Commission decision (Decision No. 75859). APS and TEP now use net billing.

- **How it works:** solar you use as it is produced offsets your usage at the retail rate. Power you export earns a lower, fixed export rate.
- **APS export rate:** about 6.17¢ per kWh for systems interconnected in 2026, according to an installer summary. The rate is locked for 10 years from interconnection.
- **Yearly reset:** the rate offered to new customers can fall by no more than 10% a year. The same summary says APS proposed about 5.55¢ from September 2026, pending approval.
- **TEP export rate:** about 5¢ to 6¢ per kWh, with a lock of up to 10 years.
- **Grid access charge:** APS has proposed doubling the monthly charge solar customers pay, which KJZZ described as currently $2 to $3.

**What it means:** at roughly 6¢ for exports against a retail rate around 15¢, solar you use yourself is worth more than twice what you export. Systems sized to daytime use, or paired with a battery, pay back faster. A larger fixed charge would lengthen payback.

Rules checked October 5, 2026. Solar tariffs change, so confirm the current terms with your utility before you sign a contract. Our [solar payback guide](/posts/solar-payback-period-without-federal-tax-credit/) shows how the retail rate and the export credit change the math.

## What to Check on Your Bill

1. Your rate plan. Most APS and TEP residential plans are time-of-use, so averages may not match your bill.
2. The basic service charge and any solar grid access charge.
3. Adjustor lines for fuel and purchased power.

For help sorting the lines on your bill, see our guide to [reading your electric bill](/posts/how-to-read-your-electric-bill/).

## What Happens Next

- **Late November 2026:** expected recommendation in the APS case.
- **By December 31, 2026:** ACC vote on APS.
- **Late 2026:** TEP expects a decision.
- **Early 2027:** new APS rates, if approved.

## Limitations

All APS and TEP figures here are requests, not approved rates. The ACC's docket system could not be opened, so timelines come from the commission's news posts and TEP's rate page. We did not find the date of the commission's TEP vote. SRP and electric cooperatives were not researched.

## Compare With Nearby States

**Statewide average for context:** the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) puts Arizona's average residential price at 15.38¢/kWh in July 2026, up 0.3% from 15.34¢ in July 2025. The U.S. average was 18.31¢. At 1,000 kWh that works out to $153.80 (1,000 × 15.38¢). This is total residential revenue divided by kWh sold across every utility in the state, fixed charges included, and it is a preliminary estimate. It is not any one utility's rate.
