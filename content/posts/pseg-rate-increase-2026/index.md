**PSE&G did not have a large electric rate increase in 2026; its rates were roughly flat. A typical home's bill is about $10 a month lower than it was in January, almost entirely because a temporary $10.00 monthly charge that repaid the summer 2025 credits ended after February. June's $3.23 supply cut at 650 kWh was largely offset by an estimated $3.11 transmission increase, and the October 1 changes roughly cancelled each other out.**

If you searched for a "PSE&G rate increase 2026," you may be remembering that $10 charge, or a different utility. Our [overview of New Jersey electric rates in 2026](/posts/new-jersey-electricity-rate-changes-2026/) covers JCP&L, Atlantic City Electric and Rockland Electric; [JCP&L's pending rate request](/posts/jcpl-rate-increase-request-2026/) is covered separately. This article lists every PSE&G residential electric change in 2026 we could trace to a BPU order or notice, with the docket number and the dollars a month. It covers electric service only, not PSE&G gas.

Note on the bases: the BPU's supply and transmission figures use a 650 kWh month. PSE&G's rider orders use 6,700 kWh a year (558 kWh in an average month, 683 kWh in a summer month). The dollar effects are close enough to add for a rough total, but they are not on one identical bill.

### The $10 deferral charge, and why it ended

In June 2025 the BPU approved a PSE&G plan to spread out bill impacts in summer 2025. The order says: "For the two (2)-month period July through August 2025, PSE&G will apply a credit of $30.00," including sales tax, "to each residential electric customer's monthly bill." The credits were then paid back: "For the six (6)-month period beginning September 2025 through February 2026, PSE&G will apply a charge to each residential electric customer's monthly bill of $10.00, including SUT."

That charge is gone from bills after February 2026, which is the largest single change in a PSE&G bill this year.

### June 1, 2026: supply prices fell slightly

New Jersey sets default supply prices through an annual Basic Generation Service auction. The BPU certified the 2026 results on February 12, 2026, and its release lists PSE&G at 650 kWh going from $183.46 to $180.23 a month on June 1, 2026, a change of −$3.23 (−1.8%), "before credits or offsets are applied." Effective the same June 1 date, a BPU order from its May 21, 2026 agenda on PSE&G's Societal Benefits Charge cut an average monthly bill by $0.05.

A separate BPU order dated June 30, 2026 approved the utilities' updates to the transmission charges inside BGS rates. The filing estimated the PSE&G change at +$3.11 a month at 650 kWh, including sales tax, and asked for a June 1 effective date. The part of the order we could read did not restate the date or the dollar figure as approved, so treat +$3.11 as the utilities' estimate.

### October 1, 2026: four small changes

On September 24, 2026, the BPU approved two PSE&G infrastructure roll-ins, both effective for service on and after October 1, 2026, and both "on a provisional basis, subject to refund and review for prudence in the Company's next base rate case":

- **Infrastructure Advancement Program:** PSE&G sought $13.60 million a year from electric customers; the approved settlement recovers $10.31 million. For a typical customer using 6,700 kWh a year, the BPU says that "would see an increase in the average monthly bill of $0.30 or approximately 0.2%."
- **Clean Energy Future-Electric Vehicle program:** PSE&G asked for $9.243 million, later updated to about $8.147 million, which the BPU approved. The order puts the effect at "an increase in the monthly bill from $155.20 to $155.43 or $0.23."
- **Transmission:** a September 2026 order approved another round of transmission rate changes "effective October 1, 2026." The petition estimated +$0.02 a month for PSE&G at 650 kWh.
- **Universal Service Fund and Lifeline:** these statewide charges fund bill help for low-income households. For rates effective October 1, 2026, the combined charge fell from $0.004508 to $0.003585 per kWh, including sales tax.

**USF math:** $0.004508 − $0.003585 = $0.000923 per kWh. At 650 kWh a month, that is $0.60 less a month. The BPU's own figure, a $7.20 annual decrease at 7,800 kWh a year, works out to the same $0.60 a month.

## What Is the Net Change for a Typical PSE&G Home?

Adding the recurring changes in the table, January 2026 to November 2026:

−$10.00 (deferral charge ended) − $3.23 (June supply) − $0.05 (June SBC) + $3.11 (transmission, estimate) + $0.30 (IAP) + $0.23 (CEF-EV) + $0.02 (October transmission) − $0.60 (USF and Lifeline) = **about −$10.22 a month**

Almost all of that is the temporary $10.00 charge ending. Without it, the 2026 rate changes net to about −$0.22 a month.

If the $3.11 transmission estimate was already reflected in June's −$3.23, the drop would be about $13.33 instead; the order text we read does not settle that, so we use the smaller figure. The one-time $25 credit in August is left out because it does not repeat.

This is a rough guide, not a bill quote. It mixes 650 kWh and 558 kWh bases, leaves out PSE&G's smaller quarterly reconciliation charges, and assumes you buy supply from PSE&G. If you buy from a third-party supplier, the BGS rows do not apply to you; check your supplier contract. To find each line on your own bill, see our guide to [reading your electric bill](/posts/how-to-read-your-electric-bill/).

## Is a PSE&G Rate Increase Coming in 2027?

Possibly, on the supply side, but no number exists yet. The 2027 Basic Generation Service auctions will set supply prices for the year starting June 1, 2027. PSE&G's notice in BPU docket ER26040105 asks the Board to approve buying supply "through a statewide competitive auction process" "with new BGS charges to become effective June 1, 2027," and says: "Auction prices are converted to customer charges, which may be higher or lower than current charges, depending on the outcome of the auction consistent with market conditions."

The October 1 infrastructure rates are provisional, so they can still be adjusted in PSE&G's next base rate case. We did not find a pending PSE&G base rate case. For how these cases work, see our explainer on [electric rate cases](/posts/how-electric-rate-cases-work/).

## What PSE&G's 2026 Changes Mean for Solar Owners

PSE&G's net metering banks kilowatt-hours, not dollars, during the year. PSE&G says "one credit is added to the bank for each excess kilowatt-hour (kWh)," and banked credits offset later months. At the end of your 12-month year, "there's an Anniversary True Up when we calculate the monetary value of any remaining energy credits," using a market price that "does not include delivery and system charges."

What that means for this year's changes:

- **Per-kWh changes move your solar savings a little.** Each kWh your panels offset avoids that kWh's supply and per-kWh delivery charges. June's small supply cut made each offset kWh worth slightly less; October's USF cut did the same by less than 0.1 cent.
- **Flat charges do not change your solar math.** The $10.00 deferral charge and the $25 credit were the same for every home, solar or not.
- **Leftover credits are valued differently.** If your system overproduces for the year, the excess is valued at a market price that excludes delivery and system charges, not at the retail rate. Sizing a system to your annual use avoids that.

Our [net metering state guide](/posts/net-metering-state-guide/) explains how New Jersey's rules compare with other states'.

**Update, Nov. 21, 2026:** the Board of Public Utilities' review of net metering for new customers (Docket No. QO24090723) was still open with no successor adopted as of Nov. 21; in May 2026 the BPU said there were "no new developments respecting net metering." See [what New Jersey's net metering review could mean for new solar customers](/posts/new-jersey-net-metering-2026/).

## Who This Applies To

PSE&G residential electric customers on default supply. Business customers, PSE&G gas bills, and PSEG Long Island customers in New York are not covered here. PSEG Long Island is a separate utility.

## Limitations

We added changes that use slightly different typical-usage assumptions, and we did not include PSE&G's quarterly reconciliation charges or its gas rates. The June 30 transmission figure is the utilities' estimate. Provisional October rates can change after review. Your bill depends on your usage, your supply choice and any assistance programs you are enrolled in.
