**Oncor's delivery charges for a home using 1,000 kWh a month are now $72.73, up from $60.41 in May 2026, an increase of $12.32, or about 20%.** The percentage varies with usage: about 19% at 500 kWh and 21% at 2,000 kWh. That is our math from the residential rates in Oncor's own tariff, effective Oct. 4, 2026: $4.06 a month in fixed charges plus 6.8673 cents per kWh. These are delivery charges only. The price per kWh in your retail plan is separate and comes from your provider.

The increase came in three steps between June and October. For how Oncor fits into a Texas bill and what other utilities are doing, see our [Texas electricity rates and solar guide](/posts/texas-electricity-rate-changes-2026/). If kWh and kW on the bill are confusing, start with [kW vs kWh](/posts/kw-vs-kwh/).

## How Much Did Oncor's Rates Go Up in 2026?

Oncor's residential delivery charges have two parts: a fixed monthly amount (a $1.48 customer charge plus a $2.58 metering charge, or $4.06) and a set of per-kWh charges. Here is what a home pays in delivery charges at each step this year.

| Period | Fixed per month | Per kWh | 500 kWh | 1,000 kWh | 1,500 kWh | 2,000 kWh |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Before June 1, 2026 | $4.23 | 5.6183¢ | $32.32 | $60.41 | $88.50 | $116.60 |
| June 1, 2026 (new base rates) | $4.06 | 6.1196¢ | $34.66 | $65.26 | $95.85 | $126.45 |
| Aug. 1, 2026 (surcharge in, transmission charge down) | $4.06 | 6.0295¢ | $34.21 | $64.36 | $94.50 | $124.65 |
| Oct. 4, 2026 (now; temporary rates pending PUC order) | $4.06 | 6.8673¢ | $38.40 | $72.73 | $107.07 | $141.41 |

How we calculated it: delivery charge = fixed charge + (per-kWh total × kWh). The Oct. 4 per-kWh total adds every residential per-kWh line in Oncor's tariff (listed in the next section). Earlier totals come from the same tariff sheets plus Oncor's 2025 residential rate table. Our results match the figures published by BKV Energy, a retail provider, for each date. Figures exclude your retail energy charge, taxes and any provider fees.

At 1,000 kWh, the change from May to now is $72.73 − $60.41 = **$12.32 a month, about 20%** of the delivery portion of the bill. Your total bill rises by a smaller percentage, because your retail energy charge is also on the bill.

## Why Did Oncor Raise Rates in 2026?

Three separate decisions and filings stack up.

### 1. New base rates from the 2026 rate case (June 1)

On April 17, 2026, the Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUC) approved a final order in Oncor's base rate case, Docket 58306, according to an 8-K filing by Oncor's parent, Sempra. The filing says the order allows an annual revenue requirement of about $6.97 billion, "an increase of approximately $560 million, or 8.7%," with a 9.75% return on equity, up from 9.70%. Oncor's second-quarter report says the new base rates "became effective on June 1, 2026."

On a bill, the base rate change mostly showed up as a higher distribution system charge (2.5344 cents per kWh in Oncor's 2025 rate table, 3.6043 cents now), while the separate distribution recovery factor dropped to zero on June 1. The fixed charges fell slightly, from $4.23 to $4.06.

For how a rate case like this works, see [how electric rate cases work](/posts/how-electric-rate-cases-work/).

### 2. A temporary surcharge (Aug. 1 to December 2026)

The order let Oncor recover the difference between old and new rates for Jan. 1 through June 1, 2026, when the new rates took effect. Oncor says that on Aug. 1, 2026 it "implemented a temporary surcharge" that reflects "approximately $212 million of deferred revenues," to be recovered "through the end of the year." Oncor puts the effect at about $3.63 a month for a residential customer using 1,000 kWh. Oncor's tariff lists it as Rider IS at 0.3633 cents per kWh and says it "shall remain in effect through December 2026."

### 3. Higher transmission and distribution recovery charges (Oct. 4)

For bills rendered on and after Oct. 4, 2026, the residential TCRF rose from 1.9046 to 2.1669 cents per kWh, and the DCRF, which had been zero since June 1, was reset to 0.5755 cents per kWh. The new rates are on Oncor's tariff; the earlier figures come from trade press and match BKV Energy's published totals. Together that is 0.8378 cents per kWh, or **$8.38 a month at 1,000 kWh**, the biggest single step this year.

These factors let Oncor update charges for transmission costs and new grid investment between rate cases. Oncor's third-quarter 2026 report cites revenue "related to the Unified Tracker Mechanism" and, in our reading of the report, higher interim distribution recovery rates. A trade publication, Texas Commercial Plans, reports that the Oct. 4 rates are temporary rates in PUC Docket 59249 and that the PUC had not issued a final order as of Oct. 2. The same report says any difference between the temporary and final rates must be refunded or credited against future bills. We could not open the PUC docket to confirm its status, so treat the docket details as reported, not verified. A final order could change these rates.

## Will Oncor Charges Go Down in January 2027?

Part of the October level is temporary. Rider IS is scheduled to end after December 2026. If nothing else changed, a home using 1,000 kWh would pay about **$69.10** in delivery charges ($4.06 + 6.5040 cents × 1,000 kWh, our math), still $8.69 a month more than in May.

That "if" matters. Oncor's transmission and distribution recovery factors are updated during the year, and the tracker case may still change the October rates. Watch for a notice from your retail provider.

## When Will You See the Increase on Your Bill?

Oncor owns the poles and wires in its delivery area but does not bill homes directly. Your retail electricity provider charges you and passes Oncor's delivery charges through.

How that shows up depends on your plan:

- **Plans that list delivery charges separately** should show the new rates on bills rendered after they take effect.
- **Fixed-rate and bundled plans** may or may not pass changes through during the contract. Your plan's Electricity Facts Label (EFL) and terms of service say whether changes in delivery (TDU) charges are passed on.
- **Shopping for a plan?** Compare offers at the same usage level and check whether the advertised price includes Oncor's charges. Our guide to [comparing electricity offers](/posts/how-to-compare-electricity-supplier-offers/) walks through it.

## What Oncor's Rate Increase Means for Solar Owners

Nearly all of Oncor's delivery charge is billed per kWh: 6.8673 of the cents on every kWh now, compared with 5.6183 cents in May. In our reading, if your plan passes those charges through per kWh, that makes solar power you use at home more valuable, because each kWh you produce and use yourself is a kWh you do not buy from the grid, along with its delivery charges.

Exports work differently. In Texas's competitive market, what you earn for kWh you send to the grid depends on your retail plan's buyback terms, not on Oncor's tariff. Check a buyback plan's EFL for the export credit and for any limits before you switch. Sizing a new system?
