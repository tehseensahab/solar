**Duke Energy Florida residential bills dip slightly in January 2027. Duke says a home using 1,000 kWh a month will pay about $0.95 less than in December 2026, and $33 less than in January 2026, after the Florida Public Service Commission (PSC) approved its request.** From March 2027, Duke says bills fall about another $10 per 1,000 kWh as its yearly March-to-November reduction returns.

Duke announced the approval on Nov. 3, 2026. The PSC's written order was not posted when we checked on Nov. 24, so the dollar figures below are Duke's. For how Florida's other utilities price power, see our [Florida electricity rates and solar guide](/rates/florida/).

## What Changes for Duke Energy Florida Customers in 2027?

Duke's Nov. 3 release lists three residential changes for a customer using 1,000 kWh a month:

| Change | Amount (1,000 kWh a month) | When |
|---|---|---|
| December 2026 to January 2027 | About $0.95 lower | January 2027 bills |
| January 2027 vs. January 2026 | $33 lower | January 2027 |
| Seasonal reduction | About $10 lower | March to November 2027 |

Duke describes the March change as "a temporary reduction DEF implements every March through November to offer relief when energy use is often highest." It is not new: Duke's May 2026 release said 2026 bills also had "an approximately $11 decrease instituted by the company every March through November."

For commercial and industrial customers, Duke says the December-to-January change is a decrease of "between 0.9% and 3.8%," depending on the account.

## Why Is January 2027 $33 Lower Than January 2026?

Because January 2026 still had a hurricane charge. Duke's Nov. 4, 2025 release said January and February 2026 bills would "temporarily increase by $7.54" at 1,000 kWh. Its May 29, 2026 release said the storm cost recovery charge for Hurricanes Debby, Helene and Milton was removed early, cutting bills by "approximately $33" in February 2026.

So the $33 is mostly a comparison with a bill that included a charge that has since ended. Compared with the bill you will get this December, January's change is under $1.

## Why Your December Bill Goes Up First

Duke Energy Florida's base rate changes with the season. The PSC's 2026 bill comparison for 1,000 kWh says: "Duke's 2026 base rates for June - November bill are $91.74; for the December 2026 - February 2027 bill are $102.48." That is a $10.74 step up in December before taxes, close to the roughly $10 reduction Duke applies each March.

Here is the PSC's October-to-December 2026 breakdown for a 1,000 kWh Duke Energy Florida bill. The base line is the PSC's yearly weighted average, not the base charge on any single month's bill.

| Charge (1,000 kWh, Oct. to Dec. 2026) | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base (weighted yearly average) | $94.43 |
| Fuel | $41.27 |
| Storm protection plan | $9.36 |
| Energy conservation (ECCR) | $3.86 |
| Asset securitization | $2.38 |
| Capacity | $1.33 |
| Environmental (ECRC) | $0.40 |
| Storm restoration recovery | $0.00 |
| Gross receipts tax and regulatory fee | $4.06 |
| **Total (PSC table)** | **$157.09** |

The PSC computes that base line as (($102.48 × 3) + ($91.74 × 9)) ÷ 12 = $94.43. The storm restoration line is $0.00; Duke says the hurricane charge came off bills in February 2026.

## Why Are the Rates Lower?

Duke gives two reasons:

- **No 2% base rate increase.** Duke's Sept. 3, 2026 filing release said a "2% increase in the base rate," set by the PSC in 2024, "is being avoided altogether" through a tax strategy that Duke says delivers "$50 million in customer savings next year."
- **Lower fuel costs.** The same release said, "Some costs, like fuel, are decreasing."

The approved residential cut is larger than requested. In September, Duke said residential customers "should see a $0.71 decrease on their bills from December 2026 to January 2027"; after approval it put the change at about $0.95.

Clause charges such as fuel, capacity, conservation and storm protection are reset by the PSC each year. Some 2027 factors were still proposals in the PSC records we found. For example, Duke proposed a 2027 residential storm protection factor of 1.364 cents per kWh, about $13.64 per 1,000 kWh before taxes, versus $9.36 in the 2026 table. Duke's overall figures already reflect its approved 2027 package, but the PSC's line-by-line 2027 table was not yet posted.

## What This Means for Solar Homeowners

For Duke Energy Florida customers with solar, a slightly lower per-kWh price makes each self-used solar kWh worth slightly less, because it avoids a cheaper kWh from Duke. The change is small: under $1 a month at 1,000 kWh between December and January. Seasonal swings matter more. Winter bills carry the higher base charge, and March to November bills the lower one, the months when, Duke says, energy use is often highest. Our [Duke Energy Florida net metering guide](/posts/duke-energy-florida-net-metering/) explains how exported kWh are credited.

If you are comparing utilities, FPL has proposed a typical 1,000 kWh bill of $141.87 from January 2027, up from $136.64 in 2026 but below its earlier projection; that proposal needs PSC approval. See our [FPL rates 2026 guide](/posts/fpl-rates-2026/).

## What to Check on Your Bill

1. Compare your January 2027 bill with December 2026, not January 2026, to see the actual 2027 change.
2. Check your usage. Duke's figures are for 1,000 kWh; the dollar change scales with your kWh.
3. Look for the March 2027 drop on your first March bill.

## Limitations

All 2027 dollar changes are Duke's figures from its Nov. 3, 2026 release. The PSC's written order, vote and 2027 bill comparison were not available when we checked on Nov. 24, 2026. The explanation of the $33 gap is our reading of Duke's 2025 and 2026 releases. PSC table amounts are for 1,000 kWh and include gross receipts tax but not local taxes or fees.
