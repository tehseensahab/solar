The average U.S. home paid **$150.68 a month** for electricity in 2025, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration's (EIA) final annual data. That is up 5.9% from $142.26 in 2024. The highest state averages were in Hawaii ($207.89) and Connecticut ($206.46). The lowest were in New Mexico ($94.65) and Utah ($99.06).

Those are residential customers only, averaged over the whole year. A bill in July or January for your home will differ, and so will a bill from your own utility.

## The Short Version

| Measure | 2024 | 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Average monthly residential bill | $142.26 | $150.68 | +$8.42 (+5.9%) |
| Average monthly use | 863 kWh | 874 kWh | +1.3% |
| Average price | 16.48¢/kWh | 17.23¢/kWh | +4.6% |

Most of the 2025 increase came from price, not from using more electricity. **kWh** (kilowatt-hour) is the unit of energy your meter counts. Price here means total residential revenue divided by total kWh sold, so it includes fixed monthly charges.

Hawaii's average bill is about 2.2 times New Mexico's ($207.89 ÷ $94.65). The median of the 50 states' averages is $147.43, so a few expensive states lift the national mean slightly above the middle state. Twenty-one states were above the national average of $150.68.

## Why a High Rate Does Not Always Mean a High Bill

A bill is roughly **price × usage**. Two states can reach the same bill from opposite directions.

| | Avg. price | Avg. monthly use | Avg. monthly bill |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hawaii | 40.40¢/kWh | 515 kWh | $207.89 |
| Maryland | 19.53¢/kWh | 944 kWh | $184.42 |
| Alabama | 16.09¢/kWh | 1,145 kWh | $184.31 |
| Louisiana | 12.52¢/kWh | 1,225 kWh | $153.32 |
| New Mexico | 14.85¢/kWh | 638 kWh | $94.65 |

- **Hawaii** has the nation's highest price and below-average use. It tops the bill ranking because of price alone.
- **Alabama and Louisiana** have some of the lowest prices in the country but the highest use, so their bills are above the U.S. average. Louisiana's average use of 1,225 kWh a month is about 2.4 times Hawaii's 515 kWh.
- **New Mexico** has a middling price and low use, which gives the lowest bill.

EIA's data give the averages but not the reasons for different usage. Climate, how many homes heat with electricity, home size and appliance mix are all plausible factors, but this table cannot separate them. Our [winter heating cost guide](/posts/winter-heating-costs-2026-27/) covers EIA's forecasts for electric-heated homes.

## What This Data Does and Does Not Tell You

- **It is an average of all residential customers in a state,** across every utility, in every month of 2025. Summer and winter bills are blended.
- **It is not a rate.** The bill includes the fixed monthly charges. See [how to read your electric bill](/posts/how-to-read-your-electric-bill/).
- **It is not what you will pay in 2026.** These are 2025 figures. EIA's most recent monthly data show higher prices in 2026: for July 2026 the national average residential price was 18.31¢/kWh, against 17.45¢ in July 2025 (see our [July 2026 price report](/posts/electricity-prices-by-state-july-2026/)). Those are single-month prices and not comparable to the 2025 annual average of 17.23¢.
- **Customers with rooftop solar are included.** They count as customers. Electricity they generate and use themselves is generally not sold by the utility, so it is not in the sales figure. Their lower purchases pull the state average down slightly; EIA's table does not separate them.
- **Averages hide spread.** A state's average bill sits between very small and very large bills.

## What It Means If You Are Considering Solar

Solar offsets kWh you would otherwise buy. Two states with the same bill can have very different savings, because the value of each kWh differs.

| State | Avg. 2025 price | Value of 100 kWh a month at that price |
|---|---|---|
| Hawaii | 40.40¢/kWh | $40.40 |
| Connecticut | 29.31¢/kWh | $29.31 |
| U.S. | 17.23¢/kWh | $17.23 |
| Alabama | 16.09¢/kWh | $16.09 |
| Louisiana | 12.52¢/kWh | $12.52 |

This is simple multiplication, not a savings estimate. Real savings depend on your utility's rate design, fixed charges (which solar does not reduce), whether the power you use at home or send to the grid is credited at the retail rate or a lower export rate, and when your panels produce. See our guides to [fixed charges and solar savings](/posts/fixed-charges-and-solar-savings/), [solar export credit rates](/posts/solar-export-credit-rates-by-utility-2026/), and the [solar payback calculation](/posts/solar-payback-period-without-federal-tax-credit/), or try the [payback calculator](/solar-payback-calculator/).

The most useful number for a solar quote is not the state average but your own: add up 12 months of kWh from your bills and divide by 12.

## How We Calculated It

We downloaded EIA's "Historical State Data" workbook for Form EIA-861 (Total Electric Industry sheet), which EIA updated with final 2025 data on October 7, 2026, and took residential revenue (thousand dollars), sales (megawatt-hours) and customers (count) for each state.

- **Average monthly bill** = revenue × 1,000 ÷ customers ÷ 12
- **Average monthly use (kWh)** = sales × 1,000 ÷ customers ÷ 12
- **Average price (¢/kWh)** = revenue ÷ sales × 100

As a check, we applied the same formulas to the 2024 data and compared them with EIA's own published "2024 Average Monthly Bill – Residential" table. The U.S. total ($142.26, 863.3 kWh, 16.48¢) and the customer count and average bill for all 50 states and D.C. matched. We could not find a published 2025 version of that table on EIA's site on the day of writing, so the 2025 figures are our calculations from EIA's final data. Percentage changes are our calculations.

## Limitations

These are annual averages for 2025 and say nothing about any individual utility or household. EIA may revise its figures. We did not find a documented explanation for Nevada's change and have not tried to infer one. The 2025 average-bill table EIA normally publishes in its sales and revenue tables was not available on its site when we checked, so all 2025 bills here are our own arithmetic on EIA's final data.
