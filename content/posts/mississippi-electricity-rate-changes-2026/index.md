Mississippi has a small October 2026 item, and it is a decrease. Entergy Mississippi asked regulators to lower a typical bill by about 41 cents a month. Earlier in the summer, its annual rate plan left rates flat.

This article covers Entergy Mississippi, which serves about 459,000 customers in 45 counties. It does not cover Mississippi Power, TVA-served areas or cooperatives.

## Quick Summary

| Item | Timing | Residential impact |
|---|---|---|
| Annual formula rate plan | July 2026 billing cycle | No increase; $4.87 a month avoided, per Entergy |
| Ad valorem tax adjustment | Filed for October 2026 | About −$0.41 a month |

## The July 2026 Rate Plan

Entergy Mississippi's rates are reviewed every year under a formula rate plan. For 2026, Entergy says the Mississippi Public Service Commission approved a plan that prevented a $4.87 monthly increase that would otherwise have started in July.

The company attributes that to revenue from data center customers. It says about $18 million of a $300 million reliability program is included in the plan.

## The October Filing

In a news release dated August 27, 2026, Entergy said it had filed an additional reduction of about $0.41 a month that would take effect in October if approved. It relates to the ad valorem (property) tax adjustment. We found no report of the commission's vote. In an earlier year, the same adjustment lowered a typical 1,000 kWh bill by 29 cents.

## What to Keep in Mind

- These figures come from Entergy's own announcement.
- "Avoided increase" is a comparison with what would have happened, not a cut to your bill.
- Fuel costs are recovered separately and change through the year.
- Entergy plans three new natural gas plants with more than 2,100 megawatts of capacity. Their costs will reach rates in later years.

## What This Means for Homeowners With Solar

Mississippi uses net billing under a Public Service Commission rule that covers Entergy Mississippi and Mississippi Power.

- **Exports:** credited monthly at the utility's avoided energy cost plus an adder. The commission's consumer guide describes a 2.5¢ per kWh adder, with a further 2¢ for qualifying low-income customers.
- **Carryover:** credits carry forward without a limit.
- **Size limit:** 20 kW for residential systems.
- **Cooperatives:** follow separate rules.

The guide we read dates from the rule's adoption. The commission revised the rule in 2022, so current adders and incentives may differ.

**What it means:** exports earn the avoided cost plus a few cents, well under the retail rate. Solar you use yourself is worth more, so size to your own load.

Rules checked October 5, 2026. Solar tariffs change, so confirm the current terms with your utility before you sign a contract. Our [solar payback guide](/posts/solar-payback-period-without-federal-tax-credit/) shows how the retail rate and the export credit change the math.

## Limitations

## Compare With Nearby States

**Statewide average for context:** the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) puts Mississippi's average residential price at 14.54¢/kWh in July 2026, up 7.1% from 13.58¢ in July 2025. The U.S. average was 18.31¢. At 1,000 kWh that works out to $145.40 (1,000 × 14.54¢). This is total residential revenue divided by kWh sold across every utility in the state, fixed charges included, and it is a preliminary estimate. It is not any one utility's rate.
