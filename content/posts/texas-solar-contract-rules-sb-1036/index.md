If you sign a contract for rooftop solar in Texas, you have until the **fifth business day** after signing to cancel in writing, with no penalty. That right has applied to residential solar sale and lease agreements since September 1, 2025. On **September 1, 2026**, a second layer took effect: the companies and individuals who sell home solar in Texas must now be registered with the state, and the law's penalties for deceptive sales practices became enforceable.

This guide covers Texas Senate Bill 1036 (89th Legislature), which added Chapter 1806 to the Texas Occupations Code, the Residential Solar Retailer Regulatory Act. It applies to **Texas homes** and to solar systems "intended or designed primarily for family, personal, or household use." It does not apply to Texas commercial buildings or to homes outside Texas.

## What Changed, and When

| Date | What took effect | Source |
|---|---|---|
| September 1, 2025 | Required contract terms (§1806.155) and the right to cancel (§1806.156), for agreements entered into on or after this date | SB 1036, Section 2 and Section 5(a); TDLR |
| September 1, 2026 | Registration requirement for solar retailers and solar salespeople (§§1806.101, 1806.102) and the enforcement subchapter, including prohibited acts and penalties (Subchapter E) | SB 1036, Section 5(b); TDLR |
| November 1, 2026 | End date of TDLR's temporary pause on enforcing two administrative rules: §71.41 (contract disclosures) and §71.43 (educational materials) | TDLR Residential Solar Retailers page |

The difference matters. The cancellation right and the installer and permit terms come from the statute and have applied since 2025. The pause TDLR announced covers only its own rules on disclosure forms and educational materials. TDLR's page says the pause is "to allow time to adjust to new compliance requirements."

## Your Right to Cancel

Section 1806.156 says a solar retailer must let a buyer or lessee cancel "without penalty or further obligation" by giving written notice "on or before the fifth business day after the date on which the agreement was executed by the buyer or lessee."

Key details from the statute:

- **"Business day"** means a calendar day other than Saturday, Sunday or a legal holiday as defined in Section 662.021 of the Texas Government Code.
- **The contract must state the last day** of your cancellation period and the mailing address or email address for sending notice.
- **If the contract leaves out that address**, you may cancel with written notice "by any reasonable method."
- **Financing:** if the sale or lease involves a lender that is affiliated with or referred by the solar retailer, the agreement must require that lender to cancel any accompanying loan when you cancel the agreement.

**Worked example (illustrative, not a real contract).** You sign on Friday, October 23, 2026. Counting only weekdays with no holidays in the window, the five business days are Monday October 26, Tuesday October 27, Wednesday October 28, Thursday October 29 and Friday October 30. Your written notice must be sent on or before **Friday, October 30, 2026**. Your contract should state that date. If it states a different date, use the earlier one and ask the company to correct it.

The statute requires *written* notice. It does not say how to prove you sent it, so keep a copy and a record of when and how you sent it. That is our practical suggestion, not a legal requirement.

## What Your Contract Must Include

If the system is installed at your residence, Section 1806.155 requires the agreement to:

1. Provide that **a licensed electrical contractor** will perform the installation.
2. **Conspicuously state the name and license number** of that contractor. The company may instead list several electrical contractors from which one must be selected.
3. Provide that the retailer or contractor will obtain any **government permit** required, the **electric utility's approval to interconnect** (connect to the grid) where Utilities Code §39.554 or §39.916 applies, and the interconnection approval of your **electric cooperative or municipally owned utility** if you are served by one.

Section 1806.154 also requires the agreement to include the **name and registration number** of the retailer and of the salesperson involved. A retailer or salesperson must give you that information on request.

## Registration Since September 1, 2026

Under the law, a company may not employ or contract with an individual to do residential solar retail for it unless the company is registered as a solar retailer, and individuals who sell must be registered as solar salespeople. "Residential solar retail" covers selling or leasing, or offering to sell or lease, a residential solar system.

TDLR's application pages list these requirements:

| Item | Solar retailer | Solar salesperson |
|---|---|---|
| Registration fee (non-refundable) | $350 | $56 |
| Term | 1 year, renewable annually | 1 year, renewable annually |
| Insurance | General liability of at least $1 million per occurrence and $2 million aggregate | Not listed |
| Exam or experience requirement | Not listed on TDLR's retailer page | None |
| Criminal background check | Required for owners and controlling persons | Required; a conviction does not automatically disqualify |

**Who is exempt.** TDLR says a licensed electrical contractor that employs the people who sell solar for it is exempt from retailer registration and the insurance requirement, and its employees are exempt from salesperson registration. Contracts where the electrical contractor is the seller or lessor are still subject to the contract-content and cancellation rules. A third-party sales representative hired by an electrical contractor is not exempt and must register.

**Systems outside the law.** The statute excludes systems intended for temporary or emergency use or to power a single appliance, and certain small systems producing less than one kilowatt of peak power. TDLR also lists multifamily buildings with four or more dwelling units or stories and nonresidential property as exempt.

## What Salespeople and Companies Cannot Do

Section 1806.201 lists prohibited acts. Among them:

- Intentionally, knowingly or recklessly making a **false, misleading or deceptive** oral or written statement when selling residential solar.
- **Falsely stating or implying an affiliation** with a public utility or government agency.
- Selling at a home in violation of **posted no-soliciting signs**, unless an occupant directs otherwise.
- **Allowing installation by someone who is not an electrical contractor.**
- Failing to provide required disclosure statements or educational materials. (TDLR's pause on enforcing its §71.41 and §71.43 rules until November 1, 2026 is relevant here.)

A retailer must reasonably supervise its salespeople and is responsible for violations by them that it knew of or reasonably should have known of (§1806.152).

## Penalties and What Regulators Can Order

| Tool | What the statute says |
|---|---|
| Civil penalty | Up to $2,500 per violation or $50,000 total for violations of a similar nature (§1806.206) |
| Civil penalty, harm to someone over 65 | If a court finds an individual over 65 was harmed, up to $10,000 per violation or $100,000 total (§1806.206) |
| Cancellation and refund | After notice and a hearing, TDLR's commission or executive director may order an agreement cancelled and refund amounts paid, up to the amount paid (§1806.207). You may still sue separately for other damages |
| Registration | TDLR may deny or refuse to renew a registration for violations (§1806.202) |

These are maximums the law allows. We did not find published TDLR enforcement actions under the new registration regime as of October 16, 2026, so we cannot say how often or how severely they will be used.

## What the Law Does Not Do

- **It does not guarantee a company stays in business.** In the statute text we reviewed, we did not find a bond, guarantee fund or similar mechanism that repays buyers if a company closes. A refund order under §1806.207 is limited to amounts paid under the agreement.
- **It does not set prices or judge whether a deal is good.** Compare offers yourself; see our guide to [comparing solar quotes](/posts/how-to-compare-solar-quotes/).
- **It does not apply retroactively.** Chapter 1806 applies only to contracts entered into on or after September 1, 2025. Older contracts are governed by the law in effect when they were signed.
- **It does not replace other laws.** Retailers must also follow the federal Truth in Lending Act, the Texas Deceptive Trade Practices-Consumer Protection Act (Business and Commerce Code Chapter 17) and Business and Commerce Code Chapter 115, according to TDLR.

## A Pre-Signing Checklist

Based on what the law requires, before you sign:

1. **Check registration.** Ask for the retailer's and salesperson's TDLR registration numbers; the law requires them to be in the contract. TDLR offers a license search on its site.
2. **Check the electrician.** Find the electrical contractor's name and license number in the contract and confirm it with TDLR's license search.
3. **Find the cancellation date and address** in the contract. If either is missing, note the date you signed and cancel in writing by any reasonable method within five business days.
4. **Read the financing terms.** If a lender was referred by the seller, confirm the contract says the loan is cancelled if you cancel.
5. **Confirm who handles permits and utility approval.** The contract must say the retailer or contractor will obtain them. See our note on [interconnection and bill credits](/posts/solar-export-credit-rates-by-utility-2026/) before assuming how your utility will credit exported power.
6. **Run the numbers independently.** Texas electric rates vary widely by provider; see our [Texas rate guide](/posts/texas-electricity-rate-changes-2026/) and our explainer on [solar payback without the federal tax credit](/posts/solar-payback-period-without-federal-tax-credit/).

## If Something Goes Wrong

- **Complaints:** TDLR lists a "File a Complaint" option for Residential Solar Retailers on its site; start at the [TDLR complaints page](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/complaints/). The department's phone numbers, per its website, are (512) 463-6599 and (800) 803-9202.
- **Lenders and card issuers:** whether you can dispute a charge or challenge a loan depends on your agreement and the lender; this law's only financing rule is the loan-cancellation term described above. Ask the lender in writing and consider speaking with a lawyer.

## Limitations

This article explains the statute and TDLR guidance as published on October 16, 2026. We did not review TDLR's full administrative rules (Texas Administrative Code Title 16, Chapter 71) beyond the two rules TDLR names in its pause notice, and TDLR may change its guidance. We found no published TDLR enforcement actions under the registration regime. Individual contracts, financing agreements and federal or other state law may affect your rights. This is general information, not legal advice.
