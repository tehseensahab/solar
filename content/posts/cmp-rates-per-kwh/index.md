**A Central Maine Power (CMP) home on the default standard offer pays 24.68 cents for each kWh above 50 a month in 2026, and that rises to about 25.59 cents on January 1, 2027.** The change is in supply: the Maine Public Utilities Commission (PUC) set CMP's 2027 residential standard offer at 13.63 cents per kWh, up from 12.72 cents, according to news reports of its November 17, 2026 announcement. The PUC estimated the increase at about $4.99 a month for an average home using about 550 kWh.

Delivery is a separate charge, and no delivery change was announced for January 1 in the sources we checked. CMP's delivery rate fell on July 1, 2026, and a larger delivery rate request is still pending. For other Maine utilities and the state's net energy billing rules, see our [guide to Maine electric rates and solar](/rates/maine/).

This article covers CMP residential customers on Rate A, the standard residential delivery rate. It does not cover Versant Power, CMP's time-of-use or Electric Technology rates, or customers of consumer-owned utilities.

## What Is CMP's Rate per kWh in 2026?

A CMP bill has two parts: delivery, billed by CMP, and supply. CMP's bill insert says it "is prohibited by legislation from generating or supplying electricity," so the supply price comes either from the PUC's standard offer (the default) or from a competitive supplier you chose.

| Charge (residential Rate A, standard offer) | 2026 price | Source and date |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly service charge (includes first 50 kWh) | $27.37 | CMP Rate A tariff, effective July 1, 2026 |
| Delivery, each kWh above 50 | 11.959¢ ($0.119590) | CMP Rate A tariff, effective July 1, 2026 |
| Standard offer supply, every kWh | 12.721¢ ($0.127210) | Maine PUC, Jan. 1 to Dec. 31, 2026 |
| **Price per kWh above 50** | **24.68¢** | Our math: 11.959 + 12.721 |

CMP's tariff says the per-kWh delivery charge "includes transmission charges" and Efficiency Maine Trust assessment charges, so there is no separate transmission line to add. Late fees and any taxes are not included.

The all-in price, counting the service charge, depends on how much you use. The Maine Department of Energy Resources lists an average CMP residential bill of $157.13 at 550 kWh as of July 1, 2026, which works out to about 28.6 cents per kWh ($157.13 ÷ 550).

## How Much Will CMP Bills Go Up in January 2027?

The PUC approved the 2027 standard offer prices on November 17, 2026. The Portland Press Herald reported that "CMP residential and small businesses will go from a rate of 12.72 cents to 13.63 cents per kWh starting in January." WMTW reported the same figures. That is an increase of 0.91 cents per kWh, or about 7.2% (our math).

| Monthly use | 2026 bill (Rate A, standard offer) | Jan. 2027 bill, if delivery is unchanged | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 550 kWh | $157.13 | $162.13 | +$5.00 |
| 1,000 kWh | $268.19 | $277.28 | +$9.09 |

How we calculated it:

- **Delivery:** $27.37 + (kWh − 50) × $0.119590. At 550 kWh that is $87.17; at 1,000 kWh, $140.98.
- **Supply:** kWh × $0.127210 for 2026 and kWh × $0.1363 for 2027. At 550 kWh: $69.97 and $74.97. At 1,000 kWh: $127.21 and $136.30.
- Our 2026 total at 550 kWh matches the Department of Energy Resources' $157.13. Our 550 kWh increase of $5.00 is a cent above the PUC's $4.99 estimate, likely because the published 2027 price is rounded to two decimals.

These are estimates for a full month at one price. A bill that spans late December and early January will mix the two supply prices.

## Why Is CMP's Standard Offer Going Up?

The Press Herald reported that the PUC "accepted final 2027 standard offer bids" from suppliers. WGME reported that "The Maine Public Utilities Commission attributes the increase to high natural gas prices driven by regional pipeline limits and global conflicts." The Maine Wire quoted Patrick Scully of the PUC: "Electricity supply prices in Maine and New England are largely driven by the price of natural gas."

For scale, the 2026 standard offer of 12.721 cents was itself up from 10.6128 cents in 2025, according to the PUC's CMP rate table. The 2027 increase is smaller in cents than the 2026 one.

The 2027 supplier names and the PUC docket number were not in the reports we read, and the PUC's CMP standard offer table had not yet added a 2027 row when we checked on November 22, 2026.

## Did CMP Delivery Rates Change in 2026?

Yes, they went down. CMP said that "As of July 1, 2026, charges on the delivery portion of bills are decreasing," by about $11 a month for an average residential customer. CMP gave two reasons: its 2025 annual compliance filing "completes the recovery of storm repair costs from prior years," worth about $8 a month, and lower Efficiency Maine Trust costs, about $3.

That July cut roughly offset the January 2026 increase. CMP said that in January 2026 "these costs increased by approximately $11 per month" for an average standard offer customer, consistent with the supply price rising from 10.6128 to 12.721 cents.

## Is a CMP Delivery Rate Increase Coming?

A delivery increase has been requested, not approved. WMTW reported that "In a separate case, CMP is seeking a $189 million increase in distribution rates," and that the PUC estimates it could add $11.06 a month to the average residential bill if approved. Maine Public reported on October 6, 2026 that regulators rejected CMP's request for a temporary rate increase. We did not find a decision date.

If the full request were approved as filed, the PUC's estimate would be more than double the January supply increase. Treat that as an upper-end scenario; the PUC has not ruled on the request.

## What the CMP Rate Change Means for Solar Owners

CMP's net energy billing FAQ says residential kWh credit agreements "offset kWh charges associated with the customer's bill," and that "Only non-residential customer accounts qualify to receive the tariff rate bill credit." In practice, for a home on Rate A and the standard offer, each credited kWh replaces a kWh you would otherwise pay 24.68 cents for in 2026, and about 25.59 cents from January 2027 (our math).

Three limits apply, according to the same FAQ:

- Credits bank on "a rolling 12 month period"; in our reading, credits not used within that period are lost.
- "CMP does not pay for the excess energy produced from a customer's generator."
- You still pay non-kWh charges for your rate class, such as the $27.37 monthly service charge.

**What it means:** the January increase makes a rooftop kWh used to offset your own use worth about 0.91 cents more. It does not change the advice to size a system to your yearly use, because surplus credits that expire earn nothing. Our [solar payback guide](/posts/solar-payback-period-without-federal-tax-credit/) shows how the per-kWh price feeds the payback math.

## Should You Switch From the Standard Offer?

Only if an offer is clearly better after you read the terms. The 13.63-cent standard offer takes effect January 1, 2027. Compare any supplier's price in cents per kWh against it, and check the contract length, any early termination fee and what the price becomes when the term ends. Our guide to [comparing an electricity supplier's offer with your utility's default price](/posts/how-to-compare-electricity-supplier-offers/) walks through the checks.

## What to Check on Your CMP Bill

1. **Supply line:** it shows "Standard Offer" and the price per kWh, or the name of your competitive supplier.
2. **Delivery lines:** "Up to 50 KWH @$27.37" and the per-kWh delivery rate for the rest.
3. **Billing period:** a period that crosses January 1, 2027 may be split between the two supply prices.

Our guide to [reading your electric bill](/posts/how-to-read-your-electric-bill/) explains each line.

## Limitations

- The 2027 standard offer price and the $4.99 estimate come from news reports citing the PUC (Portland Press Herald, WMTW, Bangor Daily News, WGME and The Maine Wire), not from the PUC's own release, which we could not retrieve. The PUC's CMP rate table did not yet list 2027 on November 22, 2026.
- The 2027 bills assume CMP's July 1, 2026 delivery rates stay in place. A decision in CMP's delivery rate case could change them.
- Bills exclude any taxes and late fees and are for Rate A only.
