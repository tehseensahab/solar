Minnesota's largest utility did not change rates on October 1, 2026. Xcel Energy's two-year electric rate case was decided earlier in 2026, and the main remaining event for customers is a refund of interim rates.

This article covers Xcel Energy (Northern States Power). It does not cover Minnesota Power, Otter Tail Power, cooperatives or municipal utilities.

## Quick Summary

| Item | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Xcel's request | $353 million (9.6%) | $137.5 million (3.6%) |
| PUC approval | $82.9 million (2.3%) | $127.3 million (3.4%) |

Total approved: about $211 million over two years.

## What Was Decided

The Minnesota Public Utilities Commission (PUC) ruled in Docket 24-320. Its news release says:

- **Approved:** $82.9 million (2.3%) for 2025 and $127.3 million (3.4%) for 2026, nearly 60% below what Xcel asked for.
- **Late fees:** cut from 1.5% to 0.45%, and waived for low-income customers.
- **Bill help:** the low-income usage credit now covers 500 kWh a month, up from 300 kWh, and a new program helps customers pay down past-due balances.
- **Executive pay:** the amount recoverable from customers is capped.

Xcel's rate case page says the commission gave final approval on rates on July 31, 2026.

The Citizens Utility Board of Minnesota (CUB) describes the original filing as $573 million over two years with a requested 10.3% return on equity, up from 9.25%.

## The Refund

Interim rates began on January 1, 2025: a 5.2% increase, about $192 million, shown on bills as "Interim Rate Adj." Final rates are lower. The PUC said the refund "will be calculated in the coming months," and Xcel says it "will apply a refund in the form of a one-time credit with interest." We did not find the refund date or amount.

A second, unrelated credit is also coming. On May 7, 2026 the PUC ordered Xcel to refund $40.6 million plus interest for replacement power bought during a 2023 outage at the Prairie Island nuclear plant, according to the Attorney General's office.

## Three Different Bill Figures

| Source | Residential impact | Basis given |
|---|---|---|
| Xcel Energy rate case page | 2.85%, or $2.78 a month | Average residential customer, "in 2025 and 2026" |
| KAAL TV report | About $5.59 a month | About 3% a year |
| CUB Minnesota | 5.8%, or $10.27 a month ($123.37 a year) | Typical residential customer, final decision |

These are not the same measure. One plausible reading is that the smaller figure is per year and the larger is the two years combined compared with rates before the case, but none of the sources says so, and we could not confirm it. Use your own bill: compare the energy and basic service charges on a bill from late 2024 with one from after final rates took effect.

## What This Means for Homeowners With Solar

Minnesota law sets net metering terms by system size.

- **Under 40 kW:** credited at the utility's average retail energy rate, according to Solar United Neighbors. Nearly all home systems fall here.
- **Monthly surplus:** becomes credits applied in later months.
- **Year end:** at investor-owned utilities such as Xcel, leftover credits are paid at the avoided-cost rate. At municipal utilities and cooperatives they can expire.
- **Fees:** municipal utilities and cooperatives may charge net metered customers extra fees to recover fixed costs.
- **No statewide cap,** though a utility can ask regulators to review once net metering reaches 4% of its annual sales.

**What it means:** credits near the retail rate mean each solar kWh offsets most of what you would have paid, including the 2025 and 2026 increases. If you are served by a cooperative or city utility, ask about fees first.

Rules checked October 5, 2026. Solar tariffs change, so confirm the current terms with your utility before you sign a contract. Our [solar payback guide](/posts/solar-payback-period-without-federal-tax-credit/) shows how the retail rate and the export credit change the math.

## What to Check on Your Bill

1. The "Interim Rate Adj." line, and when it is replaced by final rates.
2. A one-time refund credit.
3. The basic service charge and fuel clause.

For help sorting the lines on your bill, see our guide to [reading your electric bill](/posts/how-to-read-your-electric-bill/).

## Limitations

We did not open the PUC order itself. Approved amounts come from the PUC's news release, and bill figures from Xcel, a TV report and a consumer group. The three bill-impact figures are not reconciled, and the interim refund date and amount are not confirmed.

## Compare With Nearby States

**Statewide average for context:** the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) puts Minnesota's average residential price at 17.45¢/kWh in July 2026, up 3.1% from 16.92¢ in July 2025. The U.S. average was 18.31¢. At 1,000 kWh that works out to $174.50 (1,000 × 17.45¢). This is total residential revenue divided by kWh sold across every utility in the state, fixed charges included, and it is a preliminary estimate. It is not any one utility's rate.
