Minnesota's largest utility did not change rates on October 1, 2026. Xcel Energy's two-year electric rate case was decided earlier in 2026, and the main remaining event for customers is a refund of interim rates.

This article covers Xcel Energy (Northern States Power). It does not cover Minnesota Power, Otter Tail Power, cooperatives or municipal utilities.

## Quick Summary

| Item | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Xcel's request | $353 million (9.6%) | $137.5 million (3.6%) |
| PUC approval | $82.9 million (2.3%) | $127.3 million (3.4%) |

Total approved: about $211 million over two years.

## What Was Decided

- **Request:** the Citizens Utility Board of Minnesota (CUB) describes the filing as $573 million over two years, with a requested return on equity of 10.3%, up from 9.25%.
- **Interim rates:** an interim increase of 5.2%, about $192 million, was approved in late 2024 and shown on bills as "Interim Rate Adj." CUB put that at about $5 a month for a typical home.
- **Final decision:** CUB lists a final decision date of June 18, 2026 and an approved increase of 5.8%.

## The Refund

Interim rates are collected while a case is pending and trued up afterward. Xcel said that because final rates are below the interim rates set for 2025, it expects "to refund customers in the form of a one-time credit with interest." The timing depends on a plan Xcel files after the commission's written order. We did not find the refund date or amount.

## A Caution on the Bill Impact

Two figures are in circulation:

- **About $5.59 a month,** about 3% a year, from a TV report citing Xcel.
- **$10.27 a month,** or $123.37 a year, from CUB's summary of the final decision.

They may measure different things, such as each year's step versus both years combined. We could not confirm which, so we report both.

## What This Means for Homeowners With Solar

We did not verify Minnesota's current net metering or export credit rules for this article, so we give no export rates here. Ask your utility for its current solar tariff, and check how exported energy is credited before sizing a system. Fixed monthly charges are not reduced by solar.

Our [solar payback guide](/posts/solar-payback-period-without-federal-tax-credit/) shows how the retail rate and the export credit change the math.

## What to Check on Your Bill

1. The "Interim Rate Adj." line, and when it is replaced by final rates.
2. A one-time refund credit.
3. The basic service charge and fuel clause.

For help sorting the lines on your bill, see our guide to [reading your electric bill](/posts/how-to-read-your-electric-bill/).

## Limitations

We did not open the PUC order. Figures come from a TV report and a consumer group. The two bill-impact figures are not reconciled, and the refund timing is not confirmed.
