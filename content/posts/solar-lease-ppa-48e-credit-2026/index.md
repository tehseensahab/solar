A homeowner who buys rooftop solar and completes installation after December 31, 2025 gets no federal tax credit for it. A company that owns the system and rents out its output, through a lease or a power purchase agreement (PPA), can still be eligible for a federal credit. This article sets out what the law says, what it does not say, and what a homeowner can reasonably ask a provider.

Two terms first. In a **lease**, the homeowner pays a fixed monthly amount to use panels owned by a company. In a **PPA**, the homeowner pays per kilowatt-hour (kWh) of electricity the panels produce. In both cases the company, not the homeowner, owns the equipment. Contract terms vary by provider, so treat these as general descriptions.

## The short answer

| | Homeowner buys (cash or loan) | Lease or PPA |
|---|---|---|
| Who owns the panels | Homeowner | Company |
| Federal credit for installations completed after Dec. 31, 2025 | None. Section 25D is closed | The owner may claim the Section 48E business credit if it meets that section's conditions |
| Who claims any credit | Not applicable | The owner, not the homeowner |
| Does the homeowner pay less because of it? | Not applicable | Only if the contract is priced that way |

## What ended: the homeowner credit (Section 25D)

The IRS says the Residential Clean Energy Credit under Section 25D "will not be allowed for any expenditures made after December 31, 2025." The IRS also says an expenditure for installed property is treated as made when the original installation is completed. So a payment made in 2025 for a system finished in 2026 does not qualify. Our [payback guide](/posts/solar-payback-period-without-federal-tax-credit/) shows how to calculate payback without it. The ending covers every item Section 25D listed, including solar electric panels and battery storage.

## What continues: the owner's credit (Section 48E)

Section 48E is the clean electricity investment credit, a business credit claimed by the taxpayer that makes the qualified investment. Three features matter for leased and PPA rooftop systems.

**1. The leasing ban does not name solar panels.** Public Law 119-21, signed July 4, 2025, added Section 48E(i). It denies the credit for property described in Section 25D(d)(1) or (4) if the taxpayer rents or leases the property to a third party. Under Section 25D(d), paragraph (1) is solar water heating and paragraph (4) is small wind turbines. Solar electric property, which is rooftop photovoltaic (PV) panels, is paragraph (2), and battery storage is paragraph (6). Neither is in the ban. That is our plain reading of the statute, not tax advice.

**2. Size and rate.** Section 48E's base credit rate is 6%, and 30% applies to a facility with maximum net output under 1 megawatt (MW) measured in alternating current, or one that meets prevailing wage and apprenticeship requirements. One MW is 1,000 kilowatts (kW). Home systems are measured in single-digit to low double-digit kW, well under that threshold. The rate that applies to a specific provider's systems is a tax matter for that provider.

**3. A 2027 cutoff for newer projects.** Section 48E(e)(4), added by the same law, says the credit does not apply to wind or solar property placed in service after December 31, 2027. It applies to facilities whose construction begins after July 4, 2026, the date 12 months after enactment (Public Law 119-21, § 70513; IRS Notice 2025-42, Section 1). Energy storage at those facilities is excepted from the cutoff.

| Facility construction began | Rule |
|---|---|
| On or before July 4, 2026 | The December 31, 2027 placed-in-service cutoff does not apply |
| After July 4, 2026 | No credit for property placed in service after December 31, 2027 |

IRS Notice 2025-42 (effective for facilities that had not begun construction before September 2, 2025) sets how construction start is shown. Solar facilities with maximum net output of 1.5 MW or less, measured in alternating current, may use either a physical work test or a five percent safe harbor to show construction began before July 5, 2026. Other facilities are limited to the physical work test. We did not find a source saying how these tests apply to a specific provider's pool of home systems, so that question is one to ask.

**Foreign-entity restrictions.** The law also bars the 48E credit for a taxpayer that is a specified foreign entity or a foreign-influenced entity, and excludes facilities whose construction begins after December 31, 2025 and includes material assistance from a prohibited foreign entity. We did not review Treasury's implementing guidance, so we cannot say how these rules affect any particular provider.

## What this does and does not mean for a homeowner

- **The tax law does not set your price.** The credit belongs to the owner. A lower lease or PPA price is possible, but it is a pricing decision in the contract.
- **A 2027 deadline may shape what is offered.** For systems on facilities that began construction after July 4, 2026, the credit depends on being placed in service by December 31, 2027. Ask whether the provider's price or terms depend on meeting that date, and what happens to your price if the schedule slips.
- **Ownership affects other parts of your deal.** Export credits from your utility, renewable energy credits, equipment warranties and what happens if you sell the house depend on the contract and on state and utility rules. See our [net metering guide](/posts/net-metering-state-guide/) and [export credit comparison](/posts/solar-export-credit-rates-by-utility-2026/).

## Questions to ask a lease or PPA provider

1. What is the monthly payment or per-kWh price for every year of the term, including any annual escalator, in dollars and cents?
2. Does the price depend on a tax credit? If so, what happens to my price if the company cannot claim it?
3. What happens if the system is placed in service later than the date in the contract?
4. What are the terms if I sell the home: transfer to the buyer, buyout price, or removal?
5. Who receives my utility's export credits, and how are they reflected in my payments?
6. How would the same system compare with paying cash or taking a loan? Our guide to [comparing solar quotes](/posts/how-to-compare-solar-quotes/) lists the numbers to check.
