## String, optimizer or microinverter?

- **String inverter:** one central unit converts DC from a series of panels. Simple and compact; if one panel is shaded, the string can be affected unless optimizers are used.
- **String inverter plus power optimizers (SolarEdge, Tigo):** each panel gets a module-level device. Tigo's TS4-A-O provides optimization, module-level monitoring and rapid shutdown (NEC 2014, 2017 and 2020 per Tigo) and lists a 25-year warranty; it requires a Cloud Connect Advanced and Tigo Access Point and works with a list of compatible inverters.
- **Microinverters (Enphase, Qcells):** each panel has its own inverter, which suits roofs with multiple orientations or shading and provides per-panel monitoring.
- **Hybrid inverters (Tesla, EcoFlow):** one unit handles solar and a battery.

None of these is automatically more efficient in your home. Real output depends on shading, orientation, sizing and wiring.

## How much does efficiency matter?

CEC efficiency is a weighted average of conversion efficiency. Our math (illustration, not a forecast): a 7.6 kW system producing 10,000 kWh a year at 97% conversion would produce about 10,000 × (0.99 ÷ 0.97) = 10,206 kWh at 99% conversion, a gap of about 206 kWh a year, or about $37 a year at 18¢ per kWh (206 × $0.18). The assumptions are ours: same panels, same sun, 18¢ is near EIA's [2026 national forecast](/posts/eia-residential-price-forecast-sept-2026/) but your rate differs. That gap is small next to shading, orientation and a 25-year warranty difference, which is why we weight warranty and roof fit more heavily here.

## Who should skip fancy electronics

- Simple, unshaded, single-orientation roofs often do well with a basic string inverter; compare quotes with and without module-level electronics.
- If you want no battery now, a hybrid inverter may be unnecessary, though some owners add storage later.
- If your installer cannot support a brand long-term, a 25-year warranty depends on the company standing behind it. See [solar installer out of business](/posts/solar-installer-out-of-business/).

## Buying checklist

1. Roof orientations, shading and panel count.
2. Battery now, later or never.
3. Model numbers, CEC efficiency and warranty terms in writing.
4. Rapid-shutdown and monitoring approach.
5. Local utility's interconnection rules and approved equipment.
6. Installer's service record; see [how to compare solar quotes](/posts/how-to-compare-solar-quotes/).
7. What the warranty covers (parts, labor, shipping).
