**PECO's supply price for customers on default service rises 10.3% on Dec. 1, 2026, from 11.759 to 12.965 cents per kWh. For a home using 700 kWh a month, that adds about $8.44 a month to the supply part of the bill; at 1,000 kWh it is about $12.06.** The figures come from the Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission's (PUC) Dec. 1 price table. Customers who buy electricity from a competitive supplier are not affected; they pay their contract price.

It is PECO's second supply increase of 2026, after a June 1 rise. For how PECO compares with PPL, Penelec and the state's other utilities, see our [Pennsylvania electric rates and solar overview](/rates/pennsylvania/).

## What Is PECO's New Price to Compare?

The PUC published its Dec. 1 price table in a release dated Nov. 23, 2026. It lists PECO's residential Price to Compare at 11.759 cents per kWh now and 12.965 cents from Dec. 1, a change of +10.3%.

| Period | PECO residential Price to Compare | Source |
|---|---|---|
| From Dec. 1, 2025 | 11.024 ¢/kWh | PUC, Nov. 24, 2025 |
| From June 1, 2026 | 11.759 ¢/kWh (+6.67%) | PUC, June 1, 2026 |
| From Dec. 1, 2026 | 12.965 ¢/kWh (+10.3%) | PUC, Nov. 23, 2026 |
| Change vs. last winter | +1.941 ¢/kWh (+17.6%) | Our arithmetic |

PECO adjusts the generation part of its residential supply price twice a year. Its April 2026 filing describes "semi-annual adjustments to the GSA for Procurement Class 1 – Residential." That generation supply adjustment (GSA) was 10.785 cents of the 11.759-cent June 1 Price to Compare. If PECO keeps that schedule, the next change would be expected June 1, 2027; neither PECO nor the PUC has announced it.

You may have seen 12.944 cents elsewhere. Third-party rate sites reported that figure in November, one of them as pending. The PUC's Nov. 23 table shows 12.965 cents.

## What the Price to Compare Covers

The Price to Compare is the per-kWh price for the electricity itself. PECO's 2025 price-to-compare table splits it into a "Standard Generation Charge" and a "Transmission Charge," and notes that it "includes an adjustment for Gross Receipts Tax of 5.90%." It does not include distribution (delivery) charges; PECO's table notes it excludes "surcharges and sales taxes applicable to PECO distribution service." So this change moves only part of your bill.

## How Much More Will PECO Customers Pay?

Assumptions: PECO residential default service (Rate R); supply price up 1.206 cents per kWh on Dec. 1, 2026; usage the same each month through May 31, 2027. Delivery charges and sales taxes are not included. PECO's April 2026 generation supply filing describes "a typical Rate R customer using 700 kWh per month." We assume the Dec. 1 price stays in effect through May 31, 2027; neither the PUC nor PECO has confirmed that period.

| Monthly use | Extra supply cost per month | Over Dec. 2026 to May 2027 (6 months) |
|---|---|---|
| 500 kWh | $6.03 | $36.18 |
| 700 kWh | $8.44 | $50.65 |
| 1,000 kWh | $12.06 | $72.36 |

Against last winter, the difference is larger. At 700 kWh a month, a default-service customer pays about $13.59 a month more for supply than at the Dec. 1, 2025 price (12.965 vs. 11.024 cents per kWh). Homes with electric heat use more in winter, so their dollar change is bigger. As the PUC notes, "During the winter heating months, the cost of electricity can account for more than half of a typical customer bill."

## Who Is Affected?

Only customers on default service. In the PUC's words, "These changes only affect customers receiving 'default service' from their electric utility — that is, those who have not selected an EGS" (an electric generation supplier). Supplier customers keep paying their contract price. If your contract is ending, compare its renewal price with 12.965 cents before it rolls over.

Small businesses on PECO default service also see an increase: the PUC puts PECO's small business Price to Compare increase at 9.1%.

## Should You Shop for a Supplier?

The PUC's shopping site is PAPowerSwitch.com. This winter the PUC cautions that "current wholesale market trends have resulted in fewer supplier offers below the utility rate than in prior years." Before you sign:

1. Compare the price per kWh with 12.965 cents.
2. Check whether it is fixed or variable, and for how long.
3. Look for monthly fees and early cancellation fees.
4. Find out what the price becomes when the term ends.

Our [guide to comparing supplier offers](/posts/how-to-compare-electricity-supplier-offers/) explains each check. If the concern is a high winter bill rather than the rate, the PUC urges households to "call utilities now" to learn about assistance programs and other affordability options; [budget billing](/posts/budget-billing-explained/) spreads costs across the year.

## What This Means for Homeowners With Solar

For a PECO default-service customer, each kWh your panels make and you use at home avoids buying supply at 12.965 cents, plus PECO's per-kWh delivery charges. A higher Price to Compare makes that self-use worth more. Winter output is lower, so many solar homes draw more from the grid from December to February. How exported power is credited depends on PECO's net metering rules and, if you shop, your supplier's terms; check both before counting on a credit value.

## Limitations

Bill estimates cover the supply portion only and exclude delivery charges, sales tax and usage changes. We did not find PECO's own Dec. 1, 2026 generation supply adjustment filing; the Dec. 1 price is from the PUC's table.
