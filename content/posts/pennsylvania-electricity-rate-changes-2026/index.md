Pennsylvania had no October 1, 2026 rate change that we could find as of September 29, 2026. The big 2026 change came on June 1, when utilities reset the default supply price for customers who have not picked an electricity supplier.

## Quick Summary

These are residential default-service supply prices (the Price to Compare), not your total bill. Utilities marked "estimated" are labeled that way by the Pennsylvania PUC.

## What Changed on June 1, 2026?

The PUC said generation prices for all PUC-regulated electric utilities adjust on June 1. These prices are based on wholesale market rates. The PUC said generation can be about half or more of a total bill. It also noted that summer heat and air-conditioning use can affect bills more than the price change itself.

## How Much Could Your Electric Bill Change?

Our estimate multiplies the change in cents per kWh by monthly usage and covers the supply portion only. At 1,000 kWh a month, PECO's change is 0.735 cents times 1,000, or about $7.35 a month. Delivery charges, taxes and fees are not included, and your actual change depends on usage.

## Which Customers Are Affected?

The PUC said these changes only affect customers on default service. Customers who chose a competitive supplier keep their contract price until it ends. The PUC also said fewer supplier offers were below the utility's Price to Compare than in past years, and that offers below it may be short-term contracts, typically three to six months.

## Pennsylvania Net Metering and Solar Export Credits

Pennsylvania's net metering rules are in the state code, [52 Pa. Code §75.13](https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/pennsylvania/52-Pa-Code-SS-75-13).

- **Credits:** utilities credit residential solar at the full retail kWh rate, including generation, transmission and distribution, for production up to your usage in a billing period.
- **Surplus:** excess kWh carry forward; unused excess at year-end is paid at the Price to Compare.
- **Size limit:** 50 kW nameplate for residential systems.

On default service, a higher Price to Compare raises the value of each kWh your panels offset. If you buy from a competitive supplier, your contract price sets the generation part of that credit, so ask how the supplier handles net metering before switching.

## What Happens Next?

Default service prices reset again on December 1, 2026. Compare current offers at PAPowerSwitch.com, the PUC's shopping site.

PPL has posted a residential Price to Compare of 14.428 cents per kWh for Dec. 1, 2026 to May 31, 2027, up 10.3% from 13.079 cents, according to its [rates page](https://pplelectric.com/shopping). The PUC approved PPL's base rate settlement in June, with new distribution rates from July 1, 2026. Details and bill math are in our [PPL Dec. 1 price increase article](/posts/ppl-rate-increase-december-2026/).

The PUC's [Nov. 23 release](https://www.puc.pa.gov/press-release/2026/puc-encourages-consumers-to-prepare-for-rising-winter-electric-costs-as-december-1-brings-price-changes-11132026) lists Dec. 1 residential Prices to Compare. PECO rises from 11.759 to 12.965 cents per kWh (+10.3%). Penelec rises from 13.142 to 13.564 cents (+3.2%) and Met-Ed from 13.951 to 14.442 cents (+3.5%). Bill math: [PECO's December increase](/posts/peco-rate-increase-december-2026/) and [Penelec and the other FirstEnergy districts](/posts/penelec-rate-increase-december-2026/).

## Pennsylvania Statewide Average

The U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) puts Pennsylvania's average residential price at 21.77¢/kWh in August 2026, up 9.2% from 19.93¢ in August 2025. The U.S. average was 18.16¢. At 1,000 kWh that works out to $217.70 (1,000 × 21.77¢). This is EIA's statewide figure: all residential revenue divided by all residential kWh, across every utility, fixed charges included. It is preliminary, and it is not any one utility's rate.
