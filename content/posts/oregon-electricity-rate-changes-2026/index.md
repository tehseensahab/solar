Oregon's large utilities did not change rates on October 1, 2026. The year's increases took effect on April 1, and state law now blocks further increases over the winter.

This article covers Portland General Electric (PGE) and Pacific Power, which together serve about 1.5 million Oregon customers. It does not cover Idaho Power's Oregon territory, consumer-owned utilities or cooperatives.

## Quick Summary

| Utility | Effective | Residential change | Per month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Portland General Electric | April 1, 2026 | +5% | About $8 |
| Pacific Power | April 1, 2026 | +3% (4% in Albany) | More than $4 |
| Either | Nov. 1, 2026 to March 31, 2027 | No increases allowed | n/a |

## What Changed on April 1

The Oregon Public Utility Commission (PUC) approved:

- **PGE:** a 5% residential increase, about $8 a month for an average customer.
- **Pacific Power:** about 3% for most residential customers. Albany customers saw 4% because of a city street project.

A PUC spokesperson told the Oregon Capital Chronicle the utilities cited fuel costs, grid investment, wildfire mitigation, and clean energy and efficiency programs.

## The Longer Trend

- It was the **sixth year in a row** the commission approved residential increases.
- Rates are **more than 50% higher** than five years ago, more than twice the rate of inflation since 2020.
- Residential demand grew about 10% over the last decade. Industrial and data center demand grew about 70%.

## Why the Calendar Matters

The FAIR Energy Act prevents rate increases between November 1 and March 31. That protects winter heating bills, and it means several filings can stack up on April 1. Expect the next round of changes around April 1, 2027.

Oregon also has a new law requiring large technology companies to pay for the infrastructure their data centers need. The PUC was due to rule on PGE's compliance plan in spring 2026. We did not confirm the outcome.

## What This Means for Homeowners With Solar

We did not verify Oregon's current net metering or export credit rules for this article, so we give no export rates here. Ask your utility for its current solar tariff, and check how exported energy is credited before sizing a system. Fixed monthly charges are not reduced by solar.

## What to Check on Your Bill

1. The basic charge.
2. The per-kWh tiers, and whether you are on a time-of-use plan.
3. Adjustment schedules listed below the energy charges.

## Limitations

Figures come from the Oregon Capital Chronicle's report of the PUC decision. We did not open PUC orders, and we did not research pending 2027 requests, Idaho Power or consumer-owned utilities.
