**Duke Energy Florida net metering trades kWh for kWh: when your solar panels send more power to the grid than you use, the extra is credited to your energy use on the next month's bill, for up to 12 months.** Credits still unused at the end of the calendar year are paid out at a rate based on the utility's avoided energy cost, not the retail price. You still pay the monthly customer charge.

The rules come from the Florida Public Service Commission's (PSC) net metering rule, which applies to Duke Energy Florida, Florida Power & Light and the state's other investor-owned utilities. What differs is Duke's rates. This guide covers residential Duke Energy Florida customers. For the same rules on FPL bills, see our [FPL net metering guide](/posts/fpl-net-metering/), and for all Florida utilities, our [roundup of Florida electric rates and rooftop solar](/rates/florida/).

## How Does Duke Energy Florida Net Metering Work?

Florida PSC Rule 25-6.065 (last amended April 7, 2008) sets the mechanics:

1. **Monthly netting.** "Meter readings shall be taken monthly on the same cycle as required under the otherwise applicable rate schedule." If you use more than your system sends out, you pay for the difference at your normal rate.
2. **Excess becomes a kWh credit.** If your system sends out more than you use, the excess "shall be credited to the customer's energy consumption for the next month's billing cycle." The credit is in kWh, not dollars.
3. **Up to 12 months.** Credits can accumulate and be used "for a period of not more than twelve months."
4. **Year-end payout.** "At the end of each calendar year, the investor-owned utility shall pay the customer for any unused energy credits," at an average annual rate based on the utility's COG-1 as-available energy tariff.
5. **Leaving the system.** If you leave the utility's system, unused credits for excess kWh are paid at the same COG-1-based rate.
6. **Fixed charges stay.** "The customer shall continue to pay the applicable customer charge and applicable demand charge."

## What Is a Net Metering Credit Worth on a Duke Energy Florida Bill?

Because credits are counted in kWh, a credited kWh replaces a kWh you would otherwise buy. It is worth roughly the per-kWh part of your bill: base energy charges, fuel and the other per-kWh clauses.

The Florida PSC's bill comparison for October to December 2026 lists these Duke Energy Florida residential charges at 1,000 kWh. Its base rate line is Duke's yearly average, weighting three winter months and nine other months, so it is not a literal bill for any one month:

| Line item (1,000 kWh, Oct. to Dec. 2026) | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base rate charges | $94.43 |
| Fuel and purchased power | $41.27 |
| Storm protection plan | $9.36 |
| Energy conservation | $3.86 |
| Asset securitization | $2.38 |
| Capacity | $1.33 |
| Environmental | $0.40 |
| Gross receipts tax and regulatory assessment fee | $4.06 |
| **Total** | **$157.09** |

That is an average of 15.7 cents per kWh ($157.09 ÷ 1,000). The base rate figure includes the fixed customer charge, which credits do not reduce, so a credited kWh is worth somewhat less than 15.7 cents. Duke's March 2026 tariff filing listed a $14.35 customer charge. Duke's base charges have changed at least once since that filing, so the current customer charge may differ slightly. At $14.35, the rest works out to about 14.3 cents per kWh (($157.09 − $14.35) ÷ 1,000).

**Example.** Suppose that in a sunny month your home draws 900 kWh from the grid and your panels send 400 kWh back. You are billed for 500 kWh of energy, not 900. At about 14.3 cents per kWh, the 400 kWh offset is worth roughly $57 that month (400 × $0.143). This is an illustration at our assumed rate, not a quote for your home.

Two features of Duke's rate make the value vary through the year:

- **Winter is higher.** The PSC's note puts Duke's base charges for a 1,000 kWh bill at $91.74 for June to November and $102.48 for December 2026 to February 2027. Credits used in winter offset more expensive kWh.
- **Use above 1,000 kWh costs more.** Duke's residential base energy charge is higher for each kWh above 1,000 in a month, according to its 2026 tariff filing. In a high-use month, credits that bring your net use back toward 1,000 kWh offset those higher-priced kWh.

Power your panels make and your home uses right away never reaches the meter as an export. It simply lowers what you buy, at the same full price.

## What Happens to Unused Credits at the End of the Year?

They are paid out at an average annual rate based on Duke Energy Florida's COG-1 as-available energy tariff. That rate is based on the utility's avoided energy cost, not the retail price, so a leftover kWh is likely worth less at year-end than it would have been as an offset during the year. We did not find Duke Energy Florida's current COG-1 rate in a PSC filing, so we do not quote a figure.

The PSC's data show the payouts are small. Duke Energy Florida reported paying customers $1,118,373 for 2025 across 97,364 net-metered systems, or about $11.49 per system ($1,118,373 ÷ 97,364).

So when you compare quotes, the useful number is how much of your own yearly use a system would cover. Production beyond that ends the year as credits paid at the lower avoided-cost rate.

## What Size Solar System Qualifies?

Florida's rule sets three tiers by gross power rating:

| Tier | Size | Extra rules |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | 10 kW or less | No extra fees; no liability insurance required |
| Tier 2 | Above 10 kW up to 100 kW | Application fee; utility may require liability insurance of no more than $1 million |
| Tier 3 | Above 100 kW up to 2 MW | Application fee; utility may require liability insurance of no more than $2 million |

For Tier 1, the rule says customers "shall not be charged fees" beyond those charged to other customers without their own generation, and the utility may not require liability insurance. The rule also caps a system's gross power rating at 90% of your service rating.

For scale, Duke Energy Florida's 97,364 net-metered systems in 2025 totaled 950,538 kW, about 9.8 kW each on average (950,538 ÷ 97,364). That average includes businesses as well as homes.

## Duke Energy Florida Net Metering by the Numbers (2025)

From Duke Energy Florida's entry in the Florida PSC's 2025 net metering report (year ending December 31, 2025, filed April 1, 2026), all customer classes combined:

- **Net-metered systems:** 97,364, of which 97,362 solar
- **Capacity:** 950,538 kW
- **Energy sent to the grid by customers:** 675,987,636 kWh, about 6,943 kWh per system
- **Energy delivered by Duke to those customers:** 1,948,525,256 kWh
- **Payments to customers:** $1,118,373.00

## Will Lower 2027 Rates Change the Value of Solar?

A little. Duke Energy Florida said on November 3, 2026 that residential customers "will see a $33 decrease in their January 2027 bills when compared to January 2026," following the PSC's approval of its request to lower 2027 rates. WFTV reported a further $10 monthly reduction from March through November 2027. Lower per-kWh charges make each credited kWh worth slightly less. The net metering rules themselves do not change.
