## How fast is Level 2?

Power (kW) = volts × amps ÷ 1,000.

- 240 V × 48 A = 11,520 W = **11.5 kW**
- 240 V × 40 A = 9,600 W = **9.6 kW**

Charging time (our illustration): adding 60 kWh at 9.6 kW takes 60 ÷ 9.6 = **6.25 hours**, if your car accepts that rate. Many cars accept less on AC, so the car, not the charger, may set the limit.

ChargePoint says its plug-in 40 A Home Flex models add about 30 miles of range per hour and the hardwired 50 A model up to 37.

**Breaker sizing.** The [DOE's Alternative Fuels Data Center](https://afdc.energy.gov/fuels/electricity-charging-home) notes the National Electrical Code treats EV charging as a continuous load. In practice that commonly means the breaker is rated for 125% of the charger's amps: 48 A needs 60 A; 40 A needs 50 A. Our math: 48 × 1.25 = 60; 40 × 1.25 = 50. Have a licensed electrician check your panel, since an older panel may need an upgrade.

## What does it cost to run?

DOE's Alternative Fuels Data Center example uses 10.7¢ per kWh and 27 kWh per 100 miles, about $0.03 a mile, and about $6 to fill a depleted 54 kWh battery. Your rate differs. EIA's September 2026 outlook put the 2026 average US residential price at 18.2¢ per kWh (see [our summary](/posts/eia-residential-price-forecast-sept-2026/)), and rates vary by state and utility. Our math at 18.2¢: 54 kWh × $0.182 = **$9.83**. Check your own rate in [Rates by State](/rates-by-state/) or on your bill, and see [time-of-use rates](/posts/time-of-use-rates-and-solar/) if charging overnight is cheaper for you.

## Who should skip a Level 2 charger

- Drivers who cover short daily distances may be fine on Level 1. DOE says overnight Level 1 meets many owners' needs when a dedicated circuit outlet is near the parking spot.
- Renters; see [best EV chargers for renters and apartments](/posts/best-ev-chargers-for-renters-and-apartments/).
- Homes with a full panel and no budget for an upgrade.

## Incentives

The IRS lists the [30C credit](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/alternative-fuel-vehicle-refueling-property-credit) at 30% up to $1,000 per item for individuals, in eligible census tracts, for property placed in service through June 30, 2026. It does not cover a charger placed in service after that. Utility and state rebates vary; check your utility.

## Buying checklist

1. Your car's maximum AC charge rate and connector (J1772 or NACS).
2. Spare panel capacity and breaker size; electrician quote.
3. Indoor or outdoor install; outdoor-rated equipment for outdoors.
4. Plug-in or hardwired; see [hardwired vs plug-in chargers](/posts/hardwired-vs-plug-in-ev-chargers/).
5. Stated UL listing for the exact model.
6. Warranty length and who services it.
7. Utility rate plan and rebates.
8. Permit requirements; AFDC notes permits may be required.
