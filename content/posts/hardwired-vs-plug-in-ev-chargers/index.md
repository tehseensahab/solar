Choose a **plug-in** charger if you want portability and 40 amps (9.6 kW) is enough. Choose **hardwired** if you want up to 48 or 50 amps, a permanent install or no receptacle. The charger is only part of the answer: your panel, wire and breaker set the real limit. Specs are from manufacturer pages and DOE material read on October 24, 2026. We did not test these chargers. ## Side-by-side

| | Plug-in (NEMA 14-50 or 6-50) | Hardwired |
|---|---|---|
| Max current (examples) | 40 A (ChargePoint Home Flex plug models; Wallbox Pulsar Plus 40A) | Up to 50 A (ChargePoint Home Flex hardwired); up to 48 A (Tesla Wall Connector, per Tesla) |
| Max power | 40 × 240 = 9.6 kW | 48 × 240 = 11.5 kW; 50 × 240 = 12 kW |
| Installation | Electrician installs the outlet; you plug in | Electrician wires the charger to the circuit |
| Swap or move | Easy | Needs an electrician |
| Weak point | The outlet and plug | The connection is fixed; fewer contact points |
| Best for | Renters (with approval), portability, 40 A | Permanent installs, higher current |

Sources: [ChargePoint](https://www.chargepoint.com/drivers/home/), [Wallbox](https://wallbox.com/en_us/pulsar-plus-ev-charger), [Tesla](https://www.tesla.com/support/charging/wall-connector), [Emporia](https://www.emporiaenergy.com/ev-chargers/) (NEMA plug or hardwire, up to 48 A).

## The math: amps, kW and breakers

Power (kW) = volts × amps ÷ 1,000.

- 240 × 40 = 9.6 kW
- 240 × 48 = 11.52 kW

**Breaker.** DOE's [Alternative Fuels Data Center](https://afdc.energy.gov/fuels/electricity-charging-home) says the National Electrical Code treats EV charging as a continuous load, and NEC Article 625 covers most charging-equipment requirements. In practice that commonly means the breaker is rated for 125% of the charger's current: 40 × 1.25 = 50 A and 48 × 1.25 = 60 A. A licensed electrician confirms the sizing, wire gauge and panel capacity for your home. ChargePoint says its hardwired model works with circuits rated 20 to 80 A and its plug models with 40 A or 50 A circuits.

## When plug-in makes sense

- You may move and want to take the charger.
- The outlet already exists (a dryer or RV outlet) and is in good condition.
- 40 A is enough: at 9.6 kW, adding 40 kWh takes 40 ÷ 9.6 = about 4.2 hours (if your car accepts that rate).
- You want to unplug and swap units easily.

Plug-in options: ChargePoint Home Flex (NEMA 6-50 or 14-50, 40 A, listed as UL listed and ENERGY STAR certified on ChargePoint's page, 3-year warranty), Wallbox Pulsar Plus 40A (NEMA 14-50 plug install, from $649.99), Grizzl-E Classic 40A ($299.99), Emporia Pro ($599, NEMA plug or hardwire). ## When hardwired makes sense

- You want the highest rate your car and panel allow: 48 A on a 60 A circuit.
- You are installing outdoors or in a damp garage and want fewer exposed connections. DOE says outdoor installation is allowed with outdoor-rated equipment.
- The charger will stay put for years.
- You want a Tesla Wall Connector (up to 11.5 kW, 48-month residential warranty per Tesla) or a hardwired ChargePoint.

## Costs

We are not giving an installation price. We looked for a reliable, current US figure from a neutral source and did not find one we could verify, and costs depend on distance from the panel, panel capacity, wall type, permit fees and local labor. Ask each electrician for a written quote that lists materials, labor, permit fees and whether a panel upgrade is needed. Compare equipment prices (above) separately. Running cost: DOE's example of 10.7¢ per kWh implies about $6 to fill a depleted 54 kWh battery; our math at EIA's 18.2¢ forecast gives 54 × 0.182 = $9.83. ## Who should skip the upgrade

- If Level 1 meets your needs. DOE says it does for many owners with an outlet on a dedicated circuit.
- If you rent; see [renter and apartment options](/posts/best-ev-chargers-for-renters-and-apartments/).
- If your panel is full and an upgrade isn't in budget; get a load calculation first.

## Incentives

The IRS lists the 30C credit for property placed in service through June 30, 2026, so it does not cover a charger installed now. State and utility rebates vary by location; check your utility and see our [rate guides](/rates-by-state/). Confirm eligibility with a tax professional.

## Decision checklist

1. Car's maximum AC charge rate and connector type.
2. Panel capacity and spare breaker slots.
3. Distance from panel to the charger location.
4. Portability needs.
5. Indoor or outdoor mounting.
6. Permits and inspection requirements.
7. Two or more written quotes.
8. Stated UL listing for the exact model. See [best Level 2 chargers for home](/posts/best-level-2-ev-chargers-for-home/).
