**The short answer:** Virginia regulators did not adopt the deep cuts Dominion Energy Virginia asked for. On April 30, 2026, the State Corporation Commission (SCC) approved a successor net metering tariff, called NEM 2.0, that keeps energy-for-energy netting across a one-year period. The biggest change for new customers is what they are paid for energy left over at the end of the year: $0.05829 per kilowatt-hour (kWh).

This article covers Dominion Energy Virginia (legal name Virginia Electric and Power Company), Case No. PUR-2025-00079. It does not cover Appalachian Power or electric cooperatives, which have separate proceedings.

## What the SCC Decided

The SCC's May 20 order summarizes the April 30 Final Order. It did three things for the successor tariff:

1. **Kept energy-based netting.** Energy you send to the grid offsets energy you draw, kWh for kWh, over the net metering period. Dominion had proposed a monetary (dollar-based) approach at short intervals. The SCC declined it "at that time."
2. **Set the surplus rate at $0.05829 per kWh.** This is the "export credit rate" the SCC says is paid to customer-generators for excess energy over the annual period.
3. **Found 30 minutes an appropriate netting measurement interval,** as Dominion requested. Virginia law defines the net metering period itself as one year, and that is unchanged. The SCC said the interval should have no financial consequences for a customer, and that the former default monthly interval is still just and reasonable.

The SCC also approved a **$1.00 per month administrative charge** for net metering accounts.

## How It Works for a Customer

Think of the year as one running account:

- **Months you use more than you generate:** your solar output offsets your use first. Per a law firm summary of the order, new customers offset consumption at the retail rate, and carry excess forward as kWh credits for 12 months.
- **If you end the year as a net exporter:** the leftover kWh are paid at $0.05829 per kWh.
- **If you end the year as a net importer:** the SCC says the new tariff "operates akin to the former one financially."

**Worked example (our calculation):** a home that exports 1,000 kWh more than it imports over the annual period earns 1,000 × $0.05829 = **$58.29**. The $1.00 monthly charge adds up to $12.00 over 12 months. This is an illustration, not a forecast for any household.

The practical lesson: a system sized to your own annual use rarely reaches the surplus rate, so the change matters most for oversized systems. Our guide to [how export credits compare across utilities](/posts/solar-export-credit-rates-by-utility-2026/) explains why.

## Where the $0.05829 Comes From

The SCC order gives the rate but not its components. A law firm client alert (Whiteford, May 6, 2026) describes it as a 4.829¢ per kWh avoided-cost rate plus 1¢ per kWh for avoided Renewable Portfolio Standard compliance costs. Avoided cost is roughly what it costs the utility to buy or produce a unit of power instead. Trade press (pv magazine USA, May 4, 2026) reports the legacy surplus rate was based on avoided cost alone, with about a penny added. We have not checked those components against the Final Order itself.

## Who Is Affected and When

- **Applies to:** Dominion Energy Virginia's non-low-income customers with new or modified interconnections after the effective date. The SCC's May 20 order says Dominion proposed that date as "the first of the month that is at least 12 months from the date of a final order," and the SCC deemed it reasonable.
- **Our calculation of that date:** twelve months from April 30, 2026 is April 30, 2027, so the first of the month on or after it is **May 1, 2027**. The order does not print that date. Treat it as our reading and confirm with Dominion.
- **Current net metering customers:** pv magazine USA and Whiteford both report that existing customers can keep the current tariff. We did not see this in the May 20 order, so we treat it as reported, not confirmed from the order.
- **Low-income customers:** the order text describes the new terms as applying to non-low-income customers. We did not find details of how low-income customers are treated.

## What About Dominion's Other Proposals?

According to pv magazine USA, the SCC rejected Dominion's proposed application fee of $100 to $750 based on system size. The Whiteford alert also says the SCC declined to add a new application fee. We did not find the fee proposal in the May 20 order, which only mentions the $1.00 monthly charge.

## What to Do If You're Considering Solar in Dominion Territory

1. **Ask which tariff your interconnection falls under** and the date your application would be effective.
2. **Size for annual use.** The surplus rate is far below what you avoid by offsetting your own use, so avoid paying for output you will rarely use.
3. **Ask installers to model the $1.00 monthly charge and the surplus rate** explicitly.
4. **Check for a filed tariff.** Dominion must revise its NEM 2.0 tariff language to match the order. We did not find a report of that filing, so check the SCC docket and Dominion's tariff pages.

## Limitations

- We reviewed the SCC's May 20, 2026 clarification order, which summarizes the April 30 Final Order. We did not read the Final Order itself.
- We did not find a report that Dominion has filed its revised tariff.
- Conflicting figures exist: an advocacy group page describes the surplus rate and the prior rate differently from the SCC order. We used the SCC order's $0.05829.
- The 1,000 kWh example is our arithmetic, not an estimate for a typical home.
