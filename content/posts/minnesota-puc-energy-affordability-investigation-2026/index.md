The Minnesota Public Utilities Commission (PUC) has opened a statewide investigation into whether electric and natural gas bills are affordable, and what regulators and lawmakers could do about it. The first public session was held on October 13. Two more follow, and a report is due early next year.

## At a Glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Docket | 26-341 |
| Announced | September 25, 2026 |
| Covers | Electric and natural gas service from rate-regulated utilities |
| Facilitator | Regulatory Assistance Project (RAP) |
| Sessions | October 13, November 2, December 8, 2026, 9:30 a.m. to 12:30 p.m., St. Paul and livestream |
| First-phase comment deadline | October 16, 2026, per MPR News |
| Outcome | Report with recommendations to the Governor and Legislature, early 2027 |

## What the Commission Will Examine

According to the PUC, the inquiry will look at:

- How regulatory practices, state and federal requirements, and other cost drivers affect customer bills.
- Whether the Commission's current affordability tools work.
- Possible regulatory or legislative changes.

The first phase focuses on how different customer groups experience utility costs, how affordability should be defined and measured, the main drivers of rising costs, the effectiveness of existing tools, and the trade-offs of potential improvements.

The Commission says it will invite utilities, consumer advocates, labor, clean energy groups, businesses and agricultural customers to submit recommendations.

## The First Session

Attorney General Keith Ellison, whose office represents residential and small business customers before the PUC, spoke at the October 13 session. "It's hard to afford your life if you can't afford your electric bill," he said, according to his office. He cited an RMI definition of affordability, whether households can pay for essential energy "without sacrificing health or safety," and proposed adding "without sacrificing dignity."

MPR News quoted Annie Levenson-Falk of the Citizens Utility Board of Minnesota saying the number of people struggling to pay electric bills has risen significantly since the pandemic, disconnections have reached record levels in recent years, and residential customers owe about twice what they did before the pandemic. Those figures are hers as reported by MPR. We did not verify them against Commission data.

## The Context: Where Minnesota Prices Stand

The PUC itself says Minnesota's average residential electric bill was the 8th lowest in the nation in 2024 and that rate case reviews since 2020 have saved customers more than $1.25 billion compared with what utilities requested.

EIA's July 2026 figures put Minnesota's average residential price at 17.45¢ per kWh, up 3.1% from 16.92¢ a year earlier, below the U.S. average of 18.31¢. That is a statewide average including fixed charges. See our [state-by-state comparison](/posts/electricity-prices-by-state-july-2026/).

The investigation follows recent rate cases. Our [Minnesota rate guide](/posts/minnesota-electricity-rate-changes-2026/) covers the Xcel Energy decision approved July 31, 2026, about $211 million for 2025 and 2026, nearly 60% below the request.

## What It Means for You

1. **No bill changes yet.** The investigation produces recommendations. Rates change through rate cases and rules.
2. **You can be heard.** The Commission asks Minnesotans to describe how utility costs affect them. Use the public notice in Docket 26-341 for the format and deadline.
3. **Help is available now.** The PUC's affordability page points to energy assistance programs. Ask your utility about payment plans and assistance before you fall behind.
4. **Co-op or city utility customers** are outside the PUC's rate authority, so this inquiry mostly does not cover them.

## Next Dates

- **October 16:** first-phase comment deadline, per MPR News.
- **November 2:** second informational session.
- **December 8:** third informational session.
- **Early 2027:** report to the Governor and Legislature.

## Limitations

We did not read the Commission's public notice, so comment instructions and topics for the November and December sessions are not included. Hardship figures are from an advocate as reported by MPR News.
