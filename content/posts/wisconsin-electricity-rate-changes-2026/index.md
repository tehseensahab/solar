Wisconsin's electric rates did not change on October 1, 2026. The Public Service Commission of Wisconsin (PSC) usually sets rates for two calendar years at a time, so changes arrive in January. The 2026 increases are already in bills, and 2027 is partly decided.

This article covers the five large investor-owned utilities. It does not cover municipal utilities or cooperatives.

## Quick Summary

| Utility | 2026 residential electric change | 2027 | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| We Energies | +$9.73 a month | 4.7% requested, about $13 a month | 2026 approved; 2027 pending |
| Wisconsin Public Service | +$5.04 a month | 6.3% requested, about $11 a month | 2026 approved; 2027 pending |
| Alliant Energy | +$9.57 a month | +$17.45 a month | Both approved |
| Madison Gas and Electric | +$0.13 a month | +$5.44 a month | Both approved |
| Xcel Energy | +$13.47 a month | +$24.91 a month | Both approved |

Figures are average residential impacts reported by Wisconsin Public Radio. The 2027 figures for Alliant, Madison Gas and Electric and Xcel were reported without saying whether they are measured from 2025 or from 2026.

## We Energies and Wisconsin Public Service

Both are owned by WEC Energy Group. On November 7, 2024, the PSC approved:

- **We Energies:** $7.62 a month in 2025 and $9.73 in 2026 for an average residential customer.
- **Wisconsin Public Service:** $7.11 a month in 2025 and $5.04 in 2026.
- **Return on equity:** held at 9.8%. Both had asked for 10%.

**New requests.** The two utilities have asked for almost $500 million more in electric revenue over 2027 and 2028:

- **We Energies:** 4.7% in 2027 (about $13 a month) and 4.5% in 2028 (about $8 to $9).
- **Wisconsin Public Service:** 6.3% in 2027 (about $11 a month) and 3.5% in 2028 (about $5 to $5.50).

Wisconsin Public Radio reported that We Energies had the third-highest residential electric rates among Midwest investor-owned utilities in 2025.

## Alliant, Madison Gas and Electric, and Xcel

The PSC approved 2026 and 2027 rates for these three in November 2025. Settlements cut the electric requests by $57.8 million for Alliant, $28.5 million for Madison Gas and Electric and $24.6 million for Xcel.

## What This Means for Homeowners With Solar

Wisconsin has no single statewide net metering rule. Terms are set utility by utility in rate cases.

| Utility | System size limit |
|---|---|
| We Energies | 300 kW |
| Xcel Energy | 100 kW |
| Alliant (Wisconsin Power and Light) | 20 kW |
| Wisconsin Public Service | 20 kW |

Size limits as listed by EnergySage.

- **Surplus:** at We Energies, Alliant and Wisconsin Public Service, net excess generation is credited at an avoided-cost rate, not the retail rate. Xcel rolls credits over monthly at retail and pays year-end excess at a wholesale rate.
- **Rebate:** the statewide Focus on Energy program offers a small solar incentive when funding is available.
- **Pending cases:** utilities have proposed changes to solar buyback rates in past rate cases. Check whether the 2027 rate cases include one.

**What it means:** production up to your monthly use offsets retail energy charges. Surplus earns far less. With January increases already approved or requested, self-used solar gains value each year.

Rules checked October 5, 2026. Solar tariffs change, so confirm the current terms with your utility before you sign a contract. Our [solar payback guide](/posts/solar-payback-period-without-federal-tax-credit/) shows how the retail rate and the export credit change the math.

## What to Check on Your Bill

1. The fixed daily or monthly customer charge.
2. The energy charge, and whether you are on a time-of-use plan.
3. Your January 2027 bill insert, which will show the next change.

For help sorting the lines on your bill, see our guide to [reading your electric bill](/posts/how-to-read-your-electric-bill/).

## What Happens Next

The PSC typically decides rate cases late in the year for rates starting in January. Decisions on the We Energies and Wisconsin Public Service requests would be expected before January 2027. We did not confirm a date.

## Limitations

All figures are average residential impacts from Wisconsin Public Radio, not tariff rates, and each utility defines an average customer differently. We did not open PSC dockets or confirm the status of the pending 2027 cases after the requests were filed.

## Compare With Nearby States

**Statewide average for context:** the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) puts Wisconsin's average residential price at 19.06¢/kWh in July 2026, up 4.4% from 18.25¢ in July 2025. The U.S. average was 18.31¢. At 1,000 kWh that works out to $190.60 (1,000 × 19.06¢). This is total residential revenue divided by kWh sold across every utility in the state, fixed charges included, and it is a preliminary estimate. It is not any one utility's rate.
