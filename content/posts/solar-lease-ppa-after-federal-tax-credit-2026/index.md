**The short answer:** if you buy a home solar system today, you cannot claim the federal 30% residential tax credit. The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) says that credit, Section 25D, is not allowed for expenditures made after December 31, 2025. A different federal credit, Section 48E, still exists for businesses that own solar. That is why leases and power purchase agreements (PPAs) are being marketed as the way to still capture a federal incentive. The credit goes to the owner, not to you, and the window is closing.

This article is about U.S. federal tax rules for rooftop systems on homes. It is not tax advice. State and utility incentives, which vary widely, are outside its scope.

## What Ended: The Homeowner Credit (Section 25D)

Section 25D gave homeowners a credit worth 30% of the cost of qualifying solar and certain other systems. Its text now says it "shall not apply with respect to any expenditures made after December 31, 2025."

The IRS explained in FAQs published August 21, 2025 (FS-2025-05) that an expenditure is treated as made when the original installation is completed. So paying a deposit by year-end does not preserve the credit if installation finished in 2026. The statute (Section 25D(e)(8)(A)) says the same.

**What this means:** for a cash or loan purchase completed after December 31, 2025, the full installed price is your cost before any state or utility incentives. Our [payback guide](/posts/solar-payback-period-without-federal-tax-credit/) shows how to calculate payback on that basis.

**Illustration (our arithmetic):** EnergySage reported a median U.S. home solar price of $2.57 per watt in the first half of 2026, before incentives ([our summary](/posts/energysage-h1-2026-solar-market-report/)). An 8 kW (8,000 watt) system at that median would cost 8,000 × $2.57 = $20,560. The 30% credit that would have applied before 2026 is 30% × $20,560 = $6,168. This is a hypothetical, not a quote.

If you completed an installation by December 31, 2025, Section 25D allows an excess credit to be carried to the following tax year. Check your own return or a tax professional.

## What Remains: The Owner's Credit (Section 48E)

In a lease or PPA, a company owns the panels on your roof. Section 48E is a federal tax credit for qualifying clean electricity facilities. For solar:

- **The credit goes to the owner.** The customer is not the taxpayer claiming it. That follows from who owns the property, and we have not seen a provider-specific tax analysis.
- **A deadline applies.** The statute says Section 48E "shall not apply to any qualified property placed in service by the taxpayer after December 31, 2027" for applicable wind and solar facilities, and the applicable facilities are those whose construction begins after the date 12 months after July 4, 2025, which is July 4, 2026. In plain terms: a solar facility that begins construction after July 4, 2026 generally has to be placed in service by December 31, 2027.
- **Earlier starts may have longer deadlines.** A tax journal summary (The Tax Adviser, February 2026) says facilities that began construction between January 1 and July 4, 2026, and meet the "continuity safe harbor," have until December 31, 2030 to be placed in service. Whether a particular provider's systems qualify depends on its construction records, which a customer cannot see.
- **Leasing is restricted for some property.** Section 48E(i) denies the credit for certain property leased to a third party, but it points to Section 25D(d)(1) and (4), which cover solar water heating and small wind turbines. It does not mention rooftop solar electric panels.

Because today is October 12, 2026, the July 4, 2026 date has passed. For a new lease or PPA, assume the provider's system must be placed in service by December 31, 2027 unless the provider says it qualifies for an earlier-start safe harbor.

### A court ruling you may see mentioned

In June 2026, a federal court in Washington, D.C. vacated IRS Notice 2025-42, which had narrowed how developers prove construction began, according to a Gibson Dunn legal alert. The alert says a government appeal was considered likely. We did not confirm whether an appeal has been filed. The notice had exempted solar facilities of 1.5 megawatts or less, a size far above a home system. So the ruling mainly concerns large projects, and it is unlikely to change what you are asked to sign.

## Buy, Lease or PPA: A Plain Comparison

| | Buy (cash or loan) | Lease | PPA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who owns the system | You | The provider | The provider |
| What you pay | Purchase price or loan payments | Fixed monthly payment | Per-kWh price for output |
| Federal credit for you | None for installations completed after Dec. 31, 2025 | None | None |
| Federal credit for the owner | Not applicable | Possibly, under Section 48E | Possibly, under Section 48E |
| Typical risks to check | Upfront cost, export credit rules | Escalators, transfer when you sell the home | Per-kWh price rising over time |

## How Common Are Leases and PPAs Now?

EnergySage reports that the share of installers quoting third-party ownership options rose from about 14% at the start of the first half of 2026 to about 41% by the end of the half. It says these projects "can still qualify for federal incentives through 2027." See our [EnergySage report summary](/posts/energysage-h1-2026-solar-market-report/).

## Questions to Ask Before Signing

1. **Who claims the federal credit and has the system been placed in service, or will it be, before December 31, 2027?** Ask the provider to state in writing which deadline applies.
2. **What is the total cost over the full term?** Add every payment. For a PPA, check the per-kWh price in year 1 and any annual escalator.
3. **What happens if you sell the home?** Ask whether the buyer must approve a transfer.
4. **What does your utility pay for exported power?** The savings depend on [your utility's export credit rules](/posts/solar-export-credit-rates-by-utility-2026/) and [net metering](/posts/net-metering-state-guide/) in your state.
5. **What happens if the credit is reduced or recaptured?** Ask whether your price can change.
6. **Get a cash and loan quote for the same system** so you can compare like for like.
