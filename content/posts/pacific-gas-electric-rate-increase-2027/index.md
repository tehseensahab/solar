**The CPUC approved an interim PG&E rate increase for 2027: up to $456 million starting January 1, 2027.** The California Public Utilities Commission voted on Nov. 19, 2026, and NBC Bay Area reported the increase adds about $6 a month to the average bill. It is a stopgap while regulators finish PG&E's 2027 general rate case, so it is not the last change customers will see in 2027.

For a typical home, three main things will shape January's bill: this interim increase, a separate pending case on PG&E's fuel and power-purchase costs, and PG&E's final Jan. 1 rate filing, due in late December. Here is what is approved, what is still requested, and how to read your own bill. For PG&E's current rates in context with other California utilities, see our [guide to California electric rates and rooftop solar](/rates/california/).

## What did the CPUC approve for PG&E?

The CPUC's Nov. 16, 2026 ratesetting agenda lists the item for its Nov. 19 voting meeting in PG&E's 2027 general rate case (Application 25-05-009, Agenda ID 24547). It gives the estimated cost as "Up to $456 million," with interim relief "beginning January 1, 2027" that would "remain in effect until implementation of a final decision."

NBC Bay Area and KMPH reported that the commission approved it on Nov. 19, and NBC reported the vote was unanimous. We have not found the adopted decision text, so the vote result comes from news reports.

What "interim" means for you:

- **It is approved and starts Jan. 1, 2027.** PG&E does not have to wait for the final rate case decision to begin collecting this amount.
- **It is part of a larger request.** PG&E asked for a 2027 revenue requirement of $16.637 billion, "an increase of $1.237 billion, or 8%," over 2026 authorized amounts, according to the CPUC's fact sheet on the case. It also asked for 6.1% revenue increases in each of 2028, 2029 and 2030. None of that is decided.
- **It can be adjusted later.** KMPH reported that "interim collections are subject to refund." If the final decision authorizes less, the difference would be expected to come back to customers. Some ratepayer advocates argued against the interim increase before the vote.

## How much will PG&E bills go up in January 2027?

There is no single official number yet. The figures reported so far:

| Figure | What it covers | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Up to $456 million | Interim rate case revenue from Jan. 1, 2027 | CPUC agenda | Approved Nov. 19, 2026 (per news reports) |
| About $6 a month | "the average bill" (no kWh or class given) | NBC Bay Area | Reported, not a PG&E or CPUC estimate we have seen |
| 2.2% | Electric rates on Jan. 1 (base not stated; not linked to the $456 million in the report) | CBS Sacramento | Reported, not confirmed in a primary source |
| About $10.55 a month | Fuel and power-cost forecast (ERRA) request, typical bundled residential customer at 500 kWh | PG&E bill insert | Requested; pending |
| $11.18 a month (5.2%) | Full rate case request, non-CARE residential electric bill, 500 kWh, 2027 vs. 2025 | CPUC fact sheet | Requested; pending |

**An illustration, not a forecast.** PG&E's average bundled, non-CARE residential rate was 41.46 cents per kWh as of Jan. 1, 2026, according to its rate advisory. If a 2.2% increase applied evenly to that rate, it would add about 0.91 cents per kWh, or about $4.56 a month at 500 kWh (41.46 × 0.022 × 500 ÷ 100; our math). Your result will differ: plans, baseline allowances and CARE discounts change how any increase lands, and other Jan. 1 changes may raise or lower rates at the same time.

### Why the January number is still moving

PG&E, Southern California Edison and San Diego Gas & Electric filed preliminary advice letters on Nov. 16 for electric rates taking effect Jan. 1, 2027, according to California Regulatory Intelligence. Protests are due Dec. 7, and the utilities plan to file final rates in late December. The same publication reported that "typical bundled residential bills for non-CARE customers would rise at PG&E."

The biggest other piece is PG&E's 2027 ERRA forecast (Application 26-05-007), which covers fuel and power-purchase costs. In its customer notice, PG&E said it is "requesting $3,176 million in revenue for 2027" and that "a typical bundled residential customer using 500 kWh per month would see a rate increase of approximately $10.55 per month." That is a request; the CPUC has not decided it. A California Regulatory Intelligence preview lists PG&E's ERRA for the CPUC's Dec. 3 voting meeting at $3.45 billion, above the June request, so the $10.55 estimate may have changed.

## What happens next

- **Late December 2026:** PG&E files final Jan. 1 rates.
- **Jan. 1, 2027:** the interim increase and any other approved changes take effect.
- **2027:** the CPUC decides the full rate case. Its September 2025 fact sheet said it anticipated authorizing new rates "in May of 2027"; that schedule may change. The interim amount is expected to be trued up against the final decision, which could mean refunds or, if more is authorized, later charges.

If you live in Southern California Edison territory, our [SCE 2027 rate outlook](/posts/sce-rate-increase-2026/) covers that utility's pending changes. Note that "PGE" in Oregon is a different company: Portland General Electric's request is covered in our [Portland General Electric 2027 rate case article](/posts/pge-rate-increase-2027/).

## What it means if you have or want solar

Every cent added to the per-kWh price raises the value of each kWh your panels let you avoid buying from PG&E. Under California's current net billing rules, power you use yourself is usually worth more than power you export, so the households that gain most from higher rates are those that use their solar output at home, or store it in a battery for the evening.

Two cautions. First, a few percent on one year's rates usually changes a solar payback only modestly; run the numbers with your own bill rather than a salesperson's rate-escalation assumption. Our [solar payback guide](/posts/solar-payback-period-without-federal-tax-credit/) shows how. Second, the final 2027 rate case and the ERRA decision could move rates in either direction, so a quote built on the January bill alone may be off.
