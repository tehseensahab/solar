**Southern California Edison did raise rates in 2026, but only slightly and only once: on October 1, its average residential rate went from 34.4 to 34.5 cents per kWh, and SCE estimated a typical 500 kWh monthly bill rose $2.79 (1.5%). Its January and June changes were cuts, so SCE's average home rate is still about 2.3% below where it stood at the end of 2025 (and unchanged at 33.3 cents counting the California Climate Credit).** The next change depends on decisions the California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC) is scheduled to make in December.

This page lays out each 2026 change from SCE's own rate advisories, explains why October went up, and shows what is pending for 2027. For PG&E, SDG&E and statewide context, see our [California electricity rates and solar guide](/posts/california-electricity-rate-changes-2026/).

## Every SCE Rate Change in 2026

SCE publishes a rate advisory each time its rates change. Here are the three for 2026, using SCE's average residential rate and its estimate for a typical customer using 500 kWh a month.

| Effective date | Average residential rate (before → after) | Typical 500 kWh bill (before → after) | Change | CARE 500 kWh bill |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| January 1, 2026 | 35.3 → 34.5 ¢/kWh | $193.06 → $187.56 | −$5.51 (−2.9%) | $117.46 → $112.40 (−4.3%) |
| June 1, 2026 | 34.5 → 34.4 ¢/kWh | $187.56 → $187.40 | −$0.15 (−0.1%) | $112.40 → $112.27 (−0.1%) |
| October 1, 2026 | 34.4 → 34.5 ¢/kWh | $185.85 → $188.64 | +$2.79 (+1.5%) | $111.06 → $111.25 (+0.2%) |

Bills are SCE's estimates without the California Climate Credit; SCE's dollar changes may not match the before and after bills exactly because of rounding. CARE is SCE's income-qualified discount program.

Two cautions when reading the table. First, the October advisory bases its typical bill on the TOU-D-4-9PM plan, while the January and June advisories do not name a plan, which is likely why October's "before" bill ($185.85) does not match June's "after" bill ($187.40). Do not add the dollar changes together. Second, these are averages: your own change depends on your rate plan, how much you use and when.

Counting the Climate Credit, SCE lists the average residential rate at 33.2 cents after January 1, 33.2 cents after June 1 and 33.3 cents after October 1.

### Why did SCE rates go up on October 1, 2026?

SCE's October advisory gives five pieces:

- **Rate design (2025 General Rate Case, Phase 2):** SCE says Phase 2 "updates the distribution of costs amongst all rate groups" and that, "as a result, residential rates will increase beginning October 1."
- **Woolsey Fire:** $49.674 million for restoration-related costs and $140.110 million for recovery bonds that finance wildfire claims costs.
- **2022 fuel and power-purchase review:** $59.682 million to recover several memorandum accounts.
- **A small refund:** $0.246 million returned from the Affiliate Transfer Fee Memorandum Account.
- **Costs removed:** $628.062 million taken out of rates because customers have already fully paid those costs. This pulled rates down for everyone.

The result for homes was a 0.1-cent rise in the average residential rate and the $2.79 a month increase at 500 kWh shown above.

### Why did rates fall in January and June?

SCE said its January 1 decrease came "mainly" from lower generation rates, reflecting lower forecast power costs and higher forecast usage, partly offset by a $476 million increase for its 2025 General Rate Case and $122 million for Thomas Fire and Montecito debris-flow recovery bonds. In June, a $380.690 million increase for 2026 wildfire liability self-insurance was outweighed by decreases, including $214.456 million in program charges and $84.468 million in transmission rates.

## What Is SCE's Rate Now?

Since October 1, 2026, SCE's average residential rate is **34.5 cents per kWh**, or 33.3 cents counting the Climate Credit. That is 0.8 cents, or about 2.3%, below the 35.3 cents in effect before January 1, 2026 (our calculation from SCE's advisories). Counting the Climate Credit, the rate is the same as at the end of 2025: 33.3 cents.

Most SCE households also pay a fixed **Base Services Charge** that does not depend on usage. SCE says it took effect in November 2025 and is:

| Customer | Base Services Charge |
|---|---|
| Not enrolled in CARE or FERA | $24.15 a month |
| FERA, or qualifying deed-restricted affordable housing | $12.08 a month |
| CARE | $6.00 a month |

SCE says the exact amount depends on the days in your billing cycle, and that when the charge began, "the cost you pay for each kilowatt-hour (kWh) of electricity decreases by approximately 10%." Our guide to [fixed charges and solar savings](/posts/fixed-charges-and-solar-savings/) explains why that matters for panels.

## Is an SCE Rate Increase Coming in 2027?

Possibly, but the main decision has not been made. The piece we can track is SCE's 2027 Energy Resource Recovery Account (ERRA) forecast, CPUC application A.26-05-006. SCE describes it as "an estimate of how much it will cost SCE to buy and produce electricity for customers in the coming year."

| Date | Step | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| May 15, 2026 | SCE files A.26-05-006 | Proposed 2027 ERRA revenue of $4.350 billion. SCE's customer notice: residential rates +0.39 ¢/kWh (1.1%); at 500 kWh, about +$2.20 a month (1.2%) "if the forecast remains unchanged and is approved." CARE: +$1.45 (1.3%). |
| October 15, 2026 | SCE's October Update | Requests $4.510 billion beginning January 1, 2027, "an increase of $159.856 million" from the May estimate. Expects a semi-annual Climate Credit of $47.00 in 2027, less than the $54.00 in its May testimony. |
| By November 24, 2026 | CPUC proposed decision | Scheduled in the CPUC scoping memo. |
| December 17, 2026 | CPUC final decision | Scheduled in the CPUC scoping memo. |

The October Update raised the revenue request, but we did not find an updated residential bill estimate in it, so the $2.20 figure from May should be treated as out of date. SCE's October Update also estimates that average generation rates for customers who buy power from SCE will decrease about 7.36%, "from 10.15 to 9.40 cents/kWh." That is SCE's forecast for one part of the bill, not a statement about total bills.

Other pieces can also change on January 1; SCE says, for example, "Every January 1 we adjust transmission rates." We had not found a January 1, 2027 advisory when we checked on November 17, 2026. We will update this page when the CPUC rules.

## What SCE's 2026 Rates Mean for Solar Owners

For most solar homes, 2026 changed little. A 0.1-cent move in the average rate barely changes how much a kWh of solar you use yourself saves you.

If you went solar under SCE's **Solar Billing Plan** (SCE's version of California's net billing tariff), SCE says:

- "Solar Billing Plan customers are on the TOU-D-PRIME rate plan."
- "You pay for electricity used from the grid based on your applicable time-of-use (TOU) rate plan."
- Credits for power you export vary with how much you send and "how much the grid needs it," and "Solar Billing Plan credit values are less than what you pay for electricity from the grid."
- "For new Solar Billing Plan customers, rates are locked in for nine years based on the year you began using solar."

In practice, solar power you use at home offsets the per-kWh time-of-use rate you would otherwise pay, while exports earn less. Shifting use, or adding a battery, to cover evening hours tends to matter more than small rate changes. Our [time-of-use rates and solar guide](/posts/time-of-use-rates-and-solar/) explains why. The Base Services Charge applies to solar customers too, and solar cannot offset it.

## Who This Applies To

SCE residential electric customers. Business customers are not covered. If you buy power from a community choice aggregator (CCA) instead of SCE, check your CCA's generation rates as well, because SCE's typical-bill estimates are for its own customers.

PG&E and SDG&E customers should see the California guide linked above. Pacific Power customers in Northern California should see our [Pacific Power California 2027 article](/posts/pacific-power-california-rate-increase-2027/).
