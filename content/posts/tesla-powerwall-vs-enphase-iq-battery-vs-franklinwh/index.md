Buy the **Tesla Powerwall 3** if you want the most continuous power from one unit, the **FranklinWH aPower 2** if you want the biggest single battery with a 15-year warranty, and an **Enphase IQ Battery** if you want to add capacity in small steps or already run Enphase microinverters. These are our reading of manufacturer documents and published analyses as of October 24, 2026. We did not test any of them. See [how we pick products](/how-we-pick/).

## Which should you buy?

### If you want the most power from one box: Powerwall 3

Its 11.5 kW continuous discharge is the highest here, and the datasheet lists UL 1741, UL 9540, UL 9540A and UL 1973. Tesla's page says the unit includes an integrated solar inverter, and the datasheet lists up to 20 kW of PV input. In a new solar-plus-storage install that can mean fewer boxes. The trade-off is the 10-year warranty, five years shorter than the other two.

### If you want capacity and a long warranty: aPower 2

FranklinWH lists 15 kWh, 10 kW continuous, 15 years or 60 MWh, and IP67 protection. Its stacking limit of 15 units per aGate gives a ceiling of 225 kWh. The product page we read did not state a UL listing; ask for the document for the exact model.

<a href="#affiliate-link-pending" rel="sponsored nofollow">Check aPower 2 price</a>

### If you want modular steps or already have Enphase: IQ Battery

The 5P adds 5.0 kWh and 3.84 kW per unit; the 10C adds 10 kWh and 7.08 kW. Both carry a 15-year warranty up to 6,000 cycles. The 5P requires an IQ System Controller 3 or 3G for backup and Enphase-certified commissioning. Per kWh, EnergySage lists the 10C at the highest price of the batteries in its table, so compare full quotes, not spec sheets.

## Price check

EnergySage's per-kWh figures (Apr 2026) are $998 for Powerwall 3, $1,177 for aPower 2 and $1,404 for IQ Battery 10C. Solar.com's [September 2026 installer price lists](https://solar.com/learn/best-solar-batteries/) show medians of about $15,000 (Powerwall 3), $17,250 (aPower 2) and $14,250 (IQ Battery 10C) installed with solar, before incentives. These are marketplace medians and per-kWh averages, not quotes, and the 10C is smaller, so a lower total can still mean a higher price per kWh. Prices checked October 24, 2026.

Worked example (our math): $998 per kWh × 13.5 kWh = $13,473 for a Powerwall 3 at EnergySage's per-kWh figure; $1,404 × 10 kWh = $14,040 for one 10C. The 10C costs more in total for 3.5 kWh less capacity at those figures.

## Who should skip all three

- Homes with rare short outages and no time-of-use rate; a battery rarely pays back on bill savings alone.
- Buyers who need weeks of backup; solar recharge or a generator matters more than battery brand.
- Anyone counting on a federal credit; see below.

## Incentives

The IRS says the [residential clean energy credit](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/residential-clean-energy-credit) is not available for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. State, local and utility programs vary; check [Rates by State](/rates-by-state/) and your utility. Leased or financed arrangements can be taxed differently; ask the provider for the written basis.

## How to decide

1. Decide required kW (loads at once) and kWh (hours). See [how to size a home battery](/posts/how-to-size-a-home-battery/).
2. If you already have Enphase microinverters, price an IQ Battery first; otherwise price all three.
3. Ask each installer for model numbers, warranty documents and UL certification for the exact unit.
4. Compare total installed price, not price per kWh alone.
5. Read the [time-of-use guide](/posts/time-of-use-rates-and-solar/) if your utility has peak pricing.

## Limitations

Specs are manufacturer-stated and sometimes nominal rather than usable. We found no independent owner-satisfaction data for these batteries; the Solar.com review analysis rates installers, not products. This is not tax or engineering advice.
