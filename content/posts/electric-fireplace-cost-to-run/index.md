**A plug-in electric fireplace costs about 27 cents an hour to run on high heat.** All six models we checked are rated at 1,500 watts maximum, which is 1.5 kWh an hour, and the Energy Information Administration's (EIA) U.S. average residential price was 18.16 cents per kWh in August 2026. The low setting (750 watts on several models) costs about 14 cents an hour, and the flame effect alone costs about 5 cents an hour or less.

That makes the heater, not the flame, the part that shows up on your bill. This guide shows the math, what a thermostat does to it, and when a fireplace saves money and when it does not. If your home already heats with electric resistance, our [space heater cost guide](/posts/space-heater-cost-to-run/) uses the same 1,500-watt math, and [kW vs kWh](/posts/kw-vs-kwh/) explains the units.

## The Formula

**Cost = watts ÷ 1,000 × hours × your price per kWh**

Your price per kWh is on your bill. If your bill splits supply and delivery, add the per-kWh charges together and leave out the fixed monthly customer charge, which you pay whether the fireplace runs or not.

## How Much Does an Electric Fireplace Cost to Run per Hour?

At EIA's August 2026 U.S. average residential price of 18.16 cents per kWh (released Oct. 23, 2026; one month's price, not a yearly average):

| Setting | Power | kWh per hour | Cost per hour at 18.16¢ |
|---|---|---|---|
| High heat | 1,500 watts | 1.5 | 27.2¢ |
| Low heat | 750 watts | 0.75 | 13.6¢ |
| Thermostat cycling, heater on half the time | 1,500 watts × 50% | 0.75 (average) | 13.6¢ |
| Flame effect only (Dimplex figure) | just under 300 watts | under 0.3 | under 5.4¢ |

How we calculated it: watts ÷ 1,000 × $0.1816. The 50% cycling row is an example, not a measurement; how often the heater runs depends on the room, the thermostat setting and the outside temperature.

Dimplex's support page says that with heat on, "the unit uses approximately 1500 Watts," and that it produces "about 5115 BTU of total heat." That matches the physics: 1,500 watts of resistance heat is about 5,118 BTU an hour (1 watt = 3.412 BTU per hour).

## How Much Does It Cost per Month?

Most people do not run a fireplace heater around the clock. Here is the cost for 4 hours a day for 30 days at 18.16 cents per kWh:

| Setting | kWh per month (4 h × 30 days) | Cost per month |
|---|---|---|
| High heat, running the whole time | 180 | $32.69 |
| Low heat, or high with the heater on half the time | 90 | $16.34 |
| Flame only (under 300 watts) | under 36 | under $6.54 |

Over a 120-day heating season at the same 4 hours a day, high heat comes to 720 kWh, or about $131, and the flame alone to about $26 or less (our math).

How we calculated it: watts ÷ 1,000 × 4 hours × 30 days (or 120 days) × $0.1816. For scale, 180 kWh is about a fifth of the 863 kWh a month the average U.S. home bought in 2024, according to EIA; see [how much electricity a house uses](/posts/how-much-electricity-does-a-house-use/).

## Does the Flame Effect Use Much Electricity?

No. Dimplex's support page says, "The flame uses slightly under 300 Watts of power," and that the flame and heater "operate independently of each other." At 18.16 cents per kWh, 300 watts is about 5.4 cents an hour, so "slightly under" means a little less. That figure is for Dimplex's flames generally; models with LED flame effects may draw less, and your manual or the rating label on the unit will say.

Not every model lets you run heat without the flame. Touchstone's Onyx and Sideline manuals say, "The heater only works when the flame effect is on," and e-Flame USA's Breckenridge manual says, "The flame effect must be on for the heater to turn on." On those models, the flame runs whenever the heater does; the 1,500-watt rating is the unit's maximum draw.

## Does a Thermostat Lower the Cost?

Yes. A fireplace with a thermostat runs the heater only until the room reaches the set temperature, then shuts it off and turns it back on as the room cools. Duraflame's DFI-7105 manual says the heater will "automatically maintain the desired comfort level by cycling the heater on and off." Dimplex's own cost estimate assumes the heater cycles at 50%.

The ratings are the same either way. The thermostat changes how many hours the heater is actually on. Setting it a few degrees lower cuts heater run time, and the cost with it. The 750-watt setting saves money only if it leaves the room cooler than high heat would; with a thermostat, a lower setting simply runs longer to reach the same temperature.

## Is an Electric Fireplace a Cheap Way to Heat?

It depends on what it replaces.

- **Heating one room while you turn the whole-house heat down** can save money, because you are heating a smaller space. The savings depend on your main heating system and fuel, and this is the same logic that applies to space heaters.
- **Adding heat on top of your normal heating** saves nothing. It is an extra 1.5 kWh an hour.
- **Replacing a heat pump** usually costs more per unit of heat. A plug-in fireplace is a resistance heater: every kWh becomes one kWh of heat. A heat pump moves more heat than the electricity it uses (its efficiency falls in very cold weather but usually stays above a resistance heater's); see [heat pump vs gas furnace cost](/posts/heat-pump-vs-gas-furnace-cost/) for that math.

The heat-output claims in manuals (400 to 1,000 square feet in the models above) describe supplemental heat, not a home's main heating.

## What This Means for Homes With Solar

In most of the U.S., solar panels produce less in winter, which is when a fireplace heater runs, and fireplace use often falls in the evening, after the sun is down. Unless you have a battery, those evening kWh mostly come from the grid. Many time-of-use plans charge the most in late-afternoon and evening peak hours, so the per-hour cost can be higher than the averages above. Check your rate plan before assuming your panels cover the fireplace.

## Safety Basics From the Manuals

The manuals we checked call for a standard 120-volt, 15-amp grounded circuit: Touchstone's Onyx calls for a "15 AMP Grounded Circuit," and e-Flame USA says to use the heater "ONLY" on "a 120V AC 15-amp grounded circuit." A 1,500-watt heater draws about 12.5 amps (Duraflame's manuals list 12.5 amps), so avoid sharing the circuit with other large loads, and follow your model's instructions on extension cords and clearances.

## Limitations

- Ratings are from a small number of manuals and one manufacturer support page, not a market survey. Built-in and larger insert models can differ.
- The Dimplex flame figure is a general statement on its support page, not a specific model's rating.
- Prices are EIA averages for August 2026 and may not reflect all taxes and fees on your bill. Your bill's per-kWh rate is the number to use.
- The 50% cycling example is illustrative; actual heater run time varies.
