The best home battery for backup power is the one whose **continuous output (kW)** covers the loads you run at the same time and whose **capacity (kWh)** covers the hours you want to ride through. Among the batteries we reviewed, the Tesla Powerwall 3 and FranklinWH aPower 2 offer the highest output from a single unit, and Enphase IQ Batteries are the most modular. All figures below come from manufacturer documents or the published analyses we name, as of October 24, 2026.

We have not tested these batteries. Our picks rest on specs, certifications, warranties and published expert and installer-marketplace analyses. See [how we pick products](/how-we-pick/).

## What does a backup battery actually have to do?

Two numbers decide backup performance:

- **Power (kW):** the most the battery can deliver at one moment. Add up everything that might run together: refrigerator, lights, internet, well pump, furnace blower, an air conditioner.
- **Energy (kWh):** how long that power lasts. Hours of backup = usable kWh ÷ average kW load.

Example (an illustration with assumed numbers, not a measurement): a home that averages 1.5 kW of essential loads for a 12-hour overnight outage needs 1.5 × 12 = 18 kWh. One 13.5 kWh battery would run out in about 9 hours (13.5 ÷ 1.5) before any solar recharge. See [how to size a home battery](/posts/how-to-size-a-home-battery/) for the full method, and our earlier [battery sizing guide](/posts/battery-sizing-guide/) for a lighter-load example.

## Product cards

### Tesla Powerwall 3 — best for high output from one unit

[Tesla's datasheet](https://digitalassets.tesla.com/tesla-contents/image/upload/powerwall-3-datasheet-na-en.pdf) lists 13.5 kWh nominal energy, 11.5 kW maximum continuous discharge, a 10-year warranty, an operating range of -4°F to 122°F (derating possible above 104°F) and up to 20 kW of solar input across six MPPTs. It lists UL 1741, UL 9540, UL 9540A and UL 1973 among its certifications. Tesla's own product page says it includes an integrated solar inverter.

**Pros**
- Highest continuous output of the batteries we compared.
- Integrated inverter means one box can handle solar and storage in a new install.
- Broad UL certification list on the datasheet.

**Cons**
- Shortest warranty of the four (10 years).
- Datasheet states nominal, not usable, energy.
- Tesla's public pages do not list a price; installed cost varies by installer.

**Approximate price:** EnergySage's [April 2026 battery ranking](https://www.energysage.com/solar/solar-energy-storage/what-are-the-best-batteries-for-solar-panels/) lists $998 per kWh; Solar.com's [battery guide](https://solar.com/learn/best-solar-batteries/) reports a median of about $15,000 installed with solar (September 2026 installer price lists, before incentives). Prices checked October 24, 2026.

### FranklinWH aPower 2 — best for capacity and tough sites

FranklinWH's [product page](https://www.franklinwh.com/products/apower2-home-battery-backup/) lists 15 kWh capacity, 10 kW continuous power, a 15-year warranty covering 60 MWh of throughput, IP67 dust and water protection, LFP chemistry and an operating range of -4°F to 131°F. **Pros**
- Largest single-unit capacity here.

**Cons**
- Certifications are not stated on the product page we read.
- The page does not say whether 15 kWh is usable energy.
- Uses FranklinWH's own aGate controller, so it is a different ecosystem from Tesla or Enphase.

**Approximate price:** EnergySage lists $1,177 per kWh; Solar.com reports about $17,250 median installed with solar (before incentives). Prices checked October 24, 2026.

## Who should skip a battery

- **Outages are rare and short.** A battery is expensive insurance. If you mostly need to keep a phone, router and refrigerator going, a smaller system, or no battery, may do.
- **You are on a flat rate with generous net metering.** A battery adds little bill savings there. See [net metering by state](/posts/net-metering-state-guide/).
- **You plan to run a large well pump or central air for days.** Batteries ride through outages; they do not replace a generator for multi-day events unless sized and recharged by solar.
- **You expect a federal tax credit.** The credit for batteries placed in service after December 31, 2025 has ended (below).

## Incentives: what changed

The IRS says the [residential clean energy credit](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/residential-clean-energy-credit) is not available for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. Storage of at least 3 kWh used to qualify. State incentives, utility battery programs and different rules for leased systems may still apply. Check your state's page in [Rates by State](/rates-by-state/) and ask your installer for the written basis of any incentive they quote. Confirm eligibility with a qualified tax professional.

## Buying checklist

1. **Critical loads and their kW.** List what must run at once, including starting surges.
2. **Hours of backup.** Hours = usable kWh ÷ average kW.
3. **Existing solar.** Same-brand microinverters or a new hybrid system change which battery fits.
4. **Budget at full price.** Get at least three written quotes with equipment model numbers.
5. **Climate and location.** Compare operating temperature ranges and outdoor ratings.
6. **Warranty.** Compare years and any cycle or throughput cap.
7. **Utility rules.** Interconnection approval and export rules vary by utility; see our [rate and policy guides](/rates-by-state/).
8. **Certifications.** Ask for UL 9540 documentation for the exact model.
