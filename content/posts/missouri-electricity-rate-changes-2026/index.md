Missouri's large investor-owned utilities did not change base rates on October 1, 2026. Both Ameren Missouri and Evergy Missouri Metro have increases pending before the Missouri Public Service Commission (PSC).

This article covers Ameren Missouri, which serves about 1.2 million electric customers, and Evergy Missouri Metro, which serves about 313,000. It does not cover Evergy Missouri West, Liberty or electric cooperatives.

## Ameren Missouri

**The last increase.** On April 23, 2025, the PSC approved an agreement raising Ameren Missouri's electric revenue by $355 million. Ameren had asked for $446 million. The $9 residential monthly customer charge was left unchanged.

**The new request.** Ameren has filed again, in case ER-2026-0291:

- **Amount:** a net increase of $343 million a year in electric base rates.
- **Bills:** about $13 a month for a home using 1,011 kWh, according to Ameren; its filing puts the average residential increase at 10.2%, or $154.53 a year.
- **Timing:** the PSC schedule has an evidentiary hearing March 15 to 26, 2027. Ameren has said new rates would take effect in mid-2027. Details in our [Ameren Missouri rate request breakdown](/posts/ameren-missouri-rate-increase-2027/).

The PSC also approved a large-load rate plan for Ameren in 2026, with protections intended to keep other customers from paying for the cost of serving very large users.

## Evergy Missouri Metro

Evergy Missouri Metro filed on February 6, 2026 (case ER-2026-0143), asking for about $137.9 million more a year in base rates. Evergy says an average residential customer would pay about $17.70 more a month if the request is approved as filed. The PSC held public hearings in July, scheduled the evidentiary hearing for September 28 through October 9, 2026, and suspended the proposed rates until January 6, 2027. We found no decision as of October 25. Separately, Evergy Missouri Metro's fuel adjustment fell on October 1, about $1.63 a month at 1,000 kWh. Our [Evergy Missouri Metro rate case explainer](/posts/evergy-missouri-metro-rate-increase-2026/) has the details.

## A Real October 1 Change: Columbia

City-owned utilities are not regulated by the PSC. Columbia raised electric usage fees 6% starting October 1, 2026, as covered in our [Columbia, Missouri article](/posts/columbia-missouri-electric-rates-october-2026/).

## What This Means for Homeowners With Solar

Missouri requires net metering, with surplus paid at a low avoided-cost rate.

- **Size limit:** 100 kW.
- **Within the month:** your solar offsets your own use at the retail rate.
- **Monthly surplus:** Ameren Missouri's solar page lists the credit at 3.84¢ per kWh in summer (June to September) and 3.39¢ in winter (October to May).
- **Fixed charge:** Ameren's $9 residential customer charge is not reduced by solar.

**What it means:** a kWh you use yourself avoids the retail energy rate. A kWh left over at the end of the month earns under 4¢. A system sized so that monthly production stays at or below monthly use keeps almost all of its value.

Rules checked October 5, 2026. Solar tariffs change, so confirm the current terms with your utility before you sign a contract. Our [solar payback guide](/posts/solar-payback-period-without-federal-tax-credit/) shows how the retail rate and the export credit change the math.

## What Happens Next

- Evergy Missouri Metro: a PSC decision before the January 6, 2027 suspension date; Evergy expects new rates in January 2027.
- Ameren Missouri: hearings and a decision ahead of mid-2027.

## Limitations

The Ameren figures in the pending case come from Ameren's June 26, 2026 filing and announcement; they are requests. We did not confirm the effective date of the 2025 Ameren increase.

## Compare With Nearby States

**Statewide average for context:** the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) puts Missouri's average residential price at 16.09¢/kWh in July 2026, up 3.4% from 15.56¢ in July 2025. The U.S. average was 18.31¢. At 1,000 kWh that works out to $160.90 (1,000 × 16.09¢). This is total residential revenue divided by kWh sold across every utility in the state, fixed charges included, and it is a preliminary estimate. It is not any one utility's rate.
