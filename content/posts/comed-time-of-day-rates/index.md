**ComEd's Delivery Time-of-Day rate makes the delivery part of your bill cheaper at most hours and more expensive from 1 p.m. to 7 p.m., every day of the year.** It saves money if you can keep afternoon use low: by our math, a single-family ComEd home without electric heat comes out ahead if less than about 38% of its kWh fall between 1 and 7 p.m. ComEd expects the average enrolled customer to save about $2.31 a month.

The rate only touches delivery charges. Your supply price, covered in our [ComEd and Ameren Illinois price update](/posts/illinois-electricity-rate-changes-2026/), stays the same unless you also pick a time-varying supply option. If you have solar, read the net metering section first: the answer is different for systems installed before 2025 and those on the newer net metering rules.

## What Is ComEd's Delivery Time-of-Day Rate?

ComEd announced Delivery Time-of-Day (DTOD) pricing on February 11, 2026. Under the standard rate, ComEd charges the same delivery price per kWh at every hour. DTOD splits the day into four periods:

| Period | Hours (every day) | Delivery price vs standard |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | 6 a.m. to 1 p.m. | Lower |
| Mid-day peak | 1 p.m. to 7 p.m. | Higher |
| Evening | 7 p.m. to 9 p.m. | Lower |
| Overnight | 9 p.m. to 6 a.m. | Lowest |

ComEd says the rate was developed under Illinois's Climate and Equitable Jobs Act. It is separate from ComEd's Hourly Pricing program, which varies the supply price every hour. A January 2025 Illinois Commerce Commission ruling in Docket No. 24-0378 set the stage for the delivery-only rate, according to the Citizens Utility Board (CUB), the state's utility consumer advocate.

## How Much Does ComEd Delivery Cost at Each Hour?

The rate you pay depends on your delivery class, which ComEd sets by home type and heating type. CUB's March 2026 fact sheet lists these per-kWh delivery prices:

| Delivery class | Standard | Morning | Mid-day peak | Evening | Overnight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single-family, non-electric heat | 6.228¢ | 4.009¢ | 10.712¢ | 3.747¢ | 2.984¢ |
| Multi-family, non-electric heat | 4.791¢ | 3.073¢ | 8.689¢ | 2.856¢ | 2.251¢ |
| Single-family, electric heat | 3.165¢ | 1.999¢ | 5.329¢ | 1.890¢ | 1.550¢ |
| Multi-family, electric heat | 2.996¢ | 1.925¢ | 4.975¢ | 1.823¢ | 1.512¢ |

These are CUB's figures, not a ComEd tariff sheet we could retrieve. ComEd's own release gives one number: traditional-rate customers will pay about 5.9 cents per kWh for delivery in 2026, an average across customers that does not match any single class above. To see your exact prices, ComEd points customers to the Rates Comparison tool in their online account.

## Will ComEd Time-of-Day Save Me Money?

Everything turns on how much of your electricity you use from 1 to 7 p.m. We worked out the break-even share for each delivery class, assuming the rest of your use is spread across the other 18 hours in proportion to their length (7 morning hours, 2 evening hours, 9 overnight hours):

| Delivery class | Break-even share of kWh used 1 to 7 p.m. |
|---|---|
| Single-family, non-electric heat | about 38% |
| Multi-family, non-electric heat | about 36% |
| Single-family, electric heat | about 39% |
| Multi-family, electric heat | about 39% |

Below that share, DTOD lowers your delivery charges. Above it, DTOD raises them. The 1 to 7 p.m. window is 6 of 24 hours (25%), so a home that used power evenly all day would save.

### Worked example: 800 kWh a month, single-family, no electric heat

Using CUB's rates, our math (per-kWh delivery charge only):

| How the 800 kWh are spread | Standard delivery | DTOD delivery | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evenly across all 24 hours (25% at peak) | $49.82 | $42.23 | about $7.60 less |
| 15% at peak, rest spread by hours | $49.82 | $36.43 | about $13.40 less |
| 40% at peak, rest spread by hours | $49.82 | $50.92 | about $1.10 more |

Standard: 800 × 6.228¢ = $49.82. Even spread: (233.3 kWh × 4.009¢) + (200 × 10.712¢) + (66.7 × 3.747¢) + (300 × 2.984¢) = $42.23.

Real homes are not flat, and many use more in the afternoon and early evening. ComEd's estimate of average savings, about $2.31 a month, covers all delivery classes (including the lower-rate electric-heat and multi-family classes) and real usage patterns, so it is smaller than this example.

### Who ComEd delivery time-of-day suits

CUB says the best candidates are households that can shift major appliance use, adjust the thermostat from 1 to 7 p.m. (higher in summer, lower in winter), or charge an electric vehicle overnight. EV owners get an extra $2 a month per registered vehicle, for up to two vehicles and 24 months, according to ComEd. If you cancel DTOD, you lose any remaining EV credits.

It is a poor fit if someone is home running air conditioning, cooking and laundry through the afternoon and cannot move that load.

## ComEd Time-of-Day and Solar: Legacy vs New Net Metering

How DTOD interacts with solar depends on your net metering status, explained in our [guide to Illinois net metering changes](/posts/illinois-net-metering-changes/).

**Solar connected before 2025 (legacy, full retail net metering).** CUB warns that these customers may lose access to a portion of their net metering credits if they use DTOD without a time-variant supply rate such as Hourly Pricing. CUB "does not recommend the specific arrangement of combining legacy net metering, Delivery Time-of-Day Pricing, and a fixed supply rate." If you are a legacy customer, run your numbers in ComEd's Rates Comparison tool before you switch.

**Solar on the newer supply-only net metering rules (generally systems connected since January 1, 2025).** Under Illinois law, these customers remain responsible for the gross amount of delivery charges, meaning delivery is billed on every kWh you pull from the grid, whatever you export. On DTOD, what matters is when those imports happen. Panels can cover part of a sunny afternoon's use, which may shrink your 1 to 7 p.m. imports, but production typically tapers off toward evening and is lower in winter. If your ComEd account shows hourly data, check when your grid imports happen and compare that share with the break-even figures above.

In principle, a home battery that charges from solar and discharges between 1 and 7 p.m. could cut peak imports further. See [how time-of-use rates change the value of solar](/posts/time-of-use-rates-and-solar/).

## Delivery Time-of-Day vs ComEd's Full Time-of-Day Rate

On July 23, 2026, ComEd announced a recently launched Time-of-Day pricing option in which both delivery and supply prices vary by time of day, using the same four periods. ComEd's release did not publish the supply prices, and says rates depend on home and heating type. CUB's fact sheet notes that delivery TOD alone does not change your supply options: you can keep ComEd's fixed supply price, use Hourly Pricing or buy from an alternative supplier.

## How to Enroll in ComEd Delivery Time-of-Day

1. Log in to your ComEd account and open the Rates Comparison tool to see your delivery class and DTOD prices.
2. Check your hourly usage for a summer and a winter month and estimate the share between 1 and 7 p.m.
3. If you have pre-2025 solar, read CUB's warning above before you enroll.
4. Sign up through ComEd. ComEd says there is no cost to enroll or cancel and the change can take two to three billing cycles.
