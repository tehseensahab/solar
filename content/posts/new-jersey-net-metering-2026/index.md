**Yes, New Jersey still has net metering in 2026. Utilities must credit you for the extra electricity your solar panels send to the grid (PSE&G, for example, banks one credit for each excess kilowatt-hour, or kWh), credits carry forward month to month, and any left at the end of your 12-month year are paid at the avoided cost of wholesale power.** State regulators have the legal power to change this for new customers, and they opened a review in November 2024. As of Nov. 21, 2026 we found no successor policy adopted.

This guide covers the rule today, the 5.8% trigger in state law, every dated step in the Board of Public Utilities (BPU) review, what each side proposed and what it could mean if you are about to sign a solar contract. For supply and delivery rates by utility, see [our 2026 guide to New Jersey utility rates](/rates/new-jersey/).

## How Does Net Metering Work in New Jersey Now?

New Jersey's net metering rule is N.J.A.C. 14:8-4.3. It applies to customers of every electric distribution company: PSE&G, JCP&L, Atlantic City Electric and Rockland Electric. In plain terms:

| Rule | What it says | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly credit | When you produce more than you use, "the EDC and supplier/provider shall credit the customer-generator for the excess." | Extra kWh you export reduce later bills. |
| Carry-forward | Utilities "shall carry over credit earned... from monthly billing period to monthly billing period." | Summer surplus can cover winter use. |
| Annual period | Utilities must offer each customer "one opportunity to select a monthly billing period as the start of" the annualized period. | You can pick when your 12-month year ends. |
| Year-end payout | Leftover excess kWh are paid "at the electric power supplier's or basic generation service provider's avoided cost of wholesale power." | Unused credits are worth less than credits used on your bill. |
| System size | Capacity may not exceed "the amount of electricity supplied... over an historical 12-month period that the customer-generator selects." | Size the system to your annual use, not more. |

PSE&G describes the same rule in kWh terms: "one credit is added to the bank for each excess kilowatt-hour (kWh)," and credits carried forward "are used to reduce the bill." At the end of the 12-month "contract year," PSE&G values leftover credits at "the market price from New Jersey's Clean Energy Program," a price that "does not include delivery and system charges." PSE&G also keeps separate peak and off-peak banks for customers on time-of-use rates: "'Peak' and 'Off Peak' energy banks cannot be combined."

**What it means:** at PSE&G, a banked credit reduces a later bill, while credits still left at the true-up get a market price that "does not include delivery and system charges." In other words, a credit used during the year is worth more than one paid out at year-end. That is why the best results come from a system sized close to your yearly use. Our [net metering true-up explainer](/posts/net-metering-true-up/) walks through the year-end math.

## What Is the 5.8% Net Metering Threshold in New Jersey?

The threshold is in state law, N.J.S.A. 48:3-87(e). The Clean Energy Act of 2018 (P.L. 2018, c. 17, approved May 23, 2018) raised it from 2.9% to 5.8%. The law says:

> "The board may authorize an electric power supplier or basic generation service provider to cease offering net metering to customers that are not already net metered whenever the total rated generating capacity owned and operated by net metering customer-generators Statewide equals 5.8 percent of the total annual kilowatt-hours sold in this State by each electric power supplier and each basic generation service provider during the prior one-year period."

Three things matter in that sentence:

1. **It is permissive.** The board "may" authorize a change. Nothing ends automatically when the threshold is reached.
2. **It is aimed at new customers.** It covers customers "that are not already net metered."
3. **It has been reached.** In its November 2024 notice, BPU staff wrote that "during Energy Year 2024 – which ended May 31, 2024 – the State exceeded 5.8 percent of the total annual kilowatt-hours sold in-State by net metering customer-generators." That is a staff calculation, not a board order.

## Where Does the BPU Net Metering Review Stand?

The review is Docket No. QO24090723, "Net Metering for Class I Renewable Energy Systems." Every dated step we could confirm:

| Date | Step | Source |
|---|---|---|

| Feb. 5, 2025 | Notice of a technical conference, with questions for stakeholders | BPU notice |

| March 3, 2025 | Deadline for written comments | BPU notice |
| May 21, 2026 | In an incentive order, the BPU says: "At this time, there are no new developments respecting net metering." | BPU order (ADI review) |

**Status: open, with no successor adopted.** We searched BPU notices, board orders and news coverage through Nov. 21, 2026. An absence of results is not a guarantee, so check the docket number with your installer or the BPU before you sign.

A bill reported by the Assembly Budget Committee on June 28, 2026 (A3974) would change several renewable incentive programs, but its text does not amend the 5.8% provision in N.J.S.A. 48:3-87 (some of its new provisions apply "notwithstanding" that section). Its last recorded action in the text we read was that June 28 committee report.

## What Did Utilities and Solar Groups Propose?

These are positions presented at the Feb. 10, 2025 technical conference. None has been adopted.

| Group | Position (their words) |
|---|---|
| Rate Counsel (ratepayer advocate) | Move to "a 'two-channel' or 'net billing' methodology" that would "exclusively utilize market-based generation rates"; reforms in place "by January 2026"; grandfathering "for a period not to exceed five years"; system caps of "50 kW for residential installations"; estimated net metering costs "could amount to over $280 million per year, statewide" |
| NJ Utilities Association | "Current net metering policies subsidize participating customers"; "Protect customers from higher bills with high levels of net metering" |
| Atlantic City Electric | Pay exports at "the locational and temporal wholesale market generation value"; set up a stakeholder working group |
| Rockland Electric | A "customer benefit contribution (CBC) charge" that "should cover the entire cost shift" and "can be phased in over time"; "Existing customers could be grandfathered" |
| NJ Solar Energy Coalition and SEIA | Keep excess generation "Net Meter Credited"; lost utility revenue "should not be recoverable from either the customer or system owner"; the 5.8% trigger "clearly does not represent an EDC cost 'cliff'" |
| Vote Solar | Keep net metering "at least for residential DERs"; "Transition should be gradual" |
| MSSIA (solar industry group) | "We should keep net metering, but create new alternatives" |

Rate Counsel's January 2026 target date has passed without a change.

**What it means:** the two main paths on the table are keeping today's kWh-for-kWh credit, possibly with a new charge for solar customers, or paying exports at a wholesale or market value. Illinois has already moved new systems to [supply-only export credits](/posts/illinois-net-metering-changes/). Under the second path, a kWh you export would be worth less than a kWh you use at home, which would make running appliances in sunny hours, and home batteries, more valuable.

## Will Existing Solar Systems Keep Net Metering?

Nobody can promise that yet. Here is what is and is not known:

- **Known:** the statute lets the BPU stop offering net metering only to "customers that are not already net metered." The BPU's notice described the review as an updated mechanism "for new customers."
- **Not decided:** in its February 2025 notice the BPU itself asked "How long existing NEM customers are allowed to remain on the existing NEM structure" and "What is the optimal method of transition." It has not answered either question.
- **Proposed:** Rate Counsel suggested grandfathering for up to five years. Rockland Electric said existing customers "could be grandfathered." Vote Solar asked for a gradual transition.

If you install while net metering is in place, the statute's wording is on your side, but the length of any protection is still open.

## How Much Solar Incentive Is Left in New Jersey?

New home systems that net meter qualify for the Administratively Determined Incentive (ADI), paid as SREC-IIs, each representing one megawatt-hour (MWh) of solar output.

- **Value:** the BPU's May 21, 2026 order reduced the residential net-metered SREC-II "from $85 per MWh to $77 per MWh" "for all registrations received on or after July 27, 2026." That is 7.7 cents for each kWh your system produces. A system that produces 10,000 kWh in a year would earn 10 SREC-IIs, or $770 at $77 each (an illustration, not an estimate for your home).
- **Capacity:** the same order sets the net-metered residential block at 300 MW for energy year 2027 (staff recommended raising it "from 250 MW to 300 MW in EY 2027"). Registrations are accepted until the block fills "or June 1, 2027, whichever occurs first."
- **How fast it fills:** in the prior year, the BPU said the residential segment was "on track to fully subscribe its allocated 250 MW of capacity well in advance of the end of EY26," with "229.8 MW" subscribed "as of March 30, 2026."

We did not find a published figure for how much of the 300 MW is left as of November 2026. Your installer registers the system and can tell you whether it has a confirmed registration and at which SREC-II value. The federal 25D tax credit for homeowner-owned systems is a separate question; our [solar payback guide without the federal credit](/posts/solar-payback-period-without-federal-tax-credit/) covers it.

## Three Questions to Ask Your Installer

1. **"What net metering terms does your savings estimate assume, and for how many years?"** If the proposal assumes full kWh credits for 25 years, ask what the savings look like if exports were paid a wholesale value after a transition period.
2. **"What is my system's size compared with my last 12 months of use?"** The rule caps the system at your historical use, and kWh left at the true-up are paid only avoided cost.
3. **"Is my ADI registration confirmed, and at $77 or $85 per SREC-II?"** Get the registration status and value in writing.

Our [guide to comparing solar quotes](/posts/how-to-compare-solar-quotes/) has a fuller checklist, and the [net metering state guide](/posts/net-metering-state-guide/) shows how other states have changed their rules.

## Who This Applies To

Residential customers of New Jersey's investor-owned electric utilities (PSE&G, JCP&L, Atlantic City Electric and Rockland Electric) with on-site solar or other Class I renewable systems. Municipal utilities and electric cooperatives may differ. Community solar and remote net metering have separate programs not covered here.
