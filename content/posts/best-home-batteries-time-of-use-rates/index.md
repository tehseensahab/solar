If you have solar and a time-of-use (TOU) rate, the best battery is the one that lets you **schedule** when it charges and discharges, delivers enough **continuous power** for your evening loads, and fits your utility's **export and interconnection rules**. The Tesla Powerwall 3, FranklinWH aPower 2 and Enphase IQ Battery 10C are the three we compare, using manufacturer documents and published analyses as of October 24, 2026. We did not test them. See [how we pick products](/how-we-pick/).

Before you buy for the spread alone, read the math below. In our worked example it is real but small.

## What a TOU battery earns

Our [time-of-use guide](/posts/time-of-use-rates-and-solar/) uses Idaho Power's 2026 optional TOU rate for on-site generation customers: a summer on-peak kWh costs 29.9185¢ and an off-peak kWh costs 7.4797¢. It also uses a midday export value of 3.4¢ per kWh for illustration. We rely on that guide for the tariff figures and did not re-pull the tariff today.

Formula (our math): value per stored kWh = (round-trip efficiency × on-peak price) − (price of the energy you stored).

Inputs: 90% round-trip efficiency (an assumption, not a manufacturer figure), 29.9185¢ on-peak, 3.4¢ for the solar kWh you give up exporting.

- 0.90 × 29.9185¢ = 26.93¢
- 26.93¢ − 3.4¢ = **23.53¢ per stored kWh**

Scale it (illustration): shifting 10 kWh a day on summer on-peak days. Idaho Power's summer period runs June 1 to September 30, Monday to Saturday except holidays. By our count that is about 103 days in 2026 (122 days, minus 17 Sundays, minus July 4 and Labor Day).

- 10 kWh × $0.2353 = $2.35 a day
- $2.35 × 103 days = about **$242 for the summer**

That ignores the rest of the year, battery degradation and any demand charges. Against installed prices in the five figures (see below), TOU arbitrage alone is a slow payback, which is why we do not pitch a battery as a bill-saver by itself. See our [payback guide](/posts/solar-payback-period-without-federal-tax-credit/) for how to build your own estimate.

## What to look for on a TOU rate

### Schedule and mode control
You need to tell the battery when to charge and when to hold energy for the peak. We did not verify each manufacturer's TOU scheduling features in the documents we read, so ask your installer to show the app or controller settings for your exact tariff before you sign.

### Enough kW for the evening
Peak windows are when ovens, dryers, air conditioners and EV chargers run together. If continuous output is lower than your simultaneous load, the grid covers the difference at peak prices. Compare the continuous kW above to your own load.

### Enough usable kWh for the window
Idaho Power's summer on-peak runs 7 p.m. to 11 p.m., four hours. Hours covered = usable kWh ÷ average kW. A 2.5 kW evening load over four hours needs 10 kWh.

### Export rules
Some utilities pay different export credits by hour. Hawaiian Electric's credits in our TOU guide are 13.5¢ for daytime exports on Oahu and 32.9¢ from 5 p.m. to 9 p.m. Where evening exports pay well, discharging to the grid in the peak can beat self-use. Check your tariff and see [export credit rates by utility](/posts/solar-export-credit-rates-by-utility-2026/) and [net metering by state](/posts/net-metering-state-guide/).

### Round-trip efficiency
Our example assumes 90%. We did not find a round-trip efficiency figure in the manufacturer documents we read for all three batteries, so treat 90% as an assumption and ask for the datasheet figure.

## Who should skip a battery for TOU savings

- Your peak and off-peak prices are close.
- Your peak window is short and your evening load is small.
- You already shift loads (laundry, dishwasher, EV charging) into cheap hours.
- Your utility is switching to a flat or net-billing structure that changes the value; check the latest rules.

## Incentives

The IRS says the [residential clean energy credit](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/residential-clean-energy-credit) is not available for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. Utility demand-response or battery programs may still pay for discharge during grid events; ask your utility. Use [Rates by State](/rates-by-state/) to find your state's page.

## Buying checklist

1. Get your tariff's hourly prices and your own hourly use from the utility.
2. Estimate shiftable kWh per day by season.
3. Confirm the battery can follow your schedule and your utility allows it.
4. Compare continuous kW and usable kWh to your evening load.
5. Ask for datasheet round-trip efficiency and warranty terms.
6. Get three installed quotes and compute payback with your inputs.

## Limitations

The Idaho example is an illustration, not a forecast; tariffs change, and our efficiency figure is an assumption. We did not verify each battery's TOU features. This is not financial or tax advice.
