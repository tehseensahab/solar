**Duke Energy's 2027 rate increase in North Carolina is approved.** The North Carolina Utilities Commission (NCUC) approved settlements in both Duke rate cases in written orders dated November 16, 2026, and Duke says it expects the new rates to start January 1, 2027. Duke projects that a home using 1,000 kWh a month will pay $9.62 more at Duke Energy Progress and $6.53 more at Duke Energy Carolinas, with a second, smaller step on January 1, 2028.

The approval settles what was still pending when we last updated our [guide to North Carolina electric rates and solar](/posts/north-carolina-electricity-rate-changes-2026/). This article covers residential customers of Duke Energy Carolinas (DEC, Docket E-7, Sub 1329) and Duke Energy Progress (DEP, Docket E-2, Sub 1380). It does not cover Dominion Energy North Carolina, electric cooperatives or city-owned utilities.

## Is the Duke Energy Rate Increase Approved?

Yes. In an SEC filing dated November 16, 2026, Duke said the commission "issued written orders (the "Orders") approving the Comprehensive Revenue Requirement Settlements" in both cases. The settlements were filed on July 17 (DEC) and August 5, 2026 (DEP).

Duke's filing adds: "There are no material changes to the Comprehensive Settlements as a result of the Orders." It also says: "The Companies expect to implement revised customer rates effective January 1, 2027."

We did not find the commission's order text or a commission press release. The figures below come from Duke's SEC filing, its fact sheets filed with it, and its November 17 news release.

## How Much Will a 1,000 kWh Bill Go Up?

These are Duke's projections for "a typical residential customer using 1,000 kilowatt-hours per month," from its November 17, 2026 release.

| Utility | Jan. 1, 2027 | Jan. 1, 2028 | Two steps combined |
|---|---|---|---|
| Duke Energy Progress | +$9.62 a month | +$5.89 a month | +$15.51 a month |
| Duke Energy Carolinas | +$6.53 a month | +$4.66 a month | +$11.19 a month |

The combined column is our addition ($9.62 + $5.89; $6.53 + $4.66). Duke's release does not say which charges these projections include. Fuel and other riders are set in separate yearly proceedings and can move your bill up or down.

To scale the estimate to your own use, divide by 1,000 and multiply by your monthly kWh. At 1,300 kWh, for example, the DEP 2027 step would be roughly $9.62 × 1.3 = $12.51 a month. That is a rough guide only, because customer charges do not scale with usage.

### Why the Duke Energy Carolinas figure changed

When the DEC settlement was announced in July 2026, the reported 1,000 kWh increase was $9.39 in 2027 and $5.52 in 2028. Duke's November 17 release gives $6.53 and $4.66 instead. The percentages, though, still line up with the July settlement: Duke now describes the DEC residential increase as 4.8% a year on average, close to the average of the settlement's 5.9% and 3.6% steps, and NC Newsline reported about 9.5% over two years. Duke's release does not explain why the dollar figures are lower. Until the final rate sheets are filed, treat the DEC dollar amounts as Duke's estimate, not your bill.

For DEP, Duke's projections, $9.62 and $5.89, match those reported for the August settlement.

## How Much Did Duke Ask For, and What Was Approved?

Duke filed both cases on November 20, 2025. The fact sheets Duke filed with the SEC show the commission approved less than half of the original request.

| | Duke Energy Carolinas | Duke Energy Progress |
|---|---|---|
| Original request (two years) | About $1,002 million (about 15.0%) | $729 million (about 15.1%) |
| Approved, first step | $286 million | $185 million |
| Approved, second step | $210 million | $153 million |
| Approved, two years | $496 million | $338 million |
| Average yearly increase, all customers | 3.7% | About 3.4% |
| Average yearly increase, residential | 4.8% | 4.7% |
| Return on equity: asked / approved | 10.95% (cut to 10.48%) / 9.8% | 10.95% (cut to 10.48%) / 9.8% |

Together, the approved increases total $834 million over two years, against about $1,731 million first requested ($1,002 million + $729 million), or about 48%. Both fact sheets say the revenue figures are "subject to finalization with Public Staff" based on the order, so the final numbers can shift in compliance filings. The approved return on equity is based on a 53% equity share of the capital structure.

## What Else Is in the Orders?

- **Bill help:** Duke says the orders include "$20 million in new funding from Duke Energy shareholders to help customers in need," $10 million for each utility, according to the fact sheets.
- **Data centers:** Duke says the orders establish "a new rate schedule for data center customers with loads above 50 megawatts." The fact sheets list minimum 15-year contracts for data centers above 100 MW, a minimum billing demand of 75% of contract demand, and a stranded generation charge whose method is still to be set.
- **Tax credits (DEP):** the DEP fact sheet lists a federal tax credit return to customers of $120 million a year for 2027 and 2028.
- **One company, two rate sets:** WBTV reported that Duke Energy Progress and Duke Energy Carolinas become one company on January 1, 2027, but that their rates stay separate for now.

## What This Means for Homeowners With Solar

A higher per-kWh price makes each kWh your panels produce and you use at home worth a little more, because it replaces power you would otherwise buy at the new rate. Fixed customer charges and any minimum bill are not reduced by solar, and power you export is generally credited at much less than the retail rate under North Carolina's newer solar rates.

The bigger date for many existing solar owners is the same one: older net metering riders are scheduled to end on January 1, 2027, under the commission's 2023 order. Our explainer on [what changes for Duke Energy net metering in North Carolina on Jan. 1, 2027](/posts/duke-energy-net-metering-nc-2027/) covers the options. To see how the rate and the export rule change the payback, use our [solar payback guide](/posts/solar-payback-period-without-federal-tax-credit/).

## What to Check on Your Bill

1. **Your utility.** DEC and DEP have different rates and different increases.
2. **Your usage.** Duke's projections are for a typical residential customer using 1,000 kWh a month.
3. **The basic customer charge** on your January 2027 bill. We did not find the new customer charge in the sources we read.
4. **Rider lines,** especially fuel, which change in separate proceedings. Our guide to [reading your electric bill](/posts/how-to-read-your-electric-bill/) explains each line.
