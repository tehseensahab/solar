**We Energies is asking Wisconsin regulators to raise electric rates in 2027 and again in 2028. Its customer notice puts the 2027 increase at $14.67 a month for a home using 660 kWh, then another $8.69 in 2028.** Nothing is approved. The Public Service Commission of Wisconsin (PSC) is expected to decide late this year, with any new rates starting in January.

This would follow increases the PSC estimated at $7.62 a month in 2025 and $9.73 in 2026 for an average residential customer. For every major Wisconsin utility's approved and pending changes, see our [Wisconsin electricity rates and solar guide](/rates/wisconsin/).

## What We Energies Is Asking For

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Utility | Wisconsin Electric Power Company, doing business as We Energies |
| Case | Docket 5-UR-112, before the PSC |
| Status | Requested, not approved |
| 2027 electric revenue | $175.8 million (4.7%), including changes in fuel costs |
| 2028 electric revenue | $179.6 million (4.5%), not including fuel costs, which will be filed separately |
| Residential, 2027 | +$14.67 a month (10.28%) at 660 kWh, or +$13.35 with 2026 fuel costs |
| Residential, 2028 | +$8.69 a month (5.52%), excluding fuel costs, to be filed separately |
| Expected start | January 2027, if approved |

Source: We Energies' customer notice of the requested rates and its April 1, 2026 filing announcement.

The residential percentages are higher than the overall ones. The 4.7% and 4.5% figures cover all of We Energies' electric revenue, while the 10.28% and 5.52% figures are the notice's estimates for residential customers only.

The announcement described the 2027 change as "about $13 per month." The customer notice gives the more precise numbers above. The difference is fuel: the notice says $14.67 leaves out the current fuel cost adjustment, while $13.35 factors in 2026 fuel costs. Measured against today's full bill, $13.35 is the closer figure.

We Energies also asked for natural gas increases in the same case. This article covers electric rates only.

## What the Money Would Pay For

In its announcement, We Energies tied the request to underground power lines (60 to 80 miles of cable in each of 2027 and 2028), tree trimming, replacing aging equipment, and clean energy projects regulators have already approved. It said the filing includes "more than $225 million in savings from federal tax credits and earnings sharing."

The company also said data centers would pay nearly $1.9 billion in 2027 and 2028 to serve them, and that "Data center costs will not be passed on to other customers." That is the company's position. The PSC decides how costs are allocated among customer classes.

## What Consumer Advocates Want

The Citizens Utility Board (CUB), which represents residential customers, has opposed the size of the increase. In an August 25, 2026 update, CUB said its proposals on utility profits would "pare back the increases by more than $200 million" over two years. It asked the PSC to set the return on equity, the profit rate regulators allow on the utility's investments, at 9.1%. CUB also asked regulators to deny $119 million in profits tied to retired coal plants.

In the last case, the PSC approved less than We Energies requested. Wisconsin Public Radio reported in November 2024 that the commission granted an overall electric increase of 8.79% over two years, about $188 million less than requested, and held the return on equity at 9.8% instead of the 10% the company sought. That shows what the PSC did last time, not what it will do this time.

## What It Means for Your Bill

Here is what a home using 660 kWh pays under the standard residential rate (Rg1) in effect since January 1, 2026, for a 30-day month. It leaves out taxes and other charges on the bill.

| Charge | Rate | 660 kWh, 30 days |
|---|---|---|
| Customer charge | $0.49315 a day | $14.79 |
| Energy charge | $0.19342 per kWh | $127.66 |
| Fuel cost adjustment | $0.00199 per kWh | $1.31 |
| Environmental control charge | $0.00052 per kWh | $0.34 |
| **Total** | | **$144.11** |

That is close to the $144 typical bill CUB uses for the same 660 kWh. If the full request were approved:

- **2027:** about $157.46 a month ($144.11 + $13.35, the notice's figure with 2026 fuel costs), or about $160 more over the year.
- **2028:** about $166.15 a month, about $22 above today. The 2028 request does not include fuel costs, which will be filed separately.

These are illustrations based on the company's own estimates for a 660 kWh home. Homes that use more electricity would generally see larger dollar changes. The exact effect depends on how the PSC splits any approved increase between the daily customer charge and the per-kWh charges.

**This fall's credit.** Separately, We Energies said on October 8, 2026 that it is returning more than $32 million because 2025 fuel costs came in below the amount the PSC approved. Residential customers get about 2 cents per kWh of October use, about $13.60 for a 660 kWh home, on an October or November bill. The credit is separate from the rate request.

Before the decision:

1. **Check your usage.** Your bill shows monthly kWh. Compare it with the 660 kWh used in the estimates.
2. **Look at time-of-use.** We Energies' Rg2 rate charges $0.30084 per kWh on-peak and $0.10028 off-peak, with a choice of four 12-hour on-peak windows. It tends to save money only if you can move much of your use outside your on-peak window.
3. **Ask about help early.** If winter bills are already hard to cover, see our guide to [LIHEAP and other bill help](/posts/liheap-energy-assistance-2026-27/).

## What It Means for Solar Owners

Each kWh of solar you use in your home avoids the per-kWh charges: $0.19593 combined under today's Rg1 rate ($0.19342 energy, $0.00199 fuel and $0.00052 environmental). A system that covers 500 kWh of your own use a month avoids about $98 of those charges at current rates (500 × $0.19593). Solar does not reduce the $0.49315 daily customer charge.

If the PSC puts most of the increase into per-kWh charges, self-used solar is worth more. If more of it goes into the daily charge, solar is worth less. Credits for power you send back to the grid work differently from power you use yourself. Our [Wisconsin guide](/rates/wisconsin/) summarizes each utility's surplus credit, and our explainer on [fixed charges and solar savings](/posts/fixed-charges-and-solar-savings/) shows why the split matters.

## What Happens Next

According to CUB's case calendar, public hearings were held in September and legal briefs were due by early October. The PSC is likely to vote at an open meeting in November and issue a written order in December. We Energies said in April it expected a decision "later this year," with new rates in January 2027. We will update this article when the PSC votes.
