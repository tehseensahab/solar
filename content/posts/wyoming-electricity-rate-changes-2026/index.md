Wyoming did not have a rate change on October 1, 2026. Rocky Mountain Power, the state's largest utility, lowered one charge on July 1 and has a general increase pending for 2027.

This article covers Rocky Mountain Power and Black Hills Energy's northeast Wyoming system. It does not cover Cheyenne Light, Fuel and Power or the cooperatives.

## Quick Summary

| Item | Utility | Date | Residential impact | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Energy cost adjustment | Rocky Mountain Power | July 1, 2026 | About −$4.50 a month at 700 kWh | In effect |
| General rate case | Rocky Mountain Power | 2027 | 8.8% requested; 3.9% net for residential | Pending |
| Rate case | Black Hills Energy (Upton, Newcastle) | Disputed start date | About +$32 a month (32%) | Hearing Oct. 7 and 8, 2026 |

## Rocky Mountain Power

**The request.** In May 2026 the company asked the Wyoming Public Service Commission (PSC) for $70.5 million, an 8.8% general rate increase, for 2027. The PSC review takes about 10 months.

**The offset.** A separate filing lowered the energy cost adjustment by 6% on July 1, 2026. For a home using 700 kWh, that is about $4.50 less a month. The company counts that decrease when it describes the net impact as 2.8% overall and 3.9% for residential customers.

**Recent history.** Cowboy State Daily reported:

- **2023:** a 29.2% request, settled at 16.2%.
- **2025:** 10.2% approved, against a 14.7% proposal.

The company said none of its requested increases are related to data centers, and that Wyoming's large-load tariff requires those customers to pay for their own infrastructure.

## Black Hills Energy, Northeast Wyoming

Black Hills Energy serves about 2,600 customers in Upton and Newcastle. It has asked for $5.1 million a year, a 32% increase, about $32 a month for a residential customer (Docket 20002-148-ER-26). It is the system's first base rate request since 2014.

- **Consumer advocate's position:** $3.6 million, and a lower rate of return (7.51% against the 8.13% requested).
- **Effective date:** the company wants rates applied back to May 1, 2026. The consumer advocate recommends February 1, 2027.
- **Hearing:** October 7 and 8, 2026.

## What This Means for Homeowners With Solar

Wyoming has net metering with kWh credits during the year and a low cash price for what is left over.

- **Size limit:** 25 kW for residential systems.
- **Credits:** surplus earns kWh credits that offset later use during the calendar year, according to EnergySage.
- **Year end:** remaining credits are sold to Rocky Mountain Power at its Schedule 37 avoided-cost rate, about 3¢ per kWh.

**What it means:** within the year, each solar kWh offsets a retail kWh. Only production beyond your annual use falls to about 3¢. A system sized to your yearly consumption keeps nearly all of its value, and the 2025 increase and the pending 2027 request both raise that value.

Rules checked October 5, 2026. Solar tariffs change, so confirm the current terms with your utility before you sign a contract. Our [solar payback guide](/posts/solar-payback-period-without-federal-tax-credit/) shows how the retail rate and the export credit change the math.

## Limitations

## Compare With Nearby States

**Statewide average for context:** the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) puts Wyoming's average residential price at 14.36¢/kWh in July 2026, down 1.9% from 14.64¢ in July 2025. The U.S. average was 18.31¢. At 1,000 kWh that works out to $143.60 (1,000 × 14.36¢). This is total residential revenue divided by kWh sold across every utility in the state, fixed charges included, and it is a preliminary estimate. It is not any one utility's rate.
