**FirstEnergy's three Ohio utilities want to raise distribution rates each year from 2027 to 2029. For a typical home using about 1,000 kWh a month that does not shop for supply, the company estimates $4.26 more a month each year at Ohio Edison, $5.15 at The Illuminating Company (CEI) and $5.30 at Toledo Edison.** By the third year, that adds up to $12.78 to $15.90 a month more than today, according to the Office of the Ohio Consumers' Counsel (OCC). Nothing is approved. The Public Utilities Commission of Ohio (PUCO) scheduled public hearings for Dec. 2, 9 and 17, 2026.

The case covers only distribution, the part of the bill that pays for local poles, wires and tree trimming. It does not change the default supply price that was reset on June 1; for those numbers and how Ohio net metering credits work, see [what changed for Ohio electric customers in 2026](/posts/ohio-electricity-rate-changes-2026/).

## What FirstEnergy Is Asking For

FirstEnergy filed the plan on May 22, 2026, PUCO says, in Case No. 26-347-EL-AIR (with companion cases 26-348-EL-ATA, 26-349-EL-AAM and 26-350-EL-UNC). The company calls it its first "Three-Year Rate Plan."

| Utility | Year 1 revenue increase | Year 2 | Year 3 | Typical home, each year (FirstEnergy) | By year 3 (OCC) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ohio Edison | $99.8 million | $27.8 million | $36.2 million | +$4.26/month (2.2%) | +$12.78/month |
| The Illuminating Company (CEI) | $121.4 million | $17.0 million | $26.1 million | +$5.15/month (2.6%) | +$15.45/month |
| Toledo Edison | $32.8 million | $14.0 million | $17.1 million | +$5.30/month (2.8%) | +$15.90/month |

Revenue figures: PUCO notice of Nov. 6, 2026. Monthly figures: FirstEnergy, for "a typical non-shopping residential customer using about 1,000 kilowatt-hours per month," with the percentage described as an average annual increase; OCC's year-three totals cite FirstEnergy's April 22, 2026 E-5 filing. All figures are **requested**, not approved. PUCO's notice lists an amount for each year but does not say whether the year-two and year-three amounts are added on top of year one; the three year-one amounts total $254 million. PUCO's year-one amounts are much larger than years two and three, while FirstEnergy describes an equal monthly bill increase each year; the materials we reviewed do not explain the difference.

FirstEnergy says the plan supports an average of $800 million a year in investment in infrastructure upgrades, "including neighborhood poles, wires" and related equipment, plus an average of $83 million a year for vegetation management (tree trimming). The company also lists a $1 million Emergency Energy Support Fund and a new $4 million Energy Assistance Fund in 2029, and says those program costs are not passed on to customers.

## What It Means in Dollars

Assumptions: FirstEnergy's own estimates for a non-shopping home using about 1,000 kWh a month; the same increase each year, as FirstEnergy and OCC describe it.

| Utility | More per month, year 1 | More per year, year 1 | More per month by year 3 | More per year by year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ohio Edison | $4.26 | $51.12 | $12.78 | $153.36 |
| CEI | $5.15 | $61.80 | $15.45 | $185.40 |
| Toledo Edison | $5.30 | $63.60 | $15.90 | $190.80 |

If you use more or less than 1,000 kWh, your change will differ, and the filing may shift charges between the fixed monthly customer charge and per-kWh charges; FirstEnergy's announcement does not break that out. If you buy supply from a retail supplier, your total bill and the percentage change will look different, because the estimates assume default (non-shopping) service.

OCC also notes the increases would come "in addition to other rising electricity costs including generation and transmission charges."

## What It Means for Solar Homes

Solar does not take you off the distribution system. A home with panels still pays the customer charge and distribution charges on the power it draws from the grid at night and on cloudy days, so it would share in any approved increase.

Ohio net metering credits exported power at the energy part of the supply rate only, as our Ohio page explains, so a distribution increase does not raise what you earn for exports. It does make the power your panels cover directly a little more valuable, to the extent distribution is billed per kWh. That favors sizing a system and timing loads (dishwasher, laundry, EV charging) to use more of your own solar output. For a broader look at how fixed and per-kWh charges change solar savings, see [fixed charges and solar savings](/posts/fixed-charges-and-solar-savings/).

For context, Duke Energy Ohio customers in southwest Ohio are in a separate case; see our [Duke Energy Ohio rate increase coverage](/posts/duke-energy-ohio-rate-increase-2026/).
