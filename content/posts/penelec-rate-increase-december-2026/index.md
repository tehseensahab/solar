**Penelec's supply price for customers on default service rises 3.2% on Dec. 1, 2026, from 13.142 to 13.564 cents per kWh. For a home using 1,000 kWh a month, that adds about $4.22 a month to the supply part of the bill.** Met-Ed, Penn Power and West Penn Power, the other FirstEnergy Pennsylvania rate districts, rise by 2.6% to 3.5% on the same day. Customers who buy electricity from a competitive supplier are not affected by this change; they pay their contract price.

The December change is modest next to Penelec's June 1 jump of almost 12%, which our [utility-by-utility look at Pennsylvania's 2026 electricity prices](/rates/pennsylvania/) covers along with what it means for solar.

## What Is the New Penelec Price to Compare?

The Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission (PUC) published its Dec. 1 price table in a release dated Nov. 23, 2026. It lists Penelec's residential Price to Compare at 13.142 cents per kWh now and 13.564 cents from Dec. 1, a change of +3.2%. FirstEnergy's posted Penelec calculation covers the "Default Service Period December 1, 2026 through May 31, 2027."

| Item | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Penelec residential Price to Compare, June 1 to Nov. 30, 2026 | 13.142 ¢/kWh | PUC; FirstEnergy tariff supplement |
| Penelec residential Price to Compare from Dec. 1, 2026 | 13.564 ¢/kWh | PUC, Nov. 23, 2026 |
| Change | +0.422 ¢/kWh (+3.2%) | PUC; our arithmetic |
| Same season last year (from Dec. 1, 2025) | 11.747 ¢/kWh | PUC, Nov. 24, 2025 |
| Change vs. last winter | +1.817 ¢/kWh (+15.5%) | Our arithmetic |

The Price to Compare is a per-kWh supply price. Penelec's bill insert describes it as "the price per kilowatthour (kWh) you pay for electric generation and transmission" and adds: "It does not include the other costs on your monthly bill." Your distribution (delivery) charges, customer charge and taxes are separate and are not part of this change.

## How Much More Will Penelec Customers Pay?

Assumptions: Penelec residential default service; supply price up 0.422 cents per kWh on Dec. 1, 2026; usage the same each month. Delivery charges and taxes are not included.

| Monthly use | Extra supply cost per month | Over Dec. 2026 to May 2027 (6 months) |
|---|---|---|
| 500 kWh | $2.11 | $12.66 |
| 1,000 kWh | $4.22 | $25.32 |
| 1,500 kWh | $6.33 | $37.98 |

The bigger story is the year-over-year change. A Penelec default-service home using 1,000 kWh pays about $18.17 a month more for supply this winter than at the same usage last winter (13.564 vs. 11.747 cents per kWh), most of it from the June 1 reset. As the PUC's release notes, "During the winter heating months, the cost of electricity can account for more than half of a typical customer bill."

All four FirstEnergy Pennsylvania rate districts reset their default supply prices on Dec. 1. Figures are from the PUC's Nov. 23 table; the dollar column is our arithmetic.

## Who Is Affected?

Only customers on default service. As the PUC's release puts it, "These changes only affect customers receiving 'default service' from their electric utility — that is, those who have not selected an EGS" (an electric generation supplier). If you have a supplier contract, your price is set by that contract. Check its end date: if it expires and you return to default service, you would pay the Price to Compare.

Penelec says to look in the "Messages" section of your bill for your current price to compare.

## Should You Shop for a Supplier?

Penelec's insert says customers can use the price to compare "to compare prices from different electric suppliers," and points to the PUC's shopping site, PAPowerSwitch.com. The PUC cautions this year that "current wholesale market trends have resulted in fewer supplier offers below the utility rate than in prior years." Before you sign, check:

1. The price per kWh against 13.564 cents (Penelec) or your district's figure above.
2. Whether the price is fixed or variable, and for how long.
3. Any monthly fee or early cancellation fee.
4. What the price becomes when the term ends.

Our [guide to comparing supplier offers](/posts/how-to-compare-electricity-supplier-offers/) walks through each of these. A fixed price that beats the Dec. 1 Price to Compare may not beat the next one; FirstEnergy's posted calculation runs the current default service period through May 31, 2027.

If a higher winter bill is the concern, the PUC urges households to "call utilities now" to learn about assistance programs and other affordability options. Budget billing spreads costs evenly across the year; see [how budget billing works](/posts/budget-billing-explained/).

## What This Means for Homeowners With Solar

For a Penelec default-service customer, each kWh your panels produce and you use at home avoids buying a kWh of supply at 13.564 cents, plus the per-kWh delivery charge. The higher the Price to Compare, the more that self-use is worth. Winter output is lower, though, so many solar homes buy more from the grid between December and February. How exported power is credited depends on Penelec's net metering rules and, if you shop, your supplier's terms; check both before assuming a credit value.

## Limitations

Bill estimates cover the supply portion only and exclude taxes, delivery charges and any change in your usage. FirstEnergy labels its posted Penelec price-to-compare calculation illustrative; we rely on the PUC's table for the Dec. 1 prices.
