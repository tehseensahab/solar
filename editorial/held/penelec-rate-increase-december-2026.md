**Penelec's supply price for customers on default service rises 3.2% on Dec. 1, 2026, from 13.142 to 13.564 cents per kWh. For a home using 1,000 kWh a month, that adds about $4.22 a month to the supply part of the bill.** The new Price to Compare runs through May 31, 2027. Met-Ed, Penn Power and West Penn Power, the other FirstEnergy Pennsylvania rate districts, rise by 2.6% to 3.5% on the same day.

The December change is modest next to Penelec's June 1 jump of almost 12%, which our [guide to Pennsylvania electricity prices and solar](/posts/pennsylvania-electricity-rate-changes-2026/) covers utility by utility. Customers who buy electricity from a competitive supplier are not affected by this change; they pay their contract price.

## What Is the New Penelec Price to Compare?

The Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission (PUC) issued its Dec. 1 price table in a Nov. 23, 2026 release, republished in full by PennWatch; we could not find the release on the PUC's own website as of Nov. 24. FirstEnergy's posted Penelec calculation "for the Default Service Period December 1, 2026 through May 31, 2027" shows a default rate of $0.13564 per kWh, matching the PUC figure. FirstEnergy labels that calculation illustrative.

| Item | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Penelec residential Price to Compare, June 1 to Nov. 30, 2026 | 13.142 ¢/kWh | FirstEnergy tariff supplement filed with the PUC |
| Penelec residential Price to Compare, Dec. 1, 2026 to May 31, 2027 | 13.564 ¢/kWh | PUC table (via PennWatch); FirstEnergy calculation |
| Change | +0.422 ¢/kWh (+3.2%) | Our arithmetic |
| Same season last year (from Dec. 1, 2025) | 11.747 ¢/kWh | Pennsylvania PUC |
| Change vs. last winter | +1.817 ¢/kWh (+15.5%) | Our arithmetic |

The Price to Compare is a per-kWh supply price. Penelec's bill insert describes it as "the price per kilowatthour (kWh) you pay for electric generation and transmission" and adds: "It does not include the other costs on your monthly bill." Your distribution (delivery) charges, customer charge and taxes are separate and are not part of this change.

## How Much More Will Penelec Customers Pay?

Assumptions: Penelec residential default service; supply price up 0.422 cents per kWh on Dec. 1, 2026; usage the same each month. Delivery charges and taxes are not included.

| Monthly use | Extra supply cost per month | Over Dec. 2026 to May 2027 (6 months) |
|---|---|---|
| 500 kWh | $2.11 | $12.66 |
| 1,000 kWh | $4.22 | $25.32 |
| 1,500 kWh | $6.33 | $37.98 |

The bigger story is the year-over-year change. A Penelec default-service home using 1,000 kWh pays about $18.17 a month more for supply this winter than at the same usage last winter (13.564 vs. 11.747 cents per kWh), most of it from the June 1 reset. In winter, the PUC's release notes, "the cost of electricity can account for more than half of a typical customer bill."

## Met-Ed, Penn Power and West Penn Power on Dec. 1

All four FirstEnergy Pennsylvania rate districts reset their default supply prices on Dec. 1. The previous prices match FirstEnergy's June 1, 2026 tariff supplement; the new prices are from the PUC table as republished by PennWatch.

| Rate district | June 1 to Nov. 30, 2026 | Dec. 1, 2026 to May 31, 2027 | Change | Extra per month at 1,000 kWh |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Penelec | 13.142 ¢/kWh | 13.564 ¢/kWh | +3.2% | $4.22 |
| Met-Ed | 13.951 ¢/kWh | 14.442 ¢/kWh | +3.5% | $4.91 |
| Penn Power | 13.477 ¢/kWh | 13.893 ¢/kWh | +3.1% | $4.16 |
| West Penn Power | 12.075 ¢/kWh | 12.386 ¢/kWh | +2.6% | $3.11 |

Other Pennsylvania utilities rise more. In the same PUC table, PECO goes from 11.759 to 12.965 cents (+10.3%) and PPL from 13.079 to 14.428 cents (+10.3%); our [PPL Dec. 1 article](/posts/ppl-rate-increase-december-2026/) has PPL's bill math.

## Who Is Affected?

Only customers on default service. As the PUC's release puts it, "These changes only affect customers receiving 'default service' from their electric utility — that is, those who have not selected an EGS" (an electric generation supplier). If you have a supplier contract, your price is set by that contract. Check its end date: if it expires and you return to default service, you would pay the Price to Compare.

Penelec says to look in the "Messages" section of your bill each month for your current price to compare.

## Should You Shop for a Supplier?

Penelec's insert says customers can use the price to compare "to compare prices from different electric suppliers," and points to the PUC's shopping site, PAPowerSwitch.com. Before you sign, check:

1. The price per kWh against 13.564 cents (Penelec) or your district's figure above.
2. Whether the price is fixed or variable, and for how long.
3. Any monthly fee or early cancellation fee.
4. What the price becomes when the term ends.

Our [guide to comparing supplier offers](/posts/how-to-compare-electricity-supplier-offers/) walks through each of these. A fixed price that beats today's Price to Compare may not beat the next one: the Price to Compare resets again on June 1, 2027.

If a higher winter bill is the concern, the PUC also urges customers to "call utilities now" to ask about payment and assistance programs. Budget billing spreads costs evenly across the year; see [how budget billing works](/posts/budget-billing-explained/).

## What This Means for Homeowners With Solar

For a Penelec default-service customer, each kWh your panels produce and you use at home avoids buying a kWh of supply at 13.564 cents, plus the per-kWh delivery charge. The higher the Price to Compare, the more that self-use is worth. Winter output is lower, though, so most solar homes still buy more from the grid between December and February. How exported power is credited depends on Penelec's net metering rules and, if you shop, your supplier's terms; check both before assuming a credit value.

## Limitations

The Dec. 1 figures come from the PUC's Nov. 23 release as republished by PennWatch; we did not find the release on the PUC website by Nov. 24. FirstEnergy's own calculation confirms the Penelec figure but is labeled illustrative, and we could not open the Met-Ed, Penn Power and West Penn calculations. Bill estimates cover the supply portion only and exclude taxes and delivery charges.
