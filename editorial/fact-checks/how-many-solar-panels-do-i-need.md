# Fact-check: how-many-solar-panels-do-i-need

Checked October 30, 2026. Byline Craig Leslie. Independent fact-check by a separate agent (re-fetched NREL ATB 2023 residential PV and the LBNL 2026 data update); findings applied. Counts: 11 verified, 9 qualified, 1 not verified (rewritten).
- EIA FAQ: 2022 average "10,791 kilowatthours (kWh)", "about 899 kWh per month"; Louisiana 14,774; Hawaii 6,178; purchases can understate use in solar homes. VERIFIED.
- NREL ATB 2023 residential PV (re-fetched): first-year CF "12.7% for Class 10 ... to 19.6% for Class 1"; units kWDC; "7.9-kWDC, fixed tilt, roof-mounted system"; 15.7% = simple average of the 10 classes (not population-weighted). VERIFIED; average QUALIFIED as unweighted.
- LBNL 2026 data update (re-fetched): "The median U.S. residential system size was 7.7 kW in 2025"; document units are DC unless noted. VERIFIED.
- Qcells Q.TRON BLK M-G2+ 430 W, 1,722 x 1,134 mm: Greentech Renewables listing. QUALIFIED (distributor; manufacturer datasheet fetch timed out).
- MPSC DG page: up to 110% of prior 12 months' consumption. VERIFIED.
- Arithmetic recomputed: 1,717 / 1,375 / 1,113 kWh per kW; 6.28 / 7.85 / 9.70 kW; 15 / 19 / 23 panels (6.45 / 8.17 / 9.89 kW actual); outputs 11,074 / 11,236 / 11,003 kWh; 7.7 kW = 17.9 panels; 21.0 sq ft per panel; 12,000 kWh -> 8.73 kW -> 21 panels. VERIFIED.
- QUALIFIED general statements (labeled or softened): capacity factor definition; south-facing roofs usually produce most; some utilities credit exports below retail; fixed charges limit savings.
- Fixes applied: lead now "A home with average U.S. electricity use" (range does not hold for all homes: ~9 to ~31 panels across state averages); "national mean/average" -> "mid-range (average of NREL's classes)" everywhere; table adds actual kW DC; "matches real installs" -> "is close to"; FAQ 4 "Some states, such as Michigan"; Sources table notes first-year/unweighted and kW DC.
