Checked November 21, 2026. Byline John Marshall. Second article (evergreen guide) after the CenterPoint news candidate was held (current delivery rate available only from retailer blogs; CenterPoint market notices fail). Same independent fact-check agent as new-jersey-net-metering-2026. Counts (checker rows): 12 verified, 10 qualified (wording fixed or accepted), 2 not verified (fixed).

- EIA Electricity Monthly Update print version: released Oct. 23, 2026, data for August 2026, residential 18.16 c/kWh; September data not out on Nov. 21. VERIFIED.
- EIA RECS 2020 CE5.3b: most-used TV 210 kWh, second 106, third 82, all TVs and related 632 kWh per household using the end use. VERIFIED (re-fetched; million Btu values cross-check).
- EIA RECS 2020 HC4.1: weekday most-used TV 4-6 hours 41.66M homes (largest group; other groups 4.53, 29.58, 24.81, 19.17M per research log), >10 hours 19.17M; weekend 7-10 hours 32.17M, >10 hours 23.28M. VERIFIED (log).

- FR 2024-02036 (Feb. 2, 2024): 16 c from DOE national cost data; ranges table rows (40-44 $13-51; 50-54 $22-51; 55-59 $24-69; 65-69 $27-110; 69.5+ $32-155); new ranges on labels for newly produced units by May 2, 2024. VERIFIED; compliance wording fixed.

- ENERGY STAR televisions page: ~34% more efficient; 0.5 W sleep limit; "Smart TVs are the most efficient way to stream". VERIFIED. Comparison to consoles/media players removed (not in quote).
- Fixed/removed: FAQ "1 to 4 cents for most modern TVs" (NOT VERIFIED) -> two models only; "devices can draw more standby than the TV" (NOT VERIFIED) -> "also draw standby power"; description "a big TV costs about 3 cents" -> "a 65-inch model we checked"; game console power claim removed; TV size/efficiency trend and RECS 2027 date removed; evening viewing hedged.
