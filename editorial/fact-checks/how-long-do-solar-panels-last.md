# Fact-check: how-long-do-solar-panels-last

Checked November 4, 2026. Byline Craig Leslie. Independent fact-check by the same separate agent as pge-rate-increase-2027; findings applied. Counts: 20 verified, 13 qualified, 2 not verified (rewritten).
- DOE SETO, July 28, 2021: "Solar panels last about 25-30 years, which is similar to the lifetime of a roof." VERIFIED.
- NREL STAT FAQs Part 2, April 23, 2018 (re-fetched): "no end-dates per se"; median ~0.5%/yr; "could be higher in hotter climates and for rooftop systems"; "in year 20 ... approximately 90%"; "typically warrantied for 20–25 years"; output "no longer guaranteed"; repower/decommission/leave in place. VERIFIED.
- NREL/PR-5200-56690 (2012): median 0.5%/yr, average 0.8%/yr, 2,128 rates, ca. 80% below 1%/yr. VERIFIED.
- NREL/PR-5K00-83487 (2022): median system degradation -0.75%/yr; >2,200 systems; >8.1 GW; mean system 4.1 MW. VERIFIED.
- DOE success story, June 27, 2023: ~0.75%/yr median; "representative of the entire U.S. PV fleet"; ~10% of non-residential systems; slower in cooler climates (paraphrase). VERIFIED / cooler-climate QUALIFIED.
- Solar United Neighbors, March 4, 2025: panels 25-30 years; "Inverter warranties usually last 5-15 years"; workmanship quote. VERIFIED (nonprofit).
- Qcells Q.TRON M-G3R.12 BFG 495-515 datasheet 2025-03 Rev01 (third-party copy, vicoexport.com): 25-yr product, 30-yr performance; 98.5% yr 1; max 0.33%/yr; 95.53% at 10 yrs; 88.93% at 30 yrs. VERIFIED (manufacturer).
- Enphase IQ8SE US datasheet IQ8SE-DS-0001-01-EN-US-2022-03-17 (third-party copy): "limited warranty of up to 25 years." VERIFIED.
- EIA EMU Aug 2026 residential 18.16 c/kWh (verified Nov. 3). VERIFIED (preliminary).
- Arithmetic: straight-line sums 23.5 / 22.75; year-25 output 88% / 82%; gap 7,500 kWh x $0.1816 = $1,362; /25 = $54; Qcells 25-yr 98.5-24x0.33 = 90.58%. VERIFIED.
- Fixes applied: kWh table column now "Output in year 25" (88%/82%; was 87.5/81 = year 26); takeaway "in year 20"; inverter claims tied to warranty length ("often need replacing sooner"); "warranties have lengthened" -> "some newer warranties run longer"; removed "first study measured mostly modules" (NOT VERIFIED) and "string inverter ... should expect to replace" (NOT VERIFIED, rewritten as "may need replacing"); "well under 1%" -> "under 1%"; dropped "all technologies"; unsourced warranty generalizations softened; title changed from "What Field Data Shows" to "What NREL and DOE Data Show".
- National guide; no state taxonomy.
